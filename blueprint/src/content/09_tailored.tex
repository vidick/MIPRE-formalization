\section{The Aldous--Lyons track: $\mathrm{TMIP}^* = \RE$}
\label{ch:tailored}

This chapter is the plan and the progress of a second target: the main theorem of Bowen, Chapman
and Vidick's \emph{The Aldous--Lyons Conjecture II: Undecidability}~\cite{BCV25}, a computable
reduction from the halting problem to \emph{tailored} non-local games that sends halting
machines to games with a perfect Z-aligned permutation strategy commuting along edges, and
non-halting machines to games of value at most $\frac12$. With the companion
paper~\cite{BCLV24} it refutes the Aldous--Lyons conjecture. The estimate, the reuse map and
the phases are in \texttt{planning/aldous-lyons-track.md}. There is no adversarial-verification
ledger for these papers: the paper, at its arXiv version, is the authority on the mathematics,
and line numbers II:$n$ below refer to its LaTeX source.

\emph{Status.} Phase~0: the definitions and the statement are formalized
(\texttt{MIPRE/TailoredGameValue.lean} and \texttt{MIPRE/Tailored/}), and one lemma is proved,
that the definitions are not vacuous (Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-trivial-zpc}). Phase~1, first
part: the toolbox of permutation strategies is proved --- signed permutations
(Lemma~\ref{lem:signed-perm}), the Fourier transform and data processing
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:fourier-pvm} and~\ref{lem:data-processing}), the criterion for a permutation
strategy to be perfect (Lemma~\ref{lem:perm-strategy-perfect}), Lemma~\ref{lem:zpc-pcc}, and the
Magic Square (Proposition~\ref{prop:magic-square-zpc}). Phase~1, second part: a tailored
verifier is a normal form verifier, the canonical decider written as a program, with the same
games, values and completeness (Lemmas~\ref{lem:canonical-decider} and~\ref{lem:of-tnfv}).
Phase~1, third part: the halting protocol at a fixed level is proved for a search program
meeting its specification (Theorem~\ref{thm:tailored-halting-level}). Phase~1, fourth part: the
tabulation, the search program and the halting reduction (Theorem~\ref{thm:tailored-halting}),
and $\mathrm{TMIP}^* = \RE$ for the computable class (Theorem~\ref{thm:tmipstar-eq-re}).
Phase~1, last part: $\mathrm{TMIP}^* = \RE$ for the paper's polynomial-time class
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tmipstar-poly-eq-re}). So Phase~1 is proved, with a tailored gap compression
(Definition~\ref{def:tailored-compression}) as its one hypothesis.
Phase~2: the $k$-fold tailored product, the tensor powers of permutation strategies,
completeness and soundness of repeated tailored verifiers for programs meeting their
specification (Theorem~\ref{thm:tailored-rep-from-spec}), and the programs themselves with their
running times, so that parallel repetition of tailored verifiers is proved
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tailored-rep}). The other results below carry no proof marks; they are the
targets of Phases~3--5.

The formalization departs from the paper's text in the places Remark~\ref{rem:tailored-route}
lists, all of them choices this project made for $\MIPstar = \RE$ already: soundness is proved
in value form, against all finite-dimensional strategies, with direct parallel repetition, so
that neither the anchored repetition theorem of~\cite{BVY17} nor the almost-synchronous
rounding of~\cite{Vid22} is needed.

\subsection{Tailored games and permutation strategies}

\begin{definition}[Tailored game; II:1243]
\label{def:tailored-game}
\uses{def:game,def:q-value,def:normal-verifier}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.len,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.maxLen, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.Accepts,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.toGame, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.valStar,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.doubled, MIPRE.Tailored.Satisfies,
  MIPRE.Tailored.rejectConstraint}
\leanok
A \emph{tailored game} on a finite question set $X$ is a distribution $\mu$ on $X \times X$
together with, at each question $x$, a set $S_x = S_x^R \cup S_x^L$, a disjoint union, of $\ell^R(x)$
\emph{readable} and $\ell^L(x)$ \emph{linear} formal variables, and, for each pair of questions
$(x, y)$ and each assignment $\gamma^R$ of the readable variables at $x$ and $y$, a finite list
$L_{xy}(\gamma^R)$ of vectors over the disjoint union $S_x \cup S_y \cup \{J\}$, the \emph{controlled linear
constraints}. The answer at $x$ is an assignment $a \in \F_2^{S_x}$, readable bits first. The
\emph{canonical decider} (II:1757--1787) accepts $(a, b)$ at $(x, y)$ when every
$c \in L_{xy}(a^R, b^R)$ satisfies $\langle c, (a, b, 1)\rangle = 0$ over $\F_2$, the
coordinate $J$ being set to $1$; the empty list accepts every answer pair, and the list
$\{J\}$ rejects every one. The tailored game defines a game (Definition~\ref{def:game}) with
answers the bit strings of bounded length, those of the wrong length losing, and its quantum
value $\valstar$. Its \emph{doubled} game asks $(\mathsf{false}, x)$ and $(\mathsf{true}, y)$
with the weight of $(x, y)$ and no weight elsewhere; it is again a tailored game.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The three forms of a measurement with outcomes in $\F_2^k$; II:972--1006]
\label{def:fourier-forms}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.bitSign, MIPRE.Tailored.fourierFactor, MIPRE.Tailored.fourierProj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.obsChar, MIPRE.Tailored.dotBit}
\leanok
For $k$ commuting involutions $U_1, \dots, U_k$ of a complex $\ast$-algebra --- the
\emph{observable form} of a measurement with outcomes in $\F_2^k$ --- the \emph{representation
form} is the homomorphism $\alpha \mapsto U^\alpha = \prod_i U_i^{\alpha_i}$ on $\F_2^k$, and the
\emph{projective form} is the Fourier transform
\[
  P_a \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{k} \tfrac12\bigl(\Id + (-1)^{a_i}\, U_i\bigr), \qquad a \in \F_2^k.
\]
The inner product $\langle \alpha, a\rangle = \sum_i \alpha_i a_i$ is taken over $\F_2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Signed permutations; II:1008--1069]
\label{def:signed-perm}
\uses{def:fourier-forms}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix,
  MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrixHom, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.negOne,
  MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.diag, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.prod,
  MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.map, MIPRE.Tailored.signedPermMatrix, MIPRE.Tailored.IsSignedPerm}
\leanok
A \emph{signed permutation} of a finite set $\Omega$ is a permutation $\sigma$ of $\Omega$ with a
sign $(-1)^{s(j)}$ at each point: the permutation $\pm j \mapsto \pm (-1)^{s(j)} \sigma(j)$ of the
signed set $\Omega_\pm = \{\pm\} \times \Omega$, which commutes with the sign flip. They form a
group, the semidirect product of $\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ and $\F_2^\Omega$ (II:1057), which
contains the sign flip $-\Id$, the $\pm 1$ diagonals, and, for two sets, the products
$g \times h$ acting on $(\Omega \times \Omega')_\pm$ (II:3062). The \emph{matrix} of a signed
permutation is that of its action on the anti-symmetric functions in the basis $B^-$ (II:1049),
the \emph{signed permutation matrix} $e_j \mapsto (-1)^{s(j)} e_{\sigma(j)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Z-aligned permutation strategies commuting along edges; II:1043--1057,
II:1279--1283]
\label{def:zpc-strategy}
\uses{def:tailored-game,def:fourier-forms,def:signed-perm}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.proj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.value, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.HasPerfectZPC}
\leanok
A \emph{signed permutation matrix} on $\C^m$ is a matrix $e_j \mapsto (-1)^{s(j)} e_{\sigma(j)}$
for a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1, \dots, m\}$ and signs $s$: the action of a signed
permutation of $\{\pm\} \times \{1, \dots, m\}$ on the anti-symmetric functions, in their
standard basis. A \emph{permutation strategy} for a tailored game assigns to each variable $X$
at each question $x$ an observable $U(x, X)$ on $\C^m$ that is a signed permutation matrix and
an involution, the observables at a question commuting with each other; its measurement at $x$
is the Fourier transform
\[
  P^x_a \;=\; \prod_{X \in S_x} \tfrac12\bigl(\Id + (-1)^{a_X}\, U(x, X)\bigr),
  \qquad a \in \F_2^{S_x},
\]
and its value is $\sum_{x, y} \mu(x, y) \sum_{a, b} \mathrm{tr}(P^x_a P^y_b)/m$ over the
accepted $(a, b)$. It is \emph{Z-aligned} when the observables of readable variables are
diagonal, and \emph{commutes along edges} when $U(x, X)$ and $U(y, Y)$ commute whenever
$\mu(x, y) > 0$. A ZPC strategy is a Z-aligned permutation strategy commuting along edges, and
it is \emph{perfect} when its value is $1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Two conventions]
\label{rem:tailored-conventions}
The paper's loops $xx$ read a single answer, over $S_x$; here, as for the paper's tailored
normal form verifiers, whose canonical decider always receives two answers, a loop reads the
pair $(a, a)$ against constraints over two copies of $S_x$ --- a one-copy constraint $(c, c_J)$
is the two-copy constraint $(c, 0, c_J)$, and a two-copy constraint $(c_1, c_2, c_J)$ acts as
$(c_1 + c_2, c_J)$. And the completeness clauses of the pipeline are read on the doubled game,
as Definition~\ref{def:pcc} is read for the existing pipeline: the doubled game is bipartite,
so no hypothesis on the verifier is needed to make it synchronous. A ZPC strategy for a game
gives one for its doubled game, the same observables at both tags.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The trivial strategy; II:1148]
\label{lem:tailored-trivial-zpc}
\uses{def:zpc-strategy}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.trivial, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.trivial_proj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.hasPerfectZPC_of_accepts_zero}
\leanok
A tailored game that accepts the all-zero answers at every question pair of positive weight
has a perfect ZPC strategy: in dimension $1$, every observable the identity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Every observable is the identity, which is a signed permutation matrix, diagonal and an
involution, and commutes with everything. Each factor $\frac12(\Id + (-1)^{a_X}\Id)$ is $\Id$
at $a_X = 0$ and $0$ at $a_X = 1$, so $P^x_a$ is $1$ at $a = 0$ and $0$ elsewhere, and the
value is $\sum_{x, y} \mu(x, y) = 1$. This is the case of the halting protocol in which the
machine has halted (II:1978), and it shows that the definitions are not vacuous.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Signed permutation matrices]
\label{lem:signed-perm}
\uses{def:signed-perm}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_mul, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_injective,
  MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_inv, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_negOne,
  MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_diag, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_prod,
  MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_map, MIPRE.Tailored.SignedPerm.toMatrix_mem_unitaryGroup,
  MIPRE.Tailored.IsSignedPerm.isHermitian, MIPRE.Tailored.IsSignedPerm.isDiag_iff,
  MIPRE.Tailored.IsSignedPerm.kronecker, MIPRE.Tailored.IsSignedPerm.conjTranspose_eq_transpose}
\leanok
The matrix map is an injective homomorphism from the signed permutations of $\Omega$ to the
unitary matrices on $\C^\Omega$. It sends the sign flip to $-\Id$, the signs $s$ to the diagonal
$((-1)^{s(j)})_j$, products of signed sets to Kronecker products, and a relabelling of $\Omega$
to the reindexed matrix. A signed permutation matrix has real entries; it is self-adjoint when
it is an involution, and it is diagonal exactly when it is a $\pm 1$ diagonal.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Column $j$ of the matrix of $(\sigma, s)$ has a single nonzero entry, $(-1)^{s(j)}$ in row
$\sigma(j)$. The product of the matrices of $(\sigma, s)$ and $(\tau, t)$ has, in column $j$, the
single entry $(-1)^{s(\tau(j)) + t(j)}$ in row $\sigma\tau(j)$, which is the group law; and the
columns determine $(\sigma, s)$. The adjoint is the matrix of the inverse, so the matrix is
unitary, and an involution equals its adjoint. A diagonal one has $\sigma = \Id$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Fourier transform; II:974--1006]
\label{lem:fourier-pvm}
\uses{def:fourier-forms}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.isPVMIn_fourierFactor, MIPRE.Tailored.isPVMIn_fourierProj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.mul_fourierProj, MIPRE.Tailored.obsChar_mul_fourierProj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.pvmObs_fourierProj_bit, MIPRE.Tailored.pvmObs_fourierProj_dotBit,
  MIPRE.Tailored.isDiag_fourierProj}
\leanok
Let $U_1, \dots, U_k$ be commuting self-adjoint involutions. Then $(P_a)_{a \in \F_2^k}$ is a
projective measurement. $P_a$ lies in the $(-1)^{\langle \alpha, a\rangle}$-eigenspace of
$U^\alpha$, so that $U^\alpha = \sum_a (-1)^{\langle \alpha, a\rangle} P_a$, and in particular
$U_i = \sum_a (-1)^{a_i} P_a$. An element commuting with every $U_i$ commutes with every $P_a$,
and diagonal observables have diagonal projections.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
For one involution, $\frac12(\Id \pm U)$ are complementary projections. If every element of one
projective measurement commutes with every element of another, their products form a projective
measurement on the product of the outcome sets; the Fourier transform of $k + 1$ observables is
the product of that of the first and that of the other $k$, reindexed, which proves the first
claim by induction on $k$. $U_i$ commutes with the factors of $P_a$ other than the $i$-th, on
which it acts as $(-1)^{a_i}$; multiplying over the $i$ with $\alpha_i = 1$ gives the eigenvalue
relation, and summing it over $a$ the inverse transform. A product of diagonal matrices is
diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Data processing; Claims II:2902 and II:2918]
\label{lem:data-processing}
\uses{lem:fourier-pvm}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.pvmObs_coarse_affine, MIPRE.Tailored.isDiag_coarse}
\leanok
Coarse-graining the Fourier transform of commuting self-adjoint involutions $U$ along the affine
map $a \mapsto c + \langle \alpha, a\rangle$ gives the measurement with outcomes in $\F_2$ whose
observable is $(-1)^c U^\alpha$. Coarse-graining a diagonal measurement along any map gives a
diagonal measurement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The observable of the coarse-graining is $\sum_a (-1)^{c + \langle \alpha, a\rangle} P_a =
(-1)^c U^\alpha$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier-pvm}. Sums of diagonal matrices are diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[When a permutation strategy is perfect]
\label{lem:perm-strategy-perfect}
\uses{def:zpc-strategy,lem:signed-perm,lem:fourier-pvm}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.isPVMIn_proj, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.commute_proj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.value_eq_one_of, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.dotBit_of_obsChar,
  MIPRE.Tailored.satisfies_ofFn_iff}
\leanok
The measurements of a permutation strategy are projective, and those at the two ends of an edge
commute. The strategy is perfect when $P^x_a P^y_b = 0$ for every answer pair $(a, b)$ that the
canonical decider rejects at an edge $(x, y)$. The canonical decider's check of a constraint
$(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ on answers $(a, b)$ is $\langle \alpha, a\rangle + \langle \beta,
b\rangle = \gamma$; and at an edge, if $U^\alpha V^\beta$ acts as $(-1)^\gamma$ on the range of
$P^x_a P^y_b \neq 0$, where $U$ and $V$ are the observables at $x$ and $y$, then $(a, b)$
satisfies it (Claim II:2870).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The observables are involutive signed permutation matrices, hence self-adjoint
(Lemma~\ref{lem:signed-perm}), and Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier-pvm} applies. If the rejected pairs
have $P^x_a P^y_b = 0$, the accepted pairs carry $\sum_{a, b} \mathrm{tr}(P^x_a P^y_b)/m = 1$ at
each edge. The parity of the number of common ones of two vectors is their inner product. By the
eigenvalue relations, $U^\alpha V^\beta P^x_a P^y_b = (-1)^{\langle \alpha, a\rangle + \langle
\beta, b\rangle} P^x_a P^y_b$, as $V^\beta$ commutes with $P^x_a$ along an edge; comparing with
$(-1)^\gamma P^x_a P^y_b \neq 0$ gives the constraint.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[ZPC strategies are PCC strategies]
\label{lem:zpc-pcc}
\uses{lem:perm-strategy-perfect,def:pcc,def:sync-value,lem:sync-le-valstar}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.toSync, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.isPCC_toSync,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.value_toSync, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.double,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.value_double, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.HasPerfectZPC.doubled,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.HasPerfectZPC.exists_pcc,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.HasPerfectZPC.syncValue_eq_one,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.HasPerfectZPC.valStar_eq_one}
\leanok
Let $G$ be a tailored game. A permutation strategy for its doubled game defines a synchronous
strategy (Definition~\ref{def:sync-strategy}) for the doubled game of the bipartite game of $G$:
at $(t, x)$ it measures an answer string of length $\ell(x)$ with $P^{(t, x)}$ of its bits, and
gives every other string the operator $0$. It has the value of the permutation strategy, and it
commutes on the support of the question distribution, the permutation strategy commuting along
edges (Definition~\ref{def:pcc}). A permutation strategy for $G$, played at both tags, is one for
its doubled game, of the same value. In particular a tailored game with a perfect ZPC strategy
has a perfect ZPC strategy for its doubled game, and then synchronous value $1$ on the doubled
game and quantum value $1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
By Lemma~\ref{lem:perm-strategy-perfect} the measurements are projective and commute along
edges; the operators on strings of the wrong length are $0$, and summing over the strings of
length $\ell(x)$ is summing over $\F_2^{\ell(x)}$, so the measurement sums to $\Id$ and the two
values are the same sum, the bipartite decider agreeing with the canonical one on the edges.
Doubling a strategy keeps the observables, and the doubled distribution is that of $G$ on the
pairs $((\mathsf{false}, x), (\mathsf{true}, y))$ and zero elsewhere. The synchronous value
bounds the quantum value of the doubled game (Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar}), which is that of
$G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The Magic Square game; Examples II:1300--1370]
\label{prop:magic-square-zpc}
\uses{lem:zpc-pcc,lem:signed-perm}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.MagicSquare.game, MIPRE.Tailored.MagicSquare.obs,
  MIPRE.Tailored.MagicSquare.strategy, MIPRE.Tailored.MagicSquare.value_strategy,
  MIPRE.Tailored.MagicSquare.hasPerfectZPC, MIPRE.Tailored.MagicSquare.valStar_eq_one}
\leanok
Tailor the Mermin--Peres Magic Square, as a linear constraint system game, with all its variables
linear: the questions are the six constraints --- each row of the $3 \times 3$ grid summing to
$0$, each column to $1$ --- and the nine cells; an edge joins a constraint to one of its cells,
with weight $\frac1{18}$, and checks that the constraint holds and that the constraint's value
at the cell is the cell's. This game has a perfect ZPC strategy on the signed set of eight
points $(\F_2^2)_\pm$: the grid
\[
  \begin{array}{ccc}
    X \otimes \Id & \Id \otimes X & X \otimes X \\
    \Id \otimes Z & Z \otimes \Id & Z \otimes Z \\
    -X \otimes Z & -Z \otimes X & -XZ \otimes XZ
  \end{array}
\]
of signed permutations, $X$ flipping a bit and $Z$ flipping the sign at the bit $1$. Its quantum
value is therefore $1$, while the linear system has no solution, so that no deterministic
strategy, and no ZPC strategy for the tailoring with all variables readable, is perfect
(Remark II:1250).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The observables are involutions commuting along each row and each column, the rows multiply to
$\Id$ and the columns to $-\Id$; these are identities in the group of signed permutations of
four points, checked by computation, and the matrix map transports them. All variables being
linear, Z-alignment is vacuous; an edge joins a constraint to one of its cells, whose observable
is one of the constraint's, so the strategy commutes along edges. The observable of a
consistency check is the square of a cell's observable, $\Id$, and that of a row or column check
is the product of its three observables, $\Id$ or $-\Id$ as the parity requires, so every
sampled answer pair is accepted by Lemma~\ref{lem:perm-strategy-perfect}. The quantum value is
$1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:zpc-pcc}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tailored normal form verifiers}

\begin{definition}[Tailored normal form verifier; II:1758]
\label{def:tailored-verifier}
\uses{def:tailored-game,def:zpc-strategy,def:sampler,def:decider}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier, MIPRE.Tailored.LenIs, MIPRE.Tailored.LenBound,
  MIPRE.Tailored.LenTotal, MIPRE.Tailored.LpIs, MIPRE.Cost.Data.spineList,
  MIPRE.Cost.Data.bitsListD, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.progs,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.size, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.Questions,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.lenOf, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.LenDefined,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.consOf, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.tgame,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.valStar, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.HasPerfectZPC}
\leanok
A \emph{tailored normal form verifier} $\verifier = (\cal{S}, \cal{L}, \cal{P})$
is a conditionally linear sampler $\cal{S}$ (Definition~\ref{def:sampler}), an
\emph{answer-length calculator} $\cal{L}$, which on $(n, x, \kappa)$ outputs, in unary, the
number of readable ($\kappa = R$) or linear ($\kappa = L$) variables at the question $x$, and a
\emph{linear-constraints processor} $\cal{P}$, which on $(n, x, y, a^R, b^R)$ outputs the
list $L_{xy}(a^R, b^R)$; the paper's fourth machine, the canonical decider, is the same for
every verifier (Definition~\ref{def:canonical-decider}). The outputs are read totally, so that
none is malformed: a length as the length of the output's spine, a list of constraints
elementwise. The \emph{$n$-th game} $\verifier_n$ is the tailored game
on the sampler's questions that the programs compute, a question or pair at which a program
does not halt rejecting every answer. $\verifier$ \emph{has a perfect ZPC strategy at $n$} when
the doubled game of $\verifier_n$ has one (Remark~\ref{rem:tailored-conventions}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$\lambda$-bounded tailored verifiers, and budgets; II:1800]
\label{def:tailored-bounded}
\uses{def:tailored-verifier}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.IsBounded, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.Within}
\leanok
$\verifier$ is \emph{$\lambda$-bounded} when, for every $n \geq 2$, the sampler's dimension and
the running times of the three programs are at most $n^\lambda$ --- the running times read as
in Definition~\ref{def:lambda-bounded}, at degree $\lambda$ in the size of the input --- the
lengths $\cal{L}$ outputs are at most $n^\lambda$, and $\abs{\verifier} \leq \lambda$. The
answer-length calculator outputs in unary, as in the paper, but its running time is bounded
relative to the size of its input, which is not bounded by $n^\lambda$; so the running time does
not bound the lengths by $n^\lambda$, and that bound is a clause of its own. A verifier is
\emph{within a budget} at index $n$ when the same quantities are bounded by the budget's.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The canonical decider, as a program; II:1757--1787]
\label{def:canonical-decider}
\uses{def:tailored-verifier,lem:effective-binary-linear-algebra,lem:pcp-padding-programs}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.canonProg, MIPRE.Tailored.canonFinal, MIPRE.Tailored.satF,
  MIPRE.Tailored.allSatF, MIPRE.Cost.Prog.pushProg}
\leanok
The \emph{canonical decider} of a tailored verifier with answer-length calculator $\cal{L}$ and
linear-constraints processor $\cal{P}$ is the program that, on $(n, x, y, a, b)$, runs $\cal{L}$
on $(n, x, \kappa)$ and on $(n, y, \kappa)$ for both kinds $\kappa$ of variables, then $\cal{P}$ on
$(n, x, y, a^R, b^R)$, where $a^R$ and $b^R$ are the first $\ell^R(x)$ bits of $a$ and the first
$\ell^R(y)$ bits of $b$, and accepts when $\abs{a} = \ell^R(x) + \ell^L(x)$,
$\abs{b} = \ell^R(y) + \ell^L(y)$, and every constraint $c$ that $\cal{P}$ outputs has the length
of $(a, b, 1)$ and satisfies $\langle c, (a, b, 1)\rangle = 0$ over $\F_2$. It is a sequence of
steps, each of which runs one of the two programs on an input computed in polynomial time from
the state and pushes the output onto the state, followed by the check, computed in polynomial
time.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The canonical decider decides the $n$-th game]
\label{lem:canonical-decider}
\uses{def:canonical-decider}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.canonProg_accepts, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.tgame_accepts_iff}
\leanok
The canonical decider accepts $(n, x, y, a, b)$ exactly when the four runs of $\cal{L}$ and the
run of $\cal{P}$ halt and the check passes on their outputs. So, at questions $x, y$ of the
$n$-th game $\verifier_n$, it accepts $(a, b)$ exactly when $\verifier_n$ does.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A run of a sequence of steps is a run of each step, the model being deterministic, and the
polynomial-time pieces compute what they are meant to. For the second part: when the five runs
halt, their outputs are the lengths and the constraints that $\verifier_n$ is read from. When one
of them does not halt, $\verifier_n$ has at that edge the constraint list $\{J\}$, which no answer
pair satisfies, and the canonical decider does not halt, which is a rejection.
\end{proof}

\label{def:of-tnfv}
\uses{def:canonical-decider,def:normal-verifier}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.ofTNFV}
\leanok
A tailored verifier $\verifier$ is the normal form verifier (Definition~\ref{def:normal-verifier})
with the sampler of $\verifier$ and the decider that checks that both questions have the length

\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A tailored verifier and its normal form verifier have the same games]
\label{lem:of-tnfv}
\uses{def:of-tnfv,lem:canonical-decider,lem:zpc-pcc,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.ofTNFV_accepts_iff,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.ofTNFV_game_D, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.valStar_ofTNFV,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.hasPerfectPCC_ofTNFV}
\leanok
At the questions of the $n$-th game, the normal form verifier of $\verifier$ accepts exactly what
$\verifier_n$ accepts. Let $T$ be at least the answer lengths of $\verifier_n$. Then its game
$\verifier_{n,T}$ has the quantum value of $\verifier_n$, and if $\verifier$ has a perfect ZPC
strategy at $n$, the doubled game of $\verifier_{n,T}$ has a PCC strategy of value $1$
(Definition~\ref{def:pcc}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{def:value-model}
\leanok
The question-length check passes at the questions of index $n$, so acceptance is the canonical
decider's, which is that of $\verifier_n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-decider}. The two games
have the same distribution and accept the same answer pairs, and every answer pair that
$\verifier_{n,T}$ accepts has the lengths of $\verifier_n$, so is one of the answer pairs of the
game of $\verifier_n$: enlarging the answer sets by rejected answers changes no value. A perfect
ZPC strategy for the doubled game of $\verifier_n$ is a perfect PCC strategy for the doubled
bipartite game (Lemma~\ref{lem:zpc-pcc}), which, extended by zero to the answers of length at
most $T$, is one for the doubled game of $\verifier_{n,T}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{TMIP}^* = \RE$; II:1505, Theorem~7.4 of~\cite{BCLV24}]
\label{thm:tmip-re}

\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) if $\machine$ halts on the empty input, then $g(\machine)$ has a

\item (\textbf{Soundness}) if $\machine$ does not halt on the empty input, then

verifier's game at its fixed index included; the second is Phases~2 to~5.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the formalization departs from the paper]
\label{rem:tailored-route}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The soundness bound.} The paper's clause~(3) reads $\valstar(\game_\machine) <
  \frac12$; its proof (II:2046--2065) shows that $\Ent(\game_\machine, \frac12) = \infty$, that
  every finite-dimensional strategy has value $< \frac12$, which bounds the supremum by
  $\frac12$ only. Paper~I uses the theorem with $\leq \frac12$ (\cite{BCLV24}, Theorem~7.4).
\item \emph{Value form.} The paper states compression and parallel repetition in entanglement
  form, $\Ent(\verifier'_n, \frac12) \geq \max\{\Ent(\verifier_{2^n}, \frac12),
  2^{2^{\lambda n} - 1}\}$ (II:1865), proved through the anchored repetition theorem
  of~\cite{BVY17} read as dimension-preserving (II:11425) and the almost-synchronous rounding
  of~\cite{Vid22} (II:3227, a proof idea; its quotation of~\cite{Vid22} at II:11350 is commented
  out of the source). The paper's Remark~II:1876 says that what its proofs establish is the
  value form, which is what is formalized here, with direct repetition
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}) and the paper route of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting}, whose
  search branch replaces the base case $2^{2^{\lambda n} - 1}$. No entanglement bound is
  tracked.
\item \emph{All strategies.} Soundness is proved in the quantum value $\valstar$ of tensor-product
  strategies, which bounds the paper's synchronous value from above
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar}).
\item \emph{Question reduction.} The paper's question reduction is JNVWY's on its own Pauli basis
  game over $\F_2$ (II:3742), sound by a semi-stability theorem cited from de la Salle (II:3495);
  the paper notes that its combinatorial game is JNVWY's and that JNVWY's soundness covers it
  (II:5228--5233). The plan's Route~A presents this project's introspection verifier
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:introspection}) as a tailored verifier instead, which Phase~0 checked is
  possible (Remark~\ref{rem:tailored-route-a}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Route A: the two checks]
\label{rem:tailored-route-a}
Two properties of this project's introspection verifier make the tailored presentation
possible, and both hold (\texttt{planning/aldous-lyons-track.md}, section~4.2, with the evidence).
Every check of the introspective predicate is \emph{controlled-linear}: a predicate on the
readable fields alone (the introspected question, the sampled seed, the prefixes read and
hidden, and the $Z$-basis Pauli answers), or $\F_2$-affine equations on the linear fields (the
duals, the hidden tails, the $X$-basis answers, the Pair and Magic Square answers) with
coefficients computed from readable data and the questions; the input's answers must first be
padded to constant lengths, as in the paper (II:6219). And the honest strategy is a
signed-permutation strategy with diagonal readable observables when the input's is: its
$X$-type observables are translations, its $Z$-type observables $\pm 1$ characters, its
nonlinear post-processing acts on $Z$-basis outcomes only, and the input enters as controlled
direct sums.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Tailored gap compression, value form; II:1852]
\label{def:tailored-compression}
\uses{def:tailored-bounded}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGapCompression}
\leanok
A \emph{tailored gap compression} at level $\ell$ is a polynomial-time procedure that maps a
tailored verifier and $\lambda$ to a tailored verifier whose sampler and answer-length
calculator depend on $\lambda$ only, the calculator halting on every question of the sampler,
with running times, dimension and lengths at most a polynomial in $n + \lambda$, such that for
every $\lambda$-bounded input and every $n \geq C_0$: a perfect ZPC strategy for
$\verifier_{2^n}$ gives one for $\verifier'_n$; and $\valstar(\verifier_{2^n}) \leq \frac12$
implies $\valstar(\verifier'_n) \leq \frac12$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The tailored halting verifier; II:1900--1925]
\label{def:tailored-halt-verifier}
\uses{def:tailored-compression,lem:kleene}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.Vhalt, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.lp, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.lpProg,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.F, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.body, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.prep,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.comprLp, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.acceptProg,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.rejectProg, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.W, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.branch1,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.branch2}
\leanok
Let a tailored gap compression be given, with compressed sampler $\cal{S}^\lambda$ and
answer-length calculator $\cal{L}^\lambda$, a machine $\machine$, a search program $\cal{S}'$ and
a parameter $\lambda$. The \emph{tailored halting verifier}
$\verifier^{\machine,\lambda} = (\cal{S}^\lambda, \cal{L}^\lambda, \cal{P}^{\machine,\lambda})$ has
for linear-constraints processor the efficient Kleene fixed point of the map that sends a
processor $\cal{P}$ to the program which, on $(n, x, y, a^R, b^R)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item outputs the empty list of constraints if $\machine$ halts on the empty input within
  $\lceil \log_2 (n+1) \rceil$ steps;
\item otherwise outputs the list $\{J\}$ if $\cal{S}'$ halts on the description of
  $(\cal{S}^\lambda, \cal{L}^\lambda, \cal{P})$ within the same budget;
\item otherwise runs the compressed processor of $(\cal{S}^\lambda, \cal{L}^\lambda, \cal{P})$ on
  the input.
\end{enumerate}
Items 1 and 3 are the paper's map $F$ (II:1900), the empty list accepting and $\{J\}$ rejecting
every answer pair; item 2 is the search branch of the paper route of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting},
which supplies the base case that the value form of compression lacks.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The values of the tailored halting verifier; II:1978]
\label{lem:tailored-dhalt-values}
\uses{def:tailored-halt-verifier,def:zpc-strategy,lem:tailored-trivial-zpc}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.lp_runs_iff, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.hasPerfectZPC_of_branch1,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.valStar_eq_zero_of_branch2, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.hasPerfectZPC_iff_W,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.valStar_eq_W, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.hasPerfectZPC_congr,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.valStar_congr,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.valStar_eq_zero_of_rejects}
\leanok
At the level $n$: if $\machine$ halts within the budget, $\verifier^{\machine,\lambda}_n$ has a
perfect ZPC strategy; otherwise, if the search halts within the budget, it has value $0$;
otherwise it has a perfect ZPC strategy, and value, exactly when the compressed verifier of its
own programs does at $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:tailored-trivial-zpc}
\leanok
The processor is the fixed point, so on an input of index $n$ it outputs what the three
branches say. In the first, every constraint list is empty --- the answer-length calculator
halts at every question of the sampler, which is where its totality is spent --- so the game
accepts every answer pair of the right lengths, and the deterministic strategy is perfect
(Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-trivial-zpc}). In the second, every constraint list holds $J = 0$, so
no answer pair is accepted. In the third, the two verifiers have the same sampler and
answer-length calculator, and their processors output the same constraints at index $n$, so
their $n$-th games coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$\lambda$ for the tailored halting verifier; II:2002]
\label{lem:tailored-lambda}
\uses{def:tailored-halt-verifier,def:tailored-bounded,lem:lambda}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.esize_lpProg, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.lp_cost,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.exists_lp_cost_poly, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.sampler_len_clauses,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.lp_clause, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.size_clause,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.exists_lamBound, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.Lam0,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.Lam0_spec}
\leanok
There is a threshold $\Lambda_0$, depending on the compression, the universal machines and the
search program alone, such that $\verifier^{\machine,\lambda}$ is $\lambda$-bounded
(Definition~\ref{def:tailored-bounded}) for every machine $\machine$ and every
$\lambda \geq \Lambda_0 + 4\abs{\machine}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:lambda}
\leanok
The processor is a hardcoding of the fixed point's program to its own description, of size
$2(\abs{\machine} + \abs{\lambda})$ plus a constant. On $(n, d)$ it runs within the fixed point's
overhead on the hardcoded description, the branch computation, and the universal machine on the
compressed processor, whose time is the compression's $\mathrm{poly}(n + \lambda)$; together a
polynomial in $n + \abs{\machine} + \lambda$ times a power of $\abs{d} + 1$. The sampler's and the
answer-length calculator's clauses are the compression's bounds. Each clause is absorbed into
$n^\lambda$, at degree $\lambda$, past a threshold on $\lambda$, and the size into $\lambda$ once
$\lambda \geq \Lambda_0 + 4\abs{\machine}$.
\end{proof}

\label{def:tailored-search-spec}

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The tailored halting theorem at a fixed level; II:2021--2066]
\label{thm:tailored-halting-level}
\uses{lem:tailored-dhalt-values,lem:tailored-lambda,def:tailored-search-spec,lem:zpc-pcc}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.halting_tailored, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.halting_tailored_valStar,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.hasPerfectZPC_of_halts, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.valStar_le_of_not_halts,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.A_step, MIPRE.Tailored.Halting.B_step}
\leanok
Let a tailored gap compression be given and $\cal{S}'$ a program meeting the search
specification. For every machine $\machine$ and every $\lambda \geq \Lambda_0 + 4\abs{\machine}$,
at the level $C$: if $\machine$ halts on the empty input, $\verifier^{\machine,\lambda}$ has a
perfect ZPC strategy; if it does not, $\valstar(\verifier^{\machine,\lambda}_C) \leq \frac12$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:zpc-pcc}
\leanok
Along the levels $C, 2^C, 2^{2^C}, \dots$, compression carries a perfect ZPC strategy at $2^n$
down to $n$, and a value at most $\frac12$, at every level where neither of the first two
branches fires, the verifier then having the compressed verifier's games
(Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-dhalt-values}); the verifier is $\lambda$-bounded by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-lambda}. If $\machine$ halts, the first branch fires from some level on;
the search never fires below it, since a firing would put every level below it, the start level
among them, at value at most $\frac12$, while the search fires only when the start level's value
exceeds $\frac12$; so completeness descends to $C$. If $\machine$ does not halt and the value at
$C$ exceeded $\frac12$, the search would fire from some level on, and soundness would descend
from there to $C$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A presentation of a game without weight on its loops]
\label{lem:tailored-presents}
\uses{def:tailored-presents,lem:tailored-bridge,lem:fourier-pvm}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.Presents.quantumValue_eq, MIPRE.Tailored.Presents.hasPerfectZPC,
  MIPRE.Tailored.Presents.accepts_iff, MIPRE.Tailored.Presents.value_padStrategy,
  MIPRE.quantumValue_congr_support, MIPRE.quantumValue_eq_of_equiv_support,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.quantumValue_doubled_eq_valStar,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.doubled_μ_self, MIPRE.Tailored.fourierProj_castLE,
  MIPRE.Tailored.fourierProj_eq_zero_of_one}
\leanok
Let a tailored game description present a tailored game with no weight on its loops, such as a
doubled game. Then the description has the game's quantum value, and a perfect ZPC strategy of
the game, its observables padded with identities beyond a question's variables, is one of the
description. A tailored game and its doubled game have the same quantum value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{def:tailored-presents,lem:fourier-pvm,lem:model-proj-strategy-values,lem:transports-model}
\leanok
The two read answers differently: the description reads bit vectors of the largest length that
vanish beyond a vertex's variables, the game bit strings of exactly that length; and the
description also rejects unequal answers at a loop. On well-formatted answers the two
predicates agree up to the loop condition, and the quantum value sees a predicate only where the
distribution has weight. Answers pass from vectors to strings by keeping a vertex's
coordinates and back by padding with zeros, question by question, and decoding answers question
by question can only raise a value; so the two values bound each other. For the strategies: an
observable equal to the identity leaves the Fourier transform unchanged at an answer whose bit
there is $0$ and annihilates it at the others, so the padded strategy's measurements are the
strategy's on padded answers and vanish elsewhere, and it has the strategy's value.
\end{proof}

The \emph{tabulation} of a tailored verifier at index $n$, under the budget
$T \cdot (\abs{d} + 1)^k$, is the tailored game description whose vertices are the doubled
questions $\{0, 1\} \times \F_2^{s(n)}$, whose weights count the points of $\F_2^{s(n)}$ landing
on each pair of tagged marginals, and whose lengths and constraints are what the answer-length
calculator and the linear-constraints processor output, each program run under the budget. On a
description string, the verifier is $(\cal{S}^\lambda, \cal{L}^\lambda, \cal{P})$ for the string's
parameter $\lambda$ and processor $\cal{P}$, and the budget is $n^\lambda \cdot
(\abs{d}+1)^\lambda$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The class $\mathrm{TMIP}^*$; II:787, II:1505]
\label{def:tmipstar-poly}
\uses{def:mipstar,def:tailored-game,def:zpc-strategy,def:tailored-verifier}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TMIPStar, MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier.toPoly, MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier.tgame,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier.Efficient}
\leanok
A language $L \subseteq \{0,1\}^*$ is in $\mathrm{TMIP}^*$ if there are three machines, a
sampler, an answer-length calculator and a linear-constraints processor, and a polynomial $P$,
such that for every string $z$, writing $B = P(\abs{z})$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Efficiency.} The sampler is as in Definition~\ref{def:mipstar}: on $z$ and a uniform
  seed $r \in \{0,1\}^B$ it halts within time $P(\abs{z} + \abs{r})$ with a pair of strings of
  length at most $B$; write $\mu_z$ for their distribution. On $(z, x, \kappa)$ the calculator,
  and on $(z, x, y, a^R, b^R)$ the processor, halt within time $P$ of the total input length.
  The tailored game $\game_z$ has the strings of length at most $B$ as its questions, the
  distribution $\mu_z$, the lengths the calculator outputs and the constraints the processor
  outputs, read as for a tailored normal form verifier (Definition~\ref{def:tailored-verifier}).
\item \emph{Completeness.} If $z \in L$, the doubled game of $\game_z$ has a perfect ZPC
  strategy.
\item \emph{Soundness.} If $z \notin L$, then $\valstar(\game_z) \leq \frac12$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The paper's clause~(1) of Theorem~\ref{thm:tmip-re} asks that sampling a question pair and
evaluating the decision predicate take time polynomial in the input. The decision predicate is
the canonical decider (Definition~\ref{def:canonical-decider}) on what the calculator and the
processor output, so the efficiency clause bounds those two programs, as
Definition~\ref{def:mipstar} bounds the decider. The canonical decider's own running time, a
polynomial in theirs, is Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-decider-cost} for tailored normal form
verifiers; no statement here needs it. The
completeness clause is read on the doubled game, as for tailored normal form verifiers
(\texttt{TailoredVerifier.HasPerfectZPC}), and the threshold is $\leq \frac12$, as everywhere
in this project (Remark~\ref{rem:tailored-route}). In Lean the verifier is a
\texttt{TPolyVerifier}, whose sampler and bound form a \texttt{PolyVerifier}
(\texttt{toPoly}), so that the distribution of $\game_z$ is that of
Definition~\ref{def:mipstar}'s game.

\begin{lemma}[The class is contained in the computable one]
\label{lem:tmipstar-poly-sub}
\uses{def:tmipstar-poly,def:tmipstar,thm:tmipstar-eq-re,lem:tailored-presents,
  lem:tailored-tabulation,lem:mipstar-poly-sub}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TMIPStar.toComputable, MIPRE.Tailored.TMIPStar.isRE,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier.tabP, MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier.computable_tabP,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TPolyVerifier.presents_tabP}
\leanok
Every language in $\mathrm{TMIP}^*$ is in the computable class of Definition~\ref{def:tmipstar};
in particular it is recursively enumerable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:tailored-presents,thm:tmipstar-eq-re}
\leanok
On the input $z$, tabulate the doubled game of $\game_z$: its vertices are the doubled strings of
length at most $B$, its weights those of the tabulation of Definition~\ref{def:mipstar}'s game
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mipstar-poly-sub}), and its lengths and constraints what the calculator and the
processor output within the time bound $P$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-tabulation}. The table
is a computable function of $z$, and on an efficient input it presents the doubled game of
$\game_z$, which has no weight on its loops; so it has a perfect ZPC strategy when the doubled
game does, and the doubled game's quantum value, which is $\game_z$'s
(Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-presents}). So $L$ is in the computable class, and in $\RE$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:tmipstar-eq-re}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Extending a tailored game along its questions]
\label{lem:tailored-extend}
\uses{def:tailored-game,def:zpc-strategy,lem:tensor-power-zpc}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.Extends, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.Extends.valStar_eq,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.Extends.hasPerfectZPC,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.Extends.doubled}
\leanok
Let $\game'$ and $\game$ be tailored games on question sets $X'$ and $X$, and $e : X \to X'$
injective, such that $\game'$ has the weights, the lengths and the constraints of $\game$ on the
image of $e$, and no weight off it. Then $\valstar(\game') = \valstar(\game)$, a perfect ZPC
strategy of $\game$ gives one of $\game'$, and the doubled game of $\game'$ extends that of
$\game$ in the same way, along $\mathrm{id} \times e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Restricting $\game'$ to the image of $e$ does not change its value, its distribution being
supported there. The answers of length at most the largest length of $\game$ then sit inside
those of $\game'$, and the canonical decider rejects the longer ones, so the value is $\game$'s.
For a ZPC strategy of $\game$, pull it back along the inverse of $e$: its observables on the
image of $e$, and identities elsewhere, where no question is asked. An edge of positive weight
has both ends in the image, with the lengths of their preimages and an edge of $\game$ between
these, on which the two canonical deciders agree; so the pulled-back strategy is a perfect ZPC
strategy, by the pull-back of permutation strategies of the repetition phase.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The $k$-fold tailored product; II:11377, direct form]
\label{def:tailored-product}
\uses{def:tailored-game}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.repeat, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.varEquiv,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.coord, MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.coordR,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.padCons}
\leanok
The $k$-fold product of a tailored game $\game$ asks $k$-tuples of questions with the product
distribution $\prod_i \mu(x_i, y_i)$. The variables at $\bar{x}$ are those of its coordinates,
the readable ones first, as a tailored answer must have them: the readable variables of $x_1,
\ldots, x_k$ in this order, then their linear variables, so that $\ell^R(\bar{x}) = \sum_i
\ell^R(x_i)$ and $\ell^L(\bar{x}) = \sum_i \ell^L(x_i)$. The constraints at $(\bar{x},
\bar{y})$ are those of every coordinate, each written over all the variables with zero
coefficients outside its coordinate. A constraint is cut into its five blocks of coefficients
--- readable and linear at $x_i$, readable and linear at $y_i$, affine --- by prefixes and
suffixes, each put at its coordinate's place among the variables of its kind; the blocks
partition the constraint whatever its length, so a constraint of the wrong length pads to a
vector of the wrong length, which the canonical decider rejects, as it rejects the constraint
in its coordinate. No length is tested, so a program computes the padding as defined.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The canonical decider of the product]
\label{lem:tailored-product-accepts}
\uses{def:tailored-product}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.repeat_accepts_iff,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.satisfies_padCons_iff}
\leanok
The canonical decider of the product accepts a pair of answers at $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ exactly
when they have the right lengths and the canonical decider of $\game$ accepts, at every
coordinate $i$, the coordinate's answers read off them.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A coordinate's answer is two blocks of the tuple's answer, its readable bits after the
readable bits of the coordinates before it and its linear bits likewise. Cut the tuple's
answers into the thirteen blocks the padding is made of: a constraint of the right length
pairs with the whole answers block by block, the zero blocks contributing nothing and the
others pairing as the constraint does with the coordinate's answers; one of the wrong length
pads to a vector of the wrong length, and both sides reject. The readable answers of a coordinate are its block of the
readable answers of the tuple, so the constraints of the coordinate are evaluated at the
right readable answers.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tensor powers of permutation strategies; II:3043]
\label{lem:tensor-power-zpc}
\uses{def:tailored-product,lem:tailored-product-accepts,lem:signed-perm,lem:fourier-pvm,
  lem:perm-strategy-perfect,lem:tensor-power-pcc}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.slot, MIPRE.Tailored.isSignedPerm_slot, MIPRE.Tailored.isDiag_slot,
  MIPRE.Tailored.commute_slot_slot, MIPRE.Tailored.prod_slot_finRange,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.repeat, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.proj_repeat,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.proj_mul_eq_zero_of_value_eq_one,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.value_repeat_eq_one, MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.comap,
  MIPRE.Tailored.PermStrategy.value_comap_eq_one,
  MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredGame.HasPerfectZPC.repeat_doubled}
\leanok
If a tailored game $\game$ has a perfect Z-aligned permutation strategy commuting along edges,
so does its $k$-fold product, and so does the doubled product when the doubled game has one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The observable of the variable $(i, v)$ of $\bar{x}$ is $1 \otimes \ldots \otimes U(x_i, v)
\otimes \ldots \otimes 1$, on the $k$-th tensor power of the space. A slot of a signed
permutation is a signed permutation, of a diagonal matrix a diagonal matrix, and slots in two
different factors commute, so the observables are involutive signed permutations, commuting at
each question, diagonal at the readable variables, and commuting along the edges, which are
edges in every coordinate. The factors of the Fourier transform at $\bar{x}$ commute; regrouped
by coordinate they give the tensor product of the coordinates' measurements. In a perfect
strategy a rejected pair of answers on an edge has $P^x_a P^y_b = 0$: its probability is zero,
and $P^x_a P^y_b$, two commuting projections, is its own $X^* X$. A rejected pair of the product
is rejected in some coordinate (Lemma~\ref{lem:tailored-product-accepts}), and a tensor product
with a zero factor is zero, so the power is perfect (Lemma~\ref{lem:perm-strategy-perfect}).
For the doubled product, the power of the strategy for the doubled game is read at $(t,
\bar{x})$ on the questions $(t, x_1), \ldots, (t, x_k)$, a map of the questions that keeps
the lengths, the edges and the constraints.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Repeated tailored verifiers and the specification of their programs]
\label{def:tailored-rep-spec}
\uses{def:tailored-verifier,def:tailored-product,lem:cl-famsum,lem:smn}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness and soundness of repeated tailored verifiers]
\label{thm:tailored-rep-from-spec}
\uses{def:tailored-rep-spec,lem:tensor-power-zpc,lem:tailored-product-accepts,

\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.TailoredVerifier.RepSpec.accepts_iff,

\subsection{Question reduction: the honest strategy as a permutation strategy}

Theorem~\ref{thm:tailored-qr} is proved on Route~A (Remark~\ref{rem:tailored-route-a}): the
tailored question reduction is a tailored presentation of the introspection verifier of
Section~\ref{sec:ps-introspection}. Its completeness asks that the honest strategy of that
verifier, built on a perfect permutation strategy for the input, be a permutation strategy
itself. The honest strategy is a projective measurement at each question, whose outcomes are
encoded as answer bits; the lemmas of this subsection supply the observables of those bits and
show that they are signed permutations, in the three ways the honest strategy builds its
measurements: eigenbasis measurements of the Weyl operators, read out linearly; the
computational-basis measurement, read out arbitrarily; and controlled sums, applying one
signed permutation for each computational-basis state of a control register.

\begin{lemma}[The bit observables of an encoded measurement]
\label{lem:encoded-pvm-obs}
\uses{def:fourier-forms}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.encObs, MIPRE.Tailored.encObs_mul_self, MIPRE.Tailored.commute_encObs,
  MIPRE.Tailored.star_encObs, MIPRE.Tailored.fourierFactor_encObs,
  MIPRE.Tailored.fourierProj_encObs, MIPRE.Tailored.commute_encObs_encObs,
  MIPRE.Tailored.encObs_merge, MIPRE.Tailored.encObs_extend}
\leanok
Let $(P_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ be a projective measurement and $\mathrm{enc}$ a map from
$\Lambda$ to $\F_2^k$. The observables $U_i = \sum_\lambda (-1)^{\mathrm{enc}(\lambda)_i}
P_\lambda$ are commuting self-adjoint involutions, and their Fourier transform is the push-forward
of $P$ along $\mathrm{enc}$: $\prod_i \frac12(1 + (-1)^{a_i} U_i) = \sum_{\mathrm{enc}(\lambda) =
a} P_\lambda$. If every element of $P$ commutes with every element of a second projective
measurement $Q$, the bit observables of $P$ commute with those of $Q$, whatever the encodings.
Merging the outcomes of $P$ along a map $f$ and then encoding by $\mathrm{enc}$ gives the bit
observables of $P$ along $\mathrm{enc} \circ f$, and so does extending the outcome set of $P$
by zero along an injection $f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The map $\varepsilon \mapsto \sum_\lambda \varepsilon(\lambda) P_\lambda$ is multiplicative, so
the $U_i$ are commuting involutions, self-adjoint as their weights are real. Since $\sum_\lambda
P_\lambda = \Id$, the factor $\frac12(1 + (-1)^{b} U_i)$ is $\sum_\lambda \frac12(1 +
(-1)^{b + \mathrm{enc}(\lambda)_i}) P_\lambda$, the coarse-graining of $P$ onto
$\{\mathrm{enc}(\lambda)_i = b\}$; by multiplicativity the product over $i$ is the
coarse-graining onto the intersection of these sets, by induction on $k$. The last two claims
regroup the sum $\sum_\lambda$ by the fibres of $f$, resp.\ drop the outcomes outside the
image of $f$, which carry zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weyl measurements are read out by signed permutations; II:2938]
\label{lem:weyl-signed-perm}
\uses{def:generalized-pauli,lem:signed-perm,lem:encoded-pvm-obs}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.sgn_eq_bitSign, MIPRE.Tailored.wX_eq_signedPermMatrix,
  MIPRE.Tailored.isSignedPerm_wX, MIPRE.Tailored.wZ_eq_diagonal_bitSign,
  MIPRE.Tailored.isSignedPerm_wZ, MIPRE.Tailored.isDiag_wZ, MIPRE.Tailored.trDotDual,
  MIPRE.Tailored.exists_trDotL_eq, MIPRE.Tailored.encObs_proj_trDot,
  MIPRE.Tailored.exists_encObs_proj_linear, MIPRE.Tailored.isSignedPerm_encObs_xProj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.isSignedPerm_encObs_zProj}
\leanok
$X(a)$ is the permutation matrix of the translation by $a$, and $Z(b)$ the diagonal of the signs
$(-1)^{\tr(b \cdot e)}$. Every $\F_2$-linear functional on $\F_q^n$ is $e \mapsto \tr(c \cdot e)$
for some $c$, and the observable of an $\F_2$-linear bit $\varphi$ of the outcome of the
eigenbasis measurement of $X$ (of $Z$) is $X(c)$ ($Z(c)$) for the $c$ representing $\varphi$. So
every $\F_2$-linear encoding of the outcome of the $X$-basis measurement has signed-permutation
bit observables, and every encoding whatsoever of the outcome of the $Z$-basis measurement has
diagonal $\pm 1$ bit observables.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The first two claims are the definitions. The trace form is nondegenerate (a nonzero $c$ gives a
nonzero functional, since the trace is onto), so $c \mapsto \tr(c \cdot -)$ is an injective
linear map between spaces of equal finite dimension over $\F_2$, hence onto. The observable of
the bit $\tr(c \cdot -)$ is $\sum_e (-1)^{\tr(c \cdot e)} P_e = W(c)$, the expansion of a Weyl
operator in its eigenbasis projectors. The $Z$-basis projectors are the diagonal matrix units,
so any $\pm 1$ combination of them is a $\pm 1$ diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Controlled signed permutations]
\label{lem:controlled-signed-perm}

\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.blockDiag, MIPRE.Tailored.isSignedPerm_blockDiag,

of relabelling, entry by entry.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Presenting a game as a tailored game]
\label{lem:presentation-transport}
\uses{def:zpc-strategy,lem:perm-strategy-perfect,lem:encoded-pvm-obs,def:pcc,def:sync-value,
  lem:perfect-rejects-nothing,lem:transports-model}
\lean{MIPRE.SyncStrategy.isPVMIn, MIPRE.SyncStrategy.mul_eq_zero_of_value_eq_one,
  MIPRE.Tailored.valStar_le_of_dec, MIPRE.Tailored.permOfSync, MIPRE.Tailored.permOfSync_proj,
  MIPRE.Tailored.value_permOfSync, MIPRE.Tailored.hasPerfectZPC_of_sync}
\leanok
Let $\game$ be a tailored game and $\game'$ a game with the same questions and
question distribution. If a map $\mathrm{dec}$ of answers sends every pair that $\game$
accepts to one that $\game'$ accepts, then $\mathrm{val}^*(\game) \le
\mathrm{val}^*(\game')$. Conversely, let $\mathrm{enc}$ send the answers of $\game'$
at $x$ to $\F_2^{\mathrm{len}(x)}$, sending every accepted pair of answers in a set
$\mathrm{ok}$ to a pair $\game$ accepts, and let $S$ be a perfect PCC strategy of the
doubled game of $\game'$ that charges only answers in $\mathrm{ok}$, whose bit
observables along $\mathrm{enc}$ are signed permutations, diagonal at the readable bits. Then
these bit observables are a perfect Z-aligned permutation strategy of the doubled tailored
game commuting along edges.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The first claim is post-processing of the answers. For the second, the bit observables are
commuting involutions commuting along edges (Lemma~\ref{lem:encoded-pvm-obs}, from PCC), and
their measurement at $x$ is the push-forward of $S$ along $\mathrm{enc}$. A perfect PCC
strategy has $M^x_a M^y_b = 0$ for every rejected pair at an edge: the product is a
projection, as the two commute, of trace zero. So a pair $(a, b)$ that $\game$ rejects
has $P^x_a P^y_b = \sum M^x_{a'} M^y_{b'}$ over the $a', b'$ encoding to it, each term zero —
$a'$ or $b'$ outside $\mathrm{ok}$ carries zero, and otherwise $(a', b')$ is rejected by
$\game'$, or its encoding would be accepted — and the strategy is perfect by
Lemma~\ref{lem:perm-strategy-perfect}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Answer reduction: linear systems over $\F_2$}

Phase~4 of the plan (\texttt{planning/aldous-lyons-track.md}, ``Phase 4 slices'') proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:tailored-ar} with paper~II's tailored PCP (II:7736--11074). Before the PCP, the
output indicator turns the constraints of the input game at a pair of questions into a
\emph{decoupled triangulated system} (II:7751--8316), which the PCP checks against the linear
answers. The paper purifies the input verifier first (II:7783); here the indicator purifies on
the fly, so the input is not transformed.

\begin{definition}[Purified, triangulated and decoupled systems]
\label{def:ar-linear}
\uses{def:ld-encoding}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.Eqn, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.pureEqn, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.Tri,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.triangle, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.ext, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.decInd,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.DecSat}
\leanok
An equation over $\F_2$ is a list of variables and a right-hand side. The \emph{purified
equation} of a constraint $c$ of the canonical decider at answers $a^R a^L$, $b^R b^L$
(eq:purified\_equation, II:7762) is the equation on the linear answers, placed in a table of
$2L$ entries ($a^L$ from $0$, $b^L$ from $L$), whose right-hand side absorbs the readable
answers; a constraint of the wrong length becomes $0 = 1$. The \emph{triangulation} of a list
of equations (II:7952) replaces $X_{x_0} + \dots + X_{x_{k-1}} = b$ by $X_{x_0} = Y_0$,
$Y_{i-1} + X_{x_i} = Y_i$ and $Y_{k-1} = b$, each row with its own fresh variables, and the
\emph{extension} of an assignment gives $Y_i$ the partial sum $\sum_{j \le i} X_{x_j}$, a linear
map. The \emph{decoupled system} (II:8193) on the blocks $S_A = [\ell_A]$, $S_B = [\ell_B]$ and
three copies $S_1, S_2, S_3$ of $[\ell]$ has the equations $X_A = X^1$, $X_B = X^1(\ell_A + \cdot)$,
$X^1 = X^2$, $X^2 = X^3$ and each triangulated equation on the three copies; it is given by the
indicator of the equations $\varepsilon_A X_A(u_A) + \varepsilon_B X_B(u_B) + \sum_i
\varepsilon_i X^i(u_i) = \varepsilon_0$ it contains (II:8128), a block of sign $0$ taking any
index.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Purification; fact:completeness\_and\_soundness\_purification]
\label{lem:ar-purification}
\uses{def:ar-linear}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.satisfies_iff_pureEqn, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.accepts_iff_pure}
\leanok
A constraint holds of answers of the right lengths exactly when its purified equation holds of
the table of their linear parts. So a tailored game accepts $(a^R a^L, b^R b^L)$ at $(x, y)$
exactly when the purified equations of $L_{xy}(a^R, b^R)$ hold of the table.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:intro-forms}
\leanok
The inner product of $c$ with $a^R a^L b^R b^L 1$ splits over the five windows; the readable
windows and the affine coordinate are known, and in characteristic~$2$ they move to the right.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Triangulation and decoupling; rem:prop\_triangulated\_system]
\label{lem:ar-triangle-decouple}
\uses{def:ar-linear}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.triangle_complete, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.triangle_sound,

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok

indicator, and conversely every instance is one of the kinds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The extension; cor:triang\_and\_decoupling\_extend]

\subsection{Answer reduction: succinct descriptions through three windows}

The output indicator of paper~II (Definition~defn:decider-read, II:8381) reads, besides the
input verifier and its parameters, three strings: the two readable answers and the encoding $O$
of the decoupled system of Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-extend}. Its succinct description, the formula the
PCP checks, must hold each of the three in a block of variables of its own: the isolated player
of the PCP holds the second readable answer, and cannot hold a block containing $O$, which
depends on both. Lemma~\ref{lem:decoupled-5sat} has two answer blocks; the description here has
three \emph{windows} on the tableau of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat}
(prop:explicit-padded-succinct-deciders, II:8676, whose proof the paper sketches, II:8757). The
indicator reads $a = a^R O$ and $b = b^R$; it checks the lengths of its inputs, so the windows,
which cover them exactly, describe it completely.

\begin{lemma}[Decoupled 6SAT through three windows; prop:explicit-padded-succinct-deciders]
\label{lem:ar-window-describer}
\uses{def:multiinput-tm}
\lean{MIPRE.SAT.Circuit.formula6, MIPRE.SAT.Circuit.DescribesWindows, MIPRE.SAT.WindowDescriber,
  MIPRE.SAT.windowDescriber}
\leanok
There is a polynomial-time algorithm that, on window lengths $\ell_a, \ell_b, \ell_c$ in unary
and the inputs $(\decider, n, T, Q, \sigma, x, y)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat}, outputs a
circuit with inputs of lengths $\ell_a$, $\ell_b$, $\ell_c$, three of length
$r_0 = O(\log T + \log \sigma)$, with $2^{r_0} \geq 4T$, and six single bits, describing a
decoupled 6SAT formula on blocks of $2^{\ell_a}$, $2^{\ell_b}$, $2^{\ell_c}$ and three times
$2^{r_0}$ variables. On valid inputs with $\ell_a, \ell_b, \ell_c \le r_0$ the circuit has at
most $s_0 = \poly(\log n, \log T, Q, \sigma)$ gates, and if moreover $2^{\ell_a} + 2^{\ell_c}
\le 2T$ and $2^{\ell_b} \le 2T$, then three tables $A, B, C$ complete to a satisfying assignment
exactly when the decider accepts, within cost $T$, strings $a, b$ of length at most $T$ such that
$A$ and $C$ are the consecutive windows of lengths $2^{\ell_a}$ and $2^{\ell_c}$ at the start of
the tape encoding of $a$, and $B$ is the first $2^{\ell_b}$ bits of that of $b$. The parameters
$r_0, s_0$ are computable in time polynomial in the bit lengths of $n, T, Q, \sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{def:succinct-3sat}
\leanok
As for Lemma~\ref{lem:decoupled-5sat}. The circuit accepts $(i_1, \dots, i_6, o_1, \dots, o_6)$
when the circuit of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} accepts the last three indices and signs, or
when one of five link conditions holds: $i_4 = i_1 \land o_1 \neq o_4$; \;
$i_4 = i_2 + 2T \land o_2 \neq o_4$; \; $i_4 = i_3 + 2^{\ell_a} \land o_3 \neq o_4$; \;
$i_4 = i_5 \land o_4 \neq o_5$; \; $i_5 = i_6 \land o_5 \neq o_6$. For fixed indices the signs of
the blocks a condition does not constrain range over all values, so the last two conditions say
$w_1 = w_2 = w_3$ and the first three that the windows are read off the common assignment at the
offsets $0$, $2T$ and $2^{\ell_a}$. The windows lie inside the two input regions of the tableau,
so no padding condition is needed, and item~1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} relates a
satisfying assignment to acceptance. The offset of the third window is a power of two, which the
algorithm computes from $\ell_a$ without binary addition; the link conditions cost $O(r_0)$
gates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Answer reduction: the output indicator}

The output indicator $L^*$ of paper~II (Definition~defn:decider-read, II:8381) checks that a string
$O$ is the table of the decoupled triangulated purified system of the constraints at a pair of
questions; the PCP checks the system through that table
(fact:polynomial\_condition\_for\_satisfiability, II:8147). Here the input verifier is neither
padded nor purified (II:8377): $L^*$ cuts the readable answers at the readable lengths and
purifies on the fly (Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-purification}), so the paper's steps~3 and~4 have nothing
to check, and the table size $N = 2^\ell$ and the copy size $D = 2^{\delta}$ come from a
polynomial-time parameter program of the index.

\begin{definition}[The table and the output indicator]
\label{def:ar-indicator}
\uses{def:ar-linear,def:canonical-decider,lem:binary-degree-decomposition,
  lem:effective-normal-selfdualization}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.tableSize, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.indTable,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.TableSat, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.LstarOK, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstarProg,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstar}
\leanok
The index space of the table is $[N] \times [N] \times [D]^3 \times [64]$, the first coordinate
fastest, so that for $N = 2^\ell$ and $D = 2^{\delta}$ the binary digits of an index are those of
its coordinates in turn (II:8453); the last coordinate holds the six signs, one per digit. The
\emph{table} of a triangulated system is the indicator of its decoupled system
(Definition~\ref{def:ar-linear}) over the index space, a string of $64 N^2 D^3$ bits, and five
assignments \emph{satisfy} a table when every equation whose index it contains holds. The
\emph{check} at $(N, D)$ reads the four lengths at the two questions, the constraints that the
processor outputs on the readable answers, and the inputs $a = a^R O$ and $b = b^R$: the lengths
are at most $N$, $b$ has $N$ bits and $O$ has $64 N^2 D^3$, the number of constraints times $2N$,
plus $2N$, is at most $D$, and $O$ is the table of the triangulated purified equations, the
readable answers cut at the readable lengths. The \emph{output indicator} of a tailored verifier,
at a parameter program giving $(\ell, \delta)$ in unary, is the decider that runs the
answer-length calculator four times and the processor once, as the canonical decider does, and
then makes the check at $(2^\ell, 2^{\delta})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The table holds the decoupled system; fact:polynomial\_condition\_for\_satisfiability]
\label{lem:ar-indicator-table}
\uses{def:ar-indicator}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.indTable_eq_indLoop, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.tableSat_indTable_iff}
\leanok
The table is the output of the loops over the coordinates, the signs outermost and the first
coordinate innermost. If $N > 0$, $2N \le D$ and the triangulated equations are below $D$, five
assignments satisfy the table exactly when they satisfy the decoupled system.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:ar-triangle-decouple}
\leanok
An index decodes into its coordinates by division and remainder, which locates it in the loops.
Each kind of equation of the decoupled system has an index in range, a block of sign $0$ taking
the coordinate $0$, and the table contains it; conversely, every equation the table contains is
one of the system.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Properties of the output indicator; claim:properties\_of\_L*]
\label{lem:ar-indicator-properties}
\uses{lem:ar-indicator-table, lem:ar-extend}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstarOK_pad, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.accepts_of_lstarOK,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.tableSat_of_accepts}
\leanok
(1) On readable answers of the readable lengths, padded with zeros to $N$ bits, the check passes
for $O$ the table, when the lengths are at most $N$ and $D$ is large enough; and $O$ is the only
string it accepts after them. (2) When the check passes on the lengths and constraints of the game
at $(x, y)$, five assignments satisfying the table held by $a$ give answers that the game accepts:
the readable answers completed by the first two blocks, read at the linear lengths. (3) Answers
of the right lengths that the game accepts give the satisfying 5-tuple $\mathsf{Extend}$ of the
table, the last three blocks linear in the linear answers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-indicator-table} turns the table into the decoupled system, which is below $D$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-triangle-decouple} when there are enough triangulation variables, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-extend} relates the system to the game.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The output indicator as a program; eq:time\_bound\_L*]
\label{lem:ar-indicator-program}
\uses{def:ar-indicator}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstarFinal_apply, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstarProg_accepts,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstar_accepts_iff, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstarProg_halts,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.lstar_acceptsWithin}
\leanok
The check is a polynomial-time function of its input, and decides the check of
Definition~\ref{def:ar-indicator}. The output indicator accepts $(n, x, y, a, b)$ exactly when the
answer-length calculator gives the four lengths, the processor gives the constraints on the cut
readable answers, and the check passes. If the two programs halt within $T (|d| + 1)^k$ on every
input $(n, d)$, the output indicator halts on $(n, x, y, a, b)$ within an explicit bound,
polynomial in $T$, in the size of the input and in the time bound of the check, and so accepts

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:ar-indicator-table,lem:canonical-decider-cost}
\leanok
The check writes the readable rows, their triangulation and the table with all indices in unary,

at one more than the length of $b$, which it must equal, and $2^{\delta}$ at one more than the
length of $O$, which a satisfied length check exceeds, so nothing larger than the input is ever
written. The program is that of the canonical decider with the check in place of the
constraints' evaluation, and its running time is the sum of the five calls and of the check on a
state whose size is bounded by the calls' outputs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Answer reduction: the PCP and its soundness}

The PCP of paper~II (Definitions~defn:blocks\_of\_vars\_circuit and
defn:PCP\_of\_V\_n\_satisfied, II:8939--9013) certifies that the circuit of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-window-describer} describes a satisfiable formula and that the table $O$ of
Definition~\ref{def:ar-indicator} holds a satisfiable system. Its variables are the wires of the
circuit: the three windows, each a parity wire followed by the bits of a cell (of the two readable
answers, and of an index of $O$), the three witness indices, the six signs, and the gates. The
index of $O$ is itself the paper's $S_0$: the bits of the cells of the two linear answers and of
the three copies, and the six signs of the decoupled system. Two departures from the paper's text.
The windows hold tape encodings ($0 \mapsto 00$, $1 \mapsto 01$), so the literal of a window at
$(\pi, u)$ is $\pi \cdot g(u)$ for the polynomial $g$ of the raw table, and the paper's raw-table
assignments are kept. And a literal is true when its variable equals its sign, so its factor is
$g - \varepsilon$ where the paper writes $g + \varepsilon + 1$ with $\varepsilon$ the negated sign.

\begin{definition}[The PCP and its thirteen checks]
\label{def:ar-pcp}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.PcpDims, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.Pcp,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.AssignCheck, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.FormulaCheck,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.SystemCheck, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.Pcp.Passes,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.Pcp.Identities, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.Pcp.IndDeg}
\leanok
Let $\ell, \delta, r, s$ be the exponents of the table size and of the copy size, the width of
the witness indices and the number of gates, let $|S_0| = 2\ell + 3\delta + 6$, and let
$m = 2(\ell + 1) + (|S_0| + 1) + 3r + 6 + s$. A \emph{PCP} over a field $\F$ consists of eleven
\emph{assignments}, polynomials $g_A, g_B$ on $\ell$ variables, $g_O$ on $|S_0|$ and three
$g_{W,k}$ on $r$ (readable), $g_{La}, g_{Lb}$ on $\ell$ and three $g_{L,k}$ on $\delta$ (linear),
and of \emph{helpers}: $\alpha^R_X$ on the $m$ variables, $\alpha^L_X$ on $|S_0|$, and for each
assignment a $\beta_X$ on its variables. At a point $p \in \F^m$, for the circuit polynomial $T$,
it passes the \emph{formula check} when
\[
T(p) \bigl(p_{\pi_A} g_A(p_A) - p_{\varepsilon_A}\bigr)
\bigl(p_{\pi_B} g_B(p_B) - p_{\varepsilon_B}\bigr)
\bigl(p_{\pi_C} g_O(p_O) - p_{\varepsilon_C}\bigr)
\prod_{k} \bigl(g_{W,k}(p_{W,k}) - p_{\varepsilon_{W,k}}\bigr)
= \sum_X p_X (1 - p_X)\, \alpha^R_X(p),
\]
the \emph{system check} when, at $x = p_O$,
\[
g_O(x) \Bigl(x_{\varepsilon_A} g_{La}(x_A) + x_{\varepsilon_B} g_{Lb}(x_B)
+ \sum_k x_{\varepsilon_k} g_{L,k}(x_k) - x_{\varepsilon_0}\Bigr)
= \sum_X x_X (1 - x_X)\, \alpha^L_X(x),
\]
and the \emph{assignment check} of each assignment $g$ when $g(1 - g) = \sum_X x_X (1 - x_X)\,
\beta_X$ at the point $x$ of its block. It satisfies the checks \emph{identically} when each holds
at every point of its own space, and it has \emph{degree} $d$ when all its polynomials have
individual degree at most $d$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The checks as polynomials, and Schwartz--Zippel]
\label{lem:ar-pcp-schwartz-zippel}
\uses{def:ar-pcp}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.chkPolys, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.passes_iff,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.degreeOf_chkPolys_le, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.chkPolys_eq_zero,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.identities_of_chkPolys_eq_zero,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.identities_of_dense}
\leanok
The thirteen checks pass at a point exactly when thirteen polynomials vanish there, of individual
degree at most $d_T + 6(d + 1)$ for a PCP of degree $d$ and a circuit polynomial of individual
degree $d_T$. A PCP of degree $d$ over $\F_q$ passing the checks on a set of points of density
more than $m (d_T + 6(d + 1))/q$ has all thirteen polynomials zero, and so satisfies the checks
identically.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:schwartz-zippel}
\leanok
Each of the six literal factors has individual degree at most $d + 1$ and each certificate sum
at most $d + 2$; an assignment check is renamed along an injective map of the variables, which
keeps degrees. A nonzero polynomial of individual degree $e$ in $m$ variables vanishes on a set
of density at most $m e/q$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:schwartz-zippel}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[From the identities to the formula and the system;
prop:completeness\_and\_soundness\_of\_PCP\_for\_V\_n]
\label{lem:ar-pcp-sound}
\uses{def:ar-pcp,lem:pcp-circuit-arithmetization,lem:ar-window-describer}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.isAssignment_of_assignCheck,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.tableSat_of_identities, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.formula6Sat_of_identities}
\leanok
Over a field of characteristic two, let a PCP satisfy the checks identically for the circuit
polynomial of a well-formed circuit with the PCP's inputs. Then every assignment is $0$ or $1$ on
the Boolean cube; the tables of $g_{La}$, $g_{Lb}$ and the $g_{L,k}$ satisfy the system that the
table of $g_O$ holds; and the tables of the windows, $\pi \cdot g(u)$ at the index of parity
$\pi$ and cell $u$ for $g = g_A, g_B, g_O$, with the tables of the $g_{W,k}$, satisfy the
decoupled 6SAT formula that the circuit describes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{def:ar-indicator}
\leanok
On the cube the certificate sums vanish, so an assignment check says $g(1 - g) = 0$. At an index
where $g_O$ is one, the system check says that the equation holds in $\F$, hence in $\F_2$, the
binary digits of the index being those of its coordinates. For a clause that the circuit
accepts, the circuit polynomial is one at the Boolean point holding the clause's input and the
gates' values (Lemma~\ref{lem:pcp-circuit-arithmetization}), so a literal factor vanishes there,
which says that the literal is true.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The game accepts the decoded answers; cor:functional\_viewpoint\_final, item 2]
\label{thm:ar-pcp-accepts}
\uses{lem:ar-pcp-sound, lem:ar-pcp-schwartz-zippel}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.accepts_of_identities, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.accepts_of_dense}
\leanok
Let a circuit describe the output indicator $L^*$ of a tailored verifier $\verifier$ at
$(n, x, y)$ through windows of index lengths $\ell + 1$, $\ell + 1$ and $|S_0| + 1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-window-describer}), the parameter program giving $(\ell, \delta)$ at $n$. If
a PCP of degree $d$ over $\F_q$, of characteristic two, passes the thirteen checks on a set of
points of density more than $m (5 + 6(d + 1))/q$, then the game $\verifier_n$ accepts at
$(x, y)$ the answers whose readable parts are the tables of $g_A$ and $g_B$ and whose linear
parts are those of $g_{La}$ and $g_{Lb}$, each cut at its length.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:ar-window-describer, lem:ar-indicator-properties, lem:ar-indicator-program}
\leanok
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:ar-pcp-schwartz-zippel} and~\ref{lem:ar-pcp-sound} the tables of the windows
satisfy the formula, so $L^*$ accepts strings $a, b$ whose tape encodings the windows hold. The
odd positions of the windows read the strings: the readable answers are the tables of $g_A$ and
$g_B$, and the string $O$ that $L^*$ compared is the table of $g_O$. The linear tables satisfy the
system that table holds, and item~2 of Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-indicator-properties} gives the
acceptance.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Answer reduction: the completeness of the PCP}

The honest PCP of paper~II is $\mathsf{Induce}_C$ (Definition~defn:inducing\_to\_a\_PCP,
II:9081): the low-degree encodings of the tables, and helpers obtained by division by the
polynomials $X_i(1 - X_i)$ (the paper's $\mathrm{Div}$ and $\mathrm{Mod}$). The paper needs the
helpers of the linear part to depend linearly on the linear tables; here they are the values of
a linear map, chosen once.

\begin{lemma}[Linear certificates for vanishing on the cube]
\label{lem:ar-linear-certificates}
\uses{lem:pcp-zero-basis}
\lean{MIPRE.LowDegree.cubeVanishing, MIPRE.LowDegree.mem_cubeVanishing, MIPRE.LowDegree.cert,
  MIPRE.LowDegree.degreeOf_cert_le, MIPRE.LowDegree.cert_sum}
\leanok
For every $m$ and $d$ there is a linear map $f \mapsto (c_i(f))_i$ from polynomials in $m$
variables over a field to $m$-tuples of polynomials of individual degree at most $d$ such that
$f = \sum_i c_i(f) X_i (1 - X_i)$ whenever $f$ has individual degree at most $d$ and vanishes on
$\{0, 1\}^m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The map $c \mapsto \sum_i c_i X_i (1 - X_i)$, restricted to the tuples whose image vanishes on the
cube, maps onto the polynomials of individual degree at most $d$ vanishing on the cube, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:pcp-zero-basis}. Compose a linear right inverse of it with a linear projection onto
that subspace.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The honest PCP; $\mathsf{Induce}_C$]
\label{def:ar-induce}
\uses{def:ar-pcp, lem:ar-linear-certificates}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.encTbl, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.RTables, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.LTables,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.induce}
\leanok
The \emph{encoding} of a table $t$ on $\N$ in $k$ variables is the multilinear polynomial whose
value at the binary digits of $c < 2^k$ is $t(c)$. From Boolean \emph{readable tables} (the cells
of the two readable answers, the table $O$ and the three witness blocks) and $\F_2$-valued
\emph{linear tables} (the cells of the two linear answers and the three copies), the \emph{honest
PCP} of a circuit polynomial $T$ has the encodings of the tables as its assignments, and as
helpers the linear certificates (Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-linear-certificates}) of the left-hand sides
of its checks: of the formula polynomial at degree $17$, of the system polynomial at degree $3$,
and of $g(1 - g)$ for each assignment $g$ at degree $2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Completeness of the PCP; prop:completeness\_and\_soundness\_of\_PCP\_for\_V\_n]
\label{lem:ar-pcp-complete}
\uses{def:ar-induce,def:ar-indicator,lem:ar-window-describer}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.indDeg_induce, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.assignChecks_induce,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.formulaCheck_induce, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.systemCheck_induce,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.identities_induce}
\leanok
Over a field of characteristic two, the honest PCP has degree $17$ and its assignment checks hold
identically. For the circuit polynomial of a well-formed circuit with the PCP's inputs, its
formula check holds identically when the window tables, $\pi \cdot t(u)$ at the index of parity
$\pi$ and cell $u$, and the witness tables satisfy the formula that the circuit describes; its
system check holds identically when the linear tables satisfy the system that the table $O$
holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:pcp-circuit-arithmetization}
\leanok
An encoding is $0$ or $1$ on the cube and multilinear, so $g(1 - g)$ vanishes on the cube and has
individual degree at most $2$. At a Boolean point where the circuit polynomial is one, the
circuit accepts the clause whose indices and signs the point holds on its input wires
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pcp-circuit-arithmetization}), a literal of that clause is true, and its factor
vanishes there; so the formula polynomial vanishes on the cube. At an index where $O$ is one,
the equation holds in $\F_2$, hence in $\F$. In each case
Lemma~\ref{lem:ar-linear-certificates} gives the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The readable and the linear parts of the honest PCP]
\label{lem:ar-pcp-parts}
\uses{def:ar-induce}
\lean{MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.readable_induce, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.linear_induce_add,
  MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.res_encB, MIPRE.Tailored.AnsRed.resZ_encZ}
\leanok
The readable part of the honest PCP, the readable assignments and their helpers, depends only
on the readable tables. Its linear part is $\F_2$-affine in the linear tables: the linear
assignments and their helpers are additive, and the helpers of the system check affine. The
tables of the encodings on the cube are the encoded tables.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The encodings are linear, the system polynomial is affine in the linear assignments, and
$g \mapsto g(1 - g)$ is additive in characteristic two; the certificates are values of a linear
map.
\end{proof}
