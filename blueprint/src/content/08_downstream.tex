\section{Downstream results}
\label{ch:downstream}

Potential extensions of the project once the main theorem is in place. They are listed
roughly in order of increasing formalization cost. The first two stay within
finite-dimensional linear algebra plus elementary functional analysis, while the
operator-algebraic consequences require von Neumann algebra infrastructure that
largely does not yet exist in Mathlib and would constitute a major sub-project of
independent value.

\subsection{Uncomputability of the value}

Corollary~\ref{cor:value-uncomputable} is already a headline consequence under the main
theorem's compression premise: no algorithm can approximate the (synchronous or quantum) value of a
nonlocal game to within an additive constant $c < \frac14$, since an estimate of additive
error $c$ separates value $1$ from value $\frac12$ exactly when $c < \frac14$ (at
$c = \frac14$ the estimate $\frac34$ is consistent with both). The threshold rises to the
trivial $\frac12$ once the gap is amplified: repeating the game drives the soundness value
below any $\delta > 0$ while preserving value $1$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}), at a cost polynomial in the description, so for
every $c < \frac12$ no algorithm approximates the value to within additive error $c$. That
stronger form depends on the repetition theorem as well as on the main theorem.

\subsection{Semidecision from both sides}
\label{sec:ds-semidecision}

The non-explicit route mentioned below, and the explicit separating game, both rest on the
same pair of procedures: the entangled value is semidecidable from below, the commuting value
from above. The first is already available and formalized ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}, which is ledger node $1.8.1$ --- so only the second is
stated here, with the two ingredients it rests on.

\begin{theorem}[NPA convergence and compactness]
\label{thm:npa-convergence}
\ledgernode{1.8.2, 1.8.2.6}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,lem:npa-dilation,lem:npa-levels,lem:npa-moment-bound,lem:npa-diagonal,lem:npa-gns}
Fix a finite bipartite scenario. The normalized full-word level-$k$ NPA feasible sets have
uniformly bounded moments, their correlation projections $K_k$ are compact and decreasing, and
$\bigcap_k K_k = C_{qc}$, with the POVM and projective definitions of $C_{qc}$ equivalent. In
particular $C_{qc}$ is compact, every finite game payoff attains its maximum over it, and
$C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$. No finite-dimensional attainment and no convergence rate is
asserted. The five lemmas below are its ingredients.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Comments.} Not formalized. The Lean proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson} needs
only two consequences of this theorem, compactness of $C_{qc}$ and upper semidecision of
$\omegaco$, and obtains both without the hierarchy: Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}, through the quadratic module of
Definition~\ref{def:measurement-algebra}. The level presentation, the moment bound, the
diagonal limit and the projective dilation stay open.

\begin{lemma}[Common projective dilation of commuting POVMs]
\label{lem:npa-dilation}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.1}
\uses{def:co-strategy-bipartite}
Commuting finite-outcome POVMs admit a correlation-preserving common \emph{projective}
dilation. For $Vh = (\sqrt{A_a} h)_a$ the operator
$U = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & V^* \\ V & \Id - VV^* \end{pmatrix}$ is a self-adjoint
unitary on $\mH \oplus \mH^r$ whose conjugated coordinate projections compress to $A_a$, and
all questions share one embedding. Across the two players the block entries stay in commuting
algebras, so iterating the dilation preserves both commutation and the joint distribution. Zero
effects and arbitrary Hilbert space dimension are allowed.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Comments.} Two steps here are one line on paper and real work in Lean. That $U$ is a
self-adjoint unitary uses $V^*V = \Id$, which is exactly $\sum_a A_a = \Id$, and then that
$VV^*$ is a projection; $U^2 = \Id$ follows. And the commutation claim needs more than
$[A_a, B] = 0$: the dilation is built from $\sqrt{A_a}$, so what has to commute with $B$ is
$\sqrt{A_a}$, which follows from $[A_a, B] = 0$ by continuous functional calculus and not
formally. In the commuting-operator model --- where both players act on \emph{one} space and
merely commute, rather than on tensor factors --- that functional-calculus step is what keeps
the dilated PVMs commuting, so it cannot be skipped.

\begin{lemma}[The full-word levels, and that they are effectively enumerable]
\label{lem:npa-levels}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.2}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite}
For a finite Bell scenario, level $k$ of the full-word NPA hierarchy consists of the normalized
positive semidefinite moment matrices on words of length at most $k$, subject to the
contextual projection, completeness and cross-commutation relations of degree at most $2k$,
with two-letter moments equal to the correlation coordinates. These constraints are finite and
effectively enumerable, every $C_{qc}$ correlation is feasible at every level, the levels are
nested, and this redundant presentation agrees with the standard reduced one by
degree-preserving linear substitutions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Moment entries are bounded, so the levels are compact]
\label{lem:npa-moment-bound}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.3}
\uses{lem:npa-levels}
Every feasible level-$k$ moment satisfies $\abs{L_k(w)} \leq 1$ for $\abs{w} \leq 2k$: the
identity $\sum_a \Gamma[e_a v, e_a v] = \Gamma[v,v]$ with positivity bounds each
leading-generator diagonal by its suffix diagonal, and induction together with the positive
semidefinite $2 \times 2$ minors bounds every entry. Hence each finite-level feasible set, and
its correlation projection, is compact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[A diagonal subsequence, from certificates that need not be compatible]
\label{lem:npa-diagonal}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.4}
\uses{lem:npa-moment-bound}
If a correlation is feasible at every level, a countable diagonal subsequence of the bounded
certificates --- which need \emph{not} be compatible across levels, and this is the step where
a paraphrase overclaims --- defines a normalized positive functional on the polynomial
$*$-algebra modulo the measurement relations. Every polynomial positivity constraint and every
finite contextual relation survives the limit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[GNS: a positive functional gives a commuting projective representation]
\label{lem:npa-gns}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.5}
\uses{lem:npa-diagonal}
A normalized positive functional on the measurement $*$-algebra yields a separable commuting
projective representation. Quotient by the zero-norm subspace and complete; left multiplication
by a projection generator $g$ is a contraction because
$\norm{u}^2 - \norm{gu}^2 = \norm{(\Id - g)u}^2$, so the null subspace is invariant, and the
PVM and cross-commutation identities extend from the dense polynomial quotient. The
distinguished unit vector is the class of $1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Decidability of the first-order theory of real closed fields]
\label{lem:rcf-decision}
\ledgernode{1.8.3}
A first-order sentence over real closed fields with rational coefficients is decidable; in
particular so is feasibility of a finite conjunction of polynomial equalities and weak or
strict inequalities over $\R$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Source.} Tarski; the effective form used here is Basu, Pollack and
Roy~\cite{BPR06}. This is an import: the companion ledger records it as \emph{admitted}, one
of only three assumptions in the campaign (Remark~\ref{rem:admitted-nodes}), and one of the
two imports carried by this chapter rather than by the main theorem. The other is
Lemma~\ref{lem:kirchberg-forward}.

\subsection{Upper semidecision through a Positivstellensatz}
\label{sec:ds-positivstellensatz}

The Lean proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} does not go through the NPA levels. It works
with one object: the quadratic module of the measurement relations in the free $*$-algebra,
with POVM generators. Its Archimedean property and a Riesz extension give a noncommutative
Positivstellensatz (Theorem~\ref{thm:nc-positivstellensatz}; compare~\cite{HM04}), which turns
$\omegaco(\game) < r$ into the existence of an exact sum-of-squares certificate with
Gaussian-integer coefficients (Lemma~\ref{lem:sos-certificates}). The same states and the same
GNS construction give compactness of $C_{qc}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact}). No real-closed-field
decision, no dilation to projections, and no semidefinite hierarchy is involved.

\begin{definition}[The measurement algebra and its cone]
\label{def:measurement-algebra}
\uses{def:game}
\lean{MIPRE.NCPoly, MIPRE.NCPoly.eval, MIPRE.NCPoly.relSpan, MIPRE.NCPoly.qmod,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.Gen, MIPRE.Tsirelson.rels, MIPRE.Tsirelson.cone, MIPRE.Tsirelson.gamePoly,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.IsConeState}
\leanok
Let $P$ be the complex polynomials in noncommuting variables $e_{xa}$ ($x \in \mX$,
$a \in \mA$) and $f_{yb}$ ($y \in \mY$, $b \in \mB$), with the involution that conjugates
coefficients, reverses words and fixes the variables. The \emph{relations} are
$\sum_a e_{xa} - 1$, $\sum_b f_{yb} - 1$ and $e_{xa} f_{yb} - f_{yb} e_{xa}$; $I$ is the
complex span of $u \rho v$ over relations $\rho$ and $u, v \in P$. The \emph{cone} $M$ is the
set of finite nonnegative real combinations of the elements $s^* s$, $s^* e_{xa} s$,
$s^* f_{yb} s$ ($s \in P$), of the elements of $I$, and of the anti-hermitian elements of
$P$. The game polynomial of $\game$ is $W_\game = \sum_{x,y,a,b} \mu(x,y) D(x,y,a,b)\,
e_{xa} f_{yb}$. A \emph{cone state} is a complex-linear $L : P \to \C$ with $L(1) = 1$ and
$\mathrm{Re}\, L(m) \geq 0$ for every $m \in M$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Comments.} The Lean $P$ is \texttt{MonoidAlgebra} over the free monoid, under a
type synonym: Mathlib's star on \texttt{FreeAlgebra} is complex-linear, and the synonym keeps
the conjugate-linear star from becoming a global instance on \texttt{MonoidAlgebra}.

\begin{lemma}[The cone is Archimedean and positive on strategies]
\label{lem:cone-archimedean}
\uses{def:measurement-algebra,def:co-value-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.NCPoly.star_mul_mul_mem_qmod_of_mem, MIPRE.NCPoly.archimedean_of_gen,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.cone_archimedean, MIPRE.NCPoly.re_inner_eval_nonneg,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.strategy_nonneg, MIPRE.Tsirelson.value_le_of_mem}
\leanok
$a^* M a \subseteq M$ for every $a \in P$, and for every $y \in P$ some real $N$ has
$N \cdot 1 + y \in M$. Evaluating the variables at the effects of a commuting-operator
strategy gives $\mathrm{Re} \langle \psi, m(E, F) \psi \rangle \geq 0$ for every $m \in M$;
in particular $r \cdot 1 - W_\game \in M$ bounds the value of every strategy by $r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$1 - e_{xa} = \sum_{a' \neq a} e_{xa'} - (\sum_{a'} e_{xa'} - 1) \in M$, and
$e - e^2 = e(1 - e)e + (1 - e)e(1 - e) \in M$, so $1 - g^2 \in M$ for every variable $g$ and,
telescoping, $1 - w^* w \in M$ for every word $w$. Then
$(1 + \abs{\alpha}^2) \cdot 1 + \alpha w + (\alpha w)^* = (1 + \alpha w)^*(1 + \alpha w) +
\abs{\alpha}^2 (1 - w^* w)$, and the anti-hermitian part of a monomial lies in $M$. On a
strategy the relations evaluate to $0$, $\langle \psi, s^* g s \psi \rangle =
\langle s\psi, g\, s\psi \rangle \geq 0$ for positive $g$, and anti-hermitian operators have
imaginary expectations.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cone states are the states of commuting-operator strategies]
\label{lem:cone-state-gns}
\uses{lem:cone-archimedean,def:correlation-sets}
\lean{MIPRE.Tsirelson.isConeState_stateOf, MIPRE.GNS.GenData.strategy,
  MIPRE.GNS.GenData.strategy_correlation, MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateStrategy,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateStrategy_correlation, MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateStrategy_value,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.mem_Cqc_iff}
\leanok
For finite alphabets, the functional $z \mapsto \langle \psi, z(E, F) \psi \rangle$ of a
commuting-operator strategy is a cone state; conversely every cone state $L$ is that of a
commuting-operator strategy whose correlation is $\mathrm{Re}\, L(e_{xa} f_{yb})$. So $C_{qc}$
is the set of the correlations $\mathrm{Re}\, L(e_{xa} f_{yb})$ over cone states $L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A cone state is hermitian (since $z - z^*$ and $i(z + z^*)$ are anti-hermitian), vanishes on
$I$ (since $\pm j, \pm i j \in I \subseteq M$) and is positive on $s^* s$ and $s^* g s$. The
GNS construction completes $P$ for the seminorm $\mathrm{Re}\, L(a^* a)^{1/2}$; left
multiplication by a variable $g$ is a contraction because $a^*(1 - g^2) a \in M$, and extends
to a positive operator on the completion. The normalizations and the cross-commutation hold
because the corresponding vectors are null, and the class of $1$ is a unit vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$C_{qc}$ is compact]
\label{lem:cqc-compact}
\uses{lem:cone-state-gns,lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\lean{MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateSpace, MIPRE.Tsirelson.isCompact_stateSpace,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.image_correlationOf_stateSpace, MIPRE.isCompact_Cqc, MIPRE.isClosed_Cqc,
  MIPRE.Cqa_subset_Cqc}
\leanok
For finite alphabets $C_{qc}$ is compact, hence closed, and $C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A cone state is determined by its values on words. The set of those value families that are
cone states is closed in the product topology, since each condition involves finitely many
words, and lies in the product of closed unit disks, since $2 \cdot 1 + \alpha w +
(\alpha w)^* \in M$ for $\abs{\alpha} = 1$ gives $\abs{L(w)} \leq 1$. By Tychonoff it is
compact, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-state-gns} its continuous image under
$L \mapsto \mathrm{Re}\, L(e_{xa} f_{yb})$ is $C_{qc}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Positivstellensatz for the commuting-operator value]
\label{thm:nc-positivstellensatz}
\uses{lem:cone-state-gns}
\lean{MIPRE.exists_separating_functional_of_archimedean,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.exists_isConeState_of_not_mem, MIPRE.Tsirelson.sub_gamePoly_mem_cone_of_lt,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.commutingOperatorValue_lt_iff}
\leanok
If $\omegaco(\game) < r$ then $r \cdot 1 - W_\game \in M$. With nonempty answer sets,
$\omegaco(\game) < r$ holds exactly when $r' \cdot 1 - W_\game \in M$ for some $r' < r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\leanok
If $h = r \cdot 1 - W_\game \notin M$, the Riesz extension theorem applied to the cone
$M + \R_{\geq 0}(-h)$, with the functional $t \cdot 1 \mapsto t$ on the span of $1$, gives a
real functional $L_0 \geq 0$ on $M$ with $L_0(1) = 1$ and $L_0(h) \leq 0$; the Archimedean
property supplies both hypotheses. Its complexification $L_0(z) - i L_0(iz)$ is a cone state,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-state-gns} turns it into a strategy of value $L_0(W_\game) \geq r >
\omegaco(\game)$, a contradiction. The converse is Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-archimedean}, with a
strategy supplied by the deterministic one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact sum-of-squares certificates]
\label{lem:sos-certificates}
\uses{thm:nc-positivstellensatz}
\lean{MIPRE.Tsirelson.Dominant, MIPRE.Tsirelson.re_inner_eval_ge,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.commutingOperatorValue_lt_of_certificate,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.exists_certificate_of_lt, MIPRE.Tsirelson.exists_certificate_iff}
\leanok
Call $z \in P$ \emph{dominant} if the real part of its constant coefficient exceeds the sum of
$\abs{\mathrm{Re}\, z_w} + \abs{\mathrm{Im}\, z_w}$ over the nonempty words $w$. Let
$\kappa > 0$ and suppose the answer sets are nonempty. Then $\omegaco(\game) < r$ if and only
if there are $N \geq 1$, finitely many pairs $(g_i, s_i)$ with $g_i \in \{1, e_{xa},
f_{yb}\}$ and $s_i \in P$, and finitely many $(c_t, w_t, \rho_t, w'_t)$ with $c_t \in \C$,
words $w_t, w'_t$ and relations $\rho_t$, all coefficients Gaussian integers, such that
$T + T^*$ is dominant, where $T = N^2 \kappa (r \cdot 1 - W_\game) - \sum_i s_i^* g_i s_i -
\sum_t c_t w_t \rho_t w'_t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Soundness: every word acts as a contraction, so on every strategy
$2 N^2 \kappa (r - \text{value}) \geq \mathrm{Re} \langle \psi, (T + T^*)\psi \rangle$ is at
least the dominance margin of $T + T^*$, uniformly over strategies. Completeness: take
$\omegaco(\game) < r' < r$; Theorem~\ref{thm:nc-positivstellensatz} writes
$\kappa(r \cdot 1 - W_\game) = \varepsilon \cdot 1 + \sum_i \sigma_i^* g_i \sigma_i +
\sum_t c_t w_t \rho_t w'_t + a$ with $\varepsilon = \kappa(r - r') > 0$ and $a$
anti-hermitian, which symmetrization removes. Rounding the coefficients of the $\sigma_i$ at
scale $N$ and the $c_t$ at scale $N^2$ changes $T + T^*$ by $O(N)$ in the $\ell^1$ norm of
coefficients (submultiplicative and star-invariant), against a constant term
$2N^2\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Upper semidecision of the commuting-operator value]
\label{lem:valco-upper-re}
\ledgernode{1.8.4, 1.8.4.1}
\uses{def:game-description,def:co-value-bipartite,lem:sos-certificates,def:commuting-upper-re}
\lean{MIPRE.commutingUpperRE, MIPRE.Tsirelson.Coded.CheckUpper,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.Coded.primrecRel_checkUpper, MIPRE.Tsirelson.checkUpper_iff}
\leanok
Write $\game_g$ for the game described by a game description $g$
(Definition~\ref{def:game-description}). The set
\[
  \{ (g, p, q) : g \text{ a game description},\ p, q \in \N,\ \omegaco(\game_g) < p/q \}
\]
is recursively enumerable, with $p/0 = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:value-lower-approx}
\leanok
The certificates of Lemma~\ref{lem:sos-certificates} at $\kappa = q\, W_g$, where $W_g$ is
the total weight of $g$, have integer data: $\kappa(p/q \cdot 1 - W_{\game_g}) = p W_g \cdot 1 -
q \sum \mathrm{wt}(x,y)[D]\, e_{xa} f_{yb}$. The checker computes $T + T^*$ on lists of
monomials with coded Gaussian-integer coefficients, merges equal words, tests dominance, and
rejects $q = 0$ and malformed letters or relation indices; it is primitive recursive, and a
certificate passes exactly when $\omegaco(\game_g) < p/q$. Enumerating certificates gives the
semidecider.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof route.} The correspondence with nodes $1.8.4$ and $1.8.4.1$ is at the level
of the conclusion. The paper runs the semidefinite hierarchy for upper bounds; the ledger makes
that exact through the admitted real-closed-field decision of node $1.8.3$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:rcf-decision}). The proof here uses neither, and so does not rest on $1.8.3$;
\texttt{scripts/ledger-sync.py}, which walks the ledger's declared dependencies, still lists
$1.8.3$ among the imports reached from this lemma, as expected. The ledger statement is also
broader: it covers games with rational data and rational $r$, and the dovetailed form that
halts exactly when $\omegaco(\game) < 1$, on succinct descriptions. The paper's construction
of Remark~\ref{rem:separation-premises} consumes those forms; Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} need only the form above.

The value here is the \emph{bipartite} commuting-operator value of
Definition~\ref{def:co-value-bipartite}, not the tracial $\valco$ of
Definition~\ref{def:commuting-value}: the polynomial algebra has both players' variables and
its states give the bipartite set $C_{qc}$. The two must not be interchanged ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:tracial-le-co} gives $\valco \leq \omegaco$ and the reverse comparison is false
in general --- so this lemma yields a \emph{sound} semidecider for $\valco(\game) < r$ and not
a complete one. That is all the arguments below need, since they consume the correlation sets
rather than the tracial value.

\subsection{Correlation sets and the conditional separation}

\begin{definition}[Correlation sets]
\label{def:correlation-sets}
\uses{def:tensor-strategy,def:co-strategy-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.Game.payoff, MIPRE.TensorProductStrategy.correlation, MIPRE.Cq, MIPRE.Cqa,
  MIPRE.Cqc}
\leanok
Fix finite sets $\mX, \mY, \mA, \mB$. A \emph{correlation} is a function
$p : \mX \times \mY \times \mA \times \mB \to \R$, written $p(a,b|x,y)$, and its
\emph{payoff} in a game $\game$ on these alphabets is
$\sum_{x,y,a,b} \mu(x,y)\, D(x,y,a,b)\, p(a,b|x,y)$. The set $C_q$ consists of the
correlations $\bra{\psi} A^x_a \ot B^y_b \ket{\psi}$ of finite-dimensional
tensor-product strategies (Definition~\ref{def:tensor-strategy}); $C_{qa}$ is its closure
in $\R^{\mX \times \mY \times \mA \times \mB}$; and $C_{qc}$ consists of the correlations
$\bra{\psi} E^x_a F^y_b \ket{\psi}$ of commuting-operator strategies
(Definition~\ref{def:co-strategy-bipartite}). The value of a strategy of either kind is
the payoff of its correlation.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Comments.} The Lean $C_q$ uses the projective measurements of
Definition~\ref{def:tensor-strategy}, and $C_{qc}$ the POVMs of
Definition~\ref{def:co-strategy-bipartite} on a Hilbert space of any dimension. The
companion paper defines the finite-dimensional set both ways and $C_{qc}$ on separable
spaces~\cite{AuditSync26}. The closures of the projective and POVM finite-dimensional sets
agree by Naimark dilation, and a commuting-operator correlation is realized on the
separable subspace generated cyclically by $\psi$; neither identification is used below.

\begin{lemma}[Basic properties of the correlation sets]
\label{lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\uses{def:correlation-sets,def:co-value-bipartite,def:q-value}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_nonneg,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_sum,
  MIPRE.commutingOperatorValue_nonneg, MIPRE.commutingOperatorValue_le_one,
  MIPRE.TensorProductStrategy.correlation_toCommuting, MIPRE.Cq_subset_Cqc,
  MIPRE.quantumValue_le_commutingOperatorValue, MIPRE.payoff_le_quantumValue_of_mem_Cqa,
  MIPRE.payoff_le_commutingOperatorValue_of_mem_Cqc, MIPRE.Cqa_subset_Cqc_of_isClosed}
\leanok
Commuting-operator correlations are nonnegative and sum to one for each question pair, so
$0 \leq \omegaco(\game) \leq 1$. A tensor-product strategy is a commuting-operator strategy
with the same correlation, so $C_q \subseteq C_{qc}$ and
$\valstar(\game) \leq \omegaco(\game)$. The payoff of every correlation in $C_{qa}$ is at
most $\valstar(\game)$, and that of every correlation in $C_{qc}$ at most
$\omegaco(\game)$. If $C_{qc}$ is closed then $C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$E^x_a F^y_b$ is the product of two commuting positive operators, hence positive, which
gives nonnegativity; the sum over $a, b$ is $\langle \psi, \psi \rangle = 1$. On
$\C^{d_A} \ot \C^{d_B}$ the operators $A^x_a \ot \Id$ and $\Id \ot B^y_b$ are commuting
projections with the same Born probabilities. The payoff is linear, hence continuous, so
the bound $\valstar(\game)$ on $C_q$ passes to its closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Upper semidecision of the commuting-operator value]
\label{def:commuting-upper-re}
\uses{def:game-description,def:co-value-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingUpperRE}
\leanok
The proposition that the set of triples $(d, p, q)$ of a game description $d$ and
$p, q \in \N$ with $\omegaco(\game_d) < p/q$ is recursively enumerable, where $p/0 = 0$: the
threshold encoding of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}. Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}
proves it; Lemma~\ref{lem:tsirelson-conditional} takes it as a hypothesis.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional separation]
\label{lem:tsirelson-conditional}
\uses{lem:correlation-sets-basic,def:game-description,def:commuting-upper-re}
\lean{MIPRE.HaltingReductionQuantum,
  MIPRE.exists_quantumValue_lt_commutingOperatorValue, MIPRE.exists_mem_Cqc_not_mem_Cqa,
  MIPRE.exists_Cqa_ne_Cqc, MIPRE.tsirelson_of_upperRE_of_isClosed}
\leanok
Suppose that there is a computable map $g$ from machines to game descriptions with
$\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) = 1$ when $\machine$ halts on the empty input and
$\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) \leq \frac12$ otherwise (the conclusion of
Corollary~\ref{cor:main-quantum}), and that the relation $\omegaco(\game_d) < p/q$ on game
descriptions $d$ and $p, q \in \N$ is recursively enumerable, with $p/0 = 0$ (the form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} used here). Then some described game has
$\valstar(\game_d) < \omegaco(\game_d)$, and in its scenario some correlation lies in
$C_{qc}$ but not in $C_{qa}$. If moreover $C_{qc}$ is closed in every finite scenario, then
$C_{qa} \subsetneq C_{qc}$ in that scenario, whose two players share the question alphabet
and the answer alphabet.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
If $\omegaco(\game_d) \leq \valstar(\game_d)$ for every $d$, the two values agree on every
described game, since $\valstar \leq \omegaco$. Then $\machine$ fails to halt exactly when
$\omegaco(\game_{g(\machine)}) < \frac34$, so non-halting is the pullback of an r.e. relation
along a computable map, hence r.e., which it is not
(Theorem~\ref{thm:halting-undecidable}). So some $d$ has $\valstar(\game_d) <
\omegaco(\game_d)$, and some commuting-operator strategy has value above $\valstar(\game_d)$;
its correlation is in $C_{qc}$, and its payoff exceeds the bound $\valstar(\game_d)$ that
holds on $C_{qa}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}). With $C_{qc}$ closed,
$C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$, and the inclusion is strict.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Failure of Tsirelson's problem}

\begin{corollary}[Tsirelson's problem, negative answer]
\label{cor:tsirelson}
\ledgernode{1.8.5}
\uses{def:correlation-sets,lem:tsirelson-conditional,cor:main-quantum,lem:valco-upper-re,
  lem:cqc-compact}
\lean{MIPRE.tsirelson, MIPRE.tsirelson_of_haltingReduction, MIPRE.tsirelson_of_upperRE}
\leanok
There exist finite question and answer sets for which the closure $C_{qa}$ of the set
of finite-dimensional quantum correlations is strictly contained in the set $C_{qc}$
of commuting-operator correlations.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsirelson-conditional}, with the halting reduction of
Corollary~\ref{cor:main-quantum}, the upper semidecider of Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re},
and the closedness of $C_{qc}$ in every finite scenario (Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact}).
\end{proof}

The Lean proof uses no lower semidecider, no real-closed-field decision and no NPA levels. The witness scenario has one
question alphabet and one answer alphabet shared by the two players. This is the
non-explicit route; it does not use Theorem~\ref{thm:separation}, which follows from the same
two inputs and the recursion theorem. The ledger's proof of
node $1.8.5$ dovetails the lower semidecider of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx} against
the upper one into a promise decider. The argument here needs only the upper one, because
$\valstar \leq \omegaco$ always holds. Non-closure of the unclosed set $C_q$ itself is
Slofstra's earlier theorem~\cite{Slo19}, which is not formalized here.

\subsection{Separation of quantum and commuting values}

\begin{theorem}[Separation]
\label{thm:separation}
\ledgernode{1.8.8, 1.8.9, 1.8.10, 1.8.11}
\uses{def:correlation-sets,lem:correlation-sets-basic,def:commuting-upper-re,
  lem:tsirelson-conditional,lem:valco-upper-re,cor:main-quantum,lem:cqc-compact}
\lean{MIPRE.separation, MIPRE.separation_of_upperRE,
  MIPRE.exists_quantumValue_le_half_commutingOperatorValue_eq_one,
  MIPRE.exists_mem_Cqc_payoff_eq_commutingOperatorValue}
\leanok
There is an explicit finite bipartite game $\game^{\sep}$, given by a game description, with
$\valstar(\game^{\sep}) \leq \frac12$ and $\omegaco(\game^{\sep}) = 1$. Moreover, some
correlation $p \in C_{qc}$ in the scenario of $\game^{\sep}$ has payoff $1$ in $\game^{\sep}$
and lies outside $C_{qa}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Let $g$ be the computable map of Corollary~\ref{cor:main-quantum}, from machines to game
descriptions, with $\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) = 1$ when $\machine$ halts on the empty
input and $\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) \leq \frac12$ otherwise. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} at $p = q = 1$, the set of machines $\machine$ with
$\omegaco(\game_{g(\machine)}) < 1$ is recursively enumerable. Kleene's recursion theorem
therefore gives a machine $\machine_{\sep}$ that halts on the empty input exactly when
$\omegaco(\game_{g(\machine_{\sep})}) < 1$. Put $\game^{\sep} = \game_{g(\machine_{\sep})}$.

If $\machine_{\sep}$ halted, then $\valstar(\game^{\sep}) = 1$, so
$\omegaco(\game^{\sep}) = 1$ by $\valstar \leq \omegaco$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}), and $\machine_{\sep}$ would not halt. So
$\machine_{\sep}$ does not halt. Hence $\valstar(\game^{\sep}) \leq \frac12$, and
$\omegaco(\game^{\sep})$ is not below $1$, so it equals $1$.

For the correlation: the payoff is continuous, and $C_{qc}$ is compact
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact}) and nonempty, so the payoff attains its maximum on $C_{qc}$ at
some $p$. That maximum is $\omegaco(\game^{\sep}) = 1$, since every commuting-operator
strategy has value equal to the payoff of its correlation. Payoffs on $C_{qa}$ are at most
$\valstar(\game^{\sep}) \leq \frac12$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}), so
$p \notin C_{qa}$.
\end{proof}

The recursion theorem used in Lean is the two-argument form of Mathlib's
\texttt{Nat.Partrec.Code.fixed\_point}, for partial recursive codes. No running-time bound is
needed, so the efficient form of Lemma~\ref{lem:kleene} is not used.

\paragraph{Proof route.} The correspondence with nodes $1.8.8$--$1.8.11$ is at the level of the
conclusion. The game here is not the paper's $\game^{\sep} = \verifier^{\sep}_{C_0}$: it is the
halting-reduction game of a machine that runs the commuting-value semidecider on its own
game. It is explicit in the same sense as the paper's. It is obtained from self-reference,
uniformly from the codes of $g$ and of the semidecider, and neither proof exhibits a
value-$1$ commuting-operator strategy. The paper's proof needs, in the case
$\omegaco = 1$, that $\valstar \leq \frac12$ at a level of its recursion where no soundness is
otherwise known, and gets it from divergence of a Schmidt-rank entanglement requirement
(Remark~\ref{rem:separation-premises}). Here that case is the non-halting case of the
halting reduction, whose soundness is already the value-form compression of the main theorem.
The paper observes, in a footnote to its halting theorem, that the gap
$\valstar < \omegaco$ holds for infinitely many non-halting machines~\cite{AuditSync26}; the
fixed point picks one of them out.
\texttt{scripts/ledger-sync.py}, which walks the ledger's declared dependencies, still lists
the Schmidt-rank clauses of stage $1.5$ among the imports reached from this theorem, as
expected.

\begin{remark}[The paper's construction and its premises]
\label{rem:separation-premises}
\uses{rem:entanglement-form,lem:valco-upper-re,rem:pcc-representation-bridge,
  rem:runtime-contract-bridge}
The paper's proof builds $\game^{\sep}$ as the compressed verifier at a fixed level
$C_0 \geq 2$ of a self-referential family: its decider runs the upper semidecider on its own
succinct description, accepts early if the semidecider halts, and otherwise
compresses~\cite{AuditSync26}. Formalizing \emph{that} game, rather than the one of
Theorem~\ref{thm:separation}, needs the \emph{bipartite Schmidt-rank entanglement variant} of
compression, together with its effective bounded self-reference construction. Write
$\Ent_{\mathrm{bi}}(\game,\nu)$ for the least Schmidt rank of a finite-dimensional
tensor-product strategy attaining value at least $\nu$, or $\infty$ if there is none. This is
the paper's measure; it is distinct from the synchronous dimension requirement $\Ent$ of
Definition~\ref{def:ent}. The extra compression clauses must force $\Ent_{\mathrm{bi}}$ to
diverge along the recursive levels, and the construction must identify the game at $C_0$ with
the game supplied to the upper semidecider, with all specialization and timeout bounds
justified.

These are premises of the paper's construction, not of Theorem~\ref{thm:separation}, and none
of them is formalized. The value-form Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} and its current Lean
interface do not supply the Schmidt-rank clauses. If the source completeness steps are
implemented through the synchronous PCC interface, the representation transfers of
Remark~\ref{rem:pcc-representation-bridge} must also be supplied. An implementation through
the ambient verifier interfaces additionally requires the runtime and answer-cut normalization
of Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-contract-bridge}; the source construction's absolute bounds cannot
simply be read as relative cost coefficients. \texttt{planning/explicit-separation.md} records
the cost.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Which commuting value this is, and why it is not the tracial one]
\label{rem:separation-which-value}
\uses{thm:separation,lem:valco-upper-re,lem:tracial-le-co,def:commuting-value,def:co-value-bipartite}
Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} is stated for the \emph{bipartite} commuting-operator value
$\omegaco$ (Definition~\ref{def:co-value-bipartite}), not for the tracial $\valco$
(Definition~\ref{def:commuting-value}) that this blueprint otherwise carries. That is not a
slip, and the distinction is forced by the proof rather than cosmetic.

The proof turns on the semidecider of Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}, which is complete for
the event $\omegaco < 1$. For the tracial value, Lemma~\ref{lem:tracial-le-co} gives only
$\valco \leq \omegaco$, so the same procedure is a sound semidecider for $\valco < 1$ and not
a complete one, and the fixed point would conclude nothing. The semidecision event is that
the commuting value of the very game being defined is below $1$. No search for a proof of
non-halting, and no preservation theorem for arbitrary commuting-operator strategies, is
used, and the argument constructs no explicit perfect commuting-operator strategy: the
correlation of payoff $1$ is obtained by compactness.

No synchronous decision predicate is asserted: $\game^{\sep}$ is whatever game the halting
reduction produces. Even for synchronous games, Lemma~\ref{lem:tracial-le-co} only gives
$\valco\leq\omegaco$, with the reverse comparison false in general. Thus this route provides
neither a synchronous perfect correlation nor a tracial value-$1$ witness. Any such
formulation would require additional representation and value-transport results.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Refutation of Connes' Embedding Problem}

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[Kirchberg's forward implication]
\label{lem:kirchberg-forward}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.1}
If CEP has an affirmative answer then the minimal and maximal C$^*$-tensor norms coincide on
$C^*(F_\infty) \ot_{\mathrm{alg}} C^*(F_\infty)$, where $F_\infty$ is the free group on
countably many generators and $C^*$ denotes the full unital group algebra.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Source.} Kirchberg; see~\cite{Oza13}. This is the second of the two imports this
chapter carries: the companion ledger records it as \emph{admitted} (node $1.8.6.1$), and it is
the only step of the chain below that is not proved there.

\begin{lemma}[Finitely many generators]
\label{lem:cep-free-group}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.2}
\uses{lem:kirchberg-forward}
Conditional on CEP, $\min = \max$ on
$C^*(F_k) \ot_{\mathrm{alg}} C^*(F_k)$ for every finite $k \geq 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[A completely positive section onto the Bell algebra]
\label{lem:bell-algebra-section}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.3}
For $A = \ast_{x=1}^{k} \C^m$ and $B = C^*(F_k)$ with $k \geq 1$ and $m \geq 2$, the
canonical quotient $q : B \to A$ has a unital completely positive section $\Psi$, obtained as
the $(0,0)$ corner of an explicitly constructed unital $*$-homomorphism
$\gamma : A \to M_m(B)$. In particular $q \Psi = \mathrm{id}$ on all of $A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Transport to the Bell algebra]
\label{lem:cep-bell-algebra}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.4}
\uses{lem:cep-free-group,lem:bell-algebra-section}
Conditional on CEP, the minimal and maximal tensor norms coincide on
$A \ot_{\mathrm{alg}} A$ for every finite Bell algebra $A = \ast_{x=1}^{k} \C^m$,
$k \geq 1$, $m \geq 2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Commuting correlations restrict states on the maximal tensor product]
\label{lem:commuting-restricts-state}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.5}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,lem:cep-bell-algebra}
Every commuting-operator correlation in a finite bipartite scenario with common parameters
$(k,m)$ is the restriction to the coordinate projections of a state on
$A \ot_{\max} A$. Under CEP that same functional is a state on $A \ot_{\min} A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Vector states are dense on finitely many coordinates]
\label{lem:spatial-coordinates}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.6}
On any finite collection of self-adjoint coordinate observables in a faithfully represented
spatial C$^*$-tensor product, every state is approximable by finite convex combinations of
unit-vector states in that same spatial representation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[States on the spatial product give $C_{qa}$ correlations]
\label{lem:spatial-values-cqa}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.7}
\uses{lem:spatial-coordinates}
The coordinate values of any state on a spatial tensor product of finite Bell algebras belong
to $C_{qa}$, the closure of the finite-dimensional tensor-product POVM correlations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[CEP forces equality of the correlation sets]
\label{lem:cep-implies-equal}
\ledgernode{1.8.6, 1.8.6.8}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,lem:commuting-restricts-state,lem:spatial-values-cqa}
If every separable II$_1$ factor embeds into an ultrapower of the hyperfinite II$_1$ factor,
then $C_{qa} = C_{qc}$ in every finite bipartite scenario, including unequal alphabet sizes.
Consequently exhibiting one finite-scenario separation of $C_{qa}$ from $C_{qc}$ refutes CEP.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Connes' Embedding Problem, negative answer]
\label{cor:cep}
\ledgernode{1.8.7}
\uses{cor:tsirelson,lem:cep-implies-equal}
There is a separable II$_1$ factor that does not embed into an ultrapower
$\cal{R}^{\omega}$ of the hyperfinite II$_1$ factor.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Source for the implication.} The correlation-set connection is developed by
Fritz~\cite{Fri12}, Junge et al.~\cite{JNPPSW11} and Ozawa~\cite{Oza13}. The route here
uses only the forward implication in Lemma~\ref{lem:cep-implies-equal}: under CEP the
two bipartite correlation sets coincide, contradicting Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson}.
It does not require a converse equivalence, a synchronous perfect correlation for the
separating game, or a tracial value-$1$ strategy for that game.

\paragraph{Comments.} The formalization plan is the forward chain displayed above:
formulate CEP for separable II$_1$ factors and the ultrapower $\cal{R}^\omega$, then
combine the admitted Kirchberg implication with the finite-generator, Bell-algebra and
state-approximation lemmas to obtain $C_{qc}\subseteq C_{qa}$. The non-embeddable factor
follows by contraposition. A synchronous-correlation construction or a witness generated
by finitely many projections would require additional results and is not a conclusion of
this dependency chain.

\subsection{Failure of Kirchberg's QWEP conjecture}

\begin{corollary}[QWEP]
\label{cor:qwep}
\uses{cor:cep}
Kirchberg's QWEP conjecture fails: there is a C$^*$-algebra that is not a quotient of
a C$^*$-algebra with the weak expectation property; equivalently,
$C^*(F_2) \ot_{\min} C^*(F_2) \neq C^*(F_2) \ot_{\max} C^*(F_2)$.
\end{corollary}

Equivalent to CEP by Kirchberg's theorem (see~\cite{Oza13}); no new input from the
project, but the equivalence itself is another substantial operator-algebra
formalization.

\subsection{Further directions}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:further}
Other candidate consequences to record as the project matures: undecidability of the
existence of \emph{perfect} strategies for synchronous games and its group-theoretic
reformulations (via the synchronous algebra of a game); quantitative non-closure of
$C_q$ and rates for the NPA hierarchy; uncomputability results for entanglement
requirements ($\Ent(\cdot, \frac12)$ grows faster than any computable function along
the halting games), which need the entanglement form of the pipeline
(Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}) rather than the value form adopted here; the commuting-operator
counterpart $\MIPco = \coRE$~\cite{Lin23,Lin25}, for which direct parallel repetition
for commuting-operator strategies (Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-co}) and Lin's
tracial density theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:tracial-density}) are already formalized; and,
more speculatively, connections
to the undecidability of the spectral gap and to the existence of non-hyperlinear
groups, the latter being \emph{not} implied by CEP's failure and best recorded as an
open problem.
\end{remark}
