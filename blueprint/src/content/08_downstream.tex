\section{Downstream results}
\label{ch:downstream}

Potential extensions of the project once the main theorem is in place. They are listed
roughly in order of increasing formalization cost. The first two stay within
finite-dimensional linear algebra plus elementary functional analysis, while the
operator-algebraic consequences require von Neumann algebra infrastructure that
largely does not yet exist in Mathlib and would constitute a major sub-project of
independent value.

\subsection{Uncomputability of the value}

Corollary~\ref{cor:value-uncomputable} is already a headline consequence under the main
theorem's compression premise: no algorithm can approximate the (synchronous or quantum) value of a
nonlocal game to within an additive constant $c < \frac14$, since an estimate of additive
error $c$ separates value $1$ from value $\frac12$ exactly when $c < \frac14$ (at
$c = \frac14$ the estimate $\frac34$ is consistent with both). The threshold rises to the
trivial $\frac12$ once the gap is amplified: repeating the game drives the soundness value
below any $\delta > 0$ while preserving value $1$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}), at a cost polynomial in the description, so for
every $c < \frac12$ no algorithm approximates the value to within additive error $c$. That
stronger form depends on the repetition theorem as well as on the main theorem.

\subsection{Semidecision from both sides}
\label{sec:ds-semidecision}

The non-explicit route mentioned below, and the explicit separating game, both rest on the
same pair of procedures: the entangled value is semidecidable from below, the commuting value
from above. The first is already available and formalized ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}, which is ledger node $1.8.1$ --- so only the second is
stated here, with the two ingredients it rests on.

\begin{theorem}[NPA convergence and compactness]
\label{thm:npa-convergence}
\ledgernode{1.8.2, 1.8.2.6}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,lem:npa-dilation,lem:npa-levels,lem:npa-moment-bound,lem:npa-diagonal,lem:npa-gns}
Fix a finite bipartite scenario. The normalized full-word level-$k$ NPA feasible sets have
uniformly bounded moments, their correlation projections $K_k$ are compact and decreasing, and
$\bigcap_k K_k = C_{qc}$, with the POVM and projective definitions of $C_{qc}$ equivalent. In
particular $C_{qc}$ is compact, every finite game payoff attains its maximum over it, and
$C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$. No finite-dimensional attainment and no convergence rate is
asserted. The five lemmas below are its ingredients.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Comments.} Not formalized. The Lean proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson} needs
only two consequences of this theorem, compactness of $C_{qc}$ and upper semidecision of
$\omegaco$, and obtains both without the hierarchy: Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}, through the quadratic module of
Definition~\ref{def:measurement-algebra}. The level presentation, the moment bound, the
diagonal limit and the projective dilation stay open.

\begin{lemma}[Common projective dilation of commuting POVMs]
\label{lem:npa-dilation}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.1}
\uses{def:co-strategy-bipartite}
Commuting finite-outcome POVMs admit a correlation-preserving common \emph{projective}
dilation. For $Vh = (\sqrt{A_a} h)_a$ the operator
$U = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & V^* \\ V & \Id - VV^* \end{pmatrix}$ is a self-adjoint
unitary on $\mH \oplus \mH^r$ whose conjugated coordinate projections compress to $A_a$, and
all questions share one embedding. Across the two players the block entries stay in commuting
algebras, so iterating the dilation preserves both commutation and the joint distribution. Zero
effects and arbitrary Hilbert space dimension are allowed.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Comments.} Two steps here are one line on paper and real work in Lean. That $U$ is a
self-adjoint unitary uses $V^*V = \Id$, which is exactly $\sum_a A_a = \Id$, and then that
$VV^*$ is a projection; $U^2 = \Id$ follows. And the commutation claim needs more than
$[A_a, B] = 0$: the dilation is built from $\sqrt{A_a}$, so what has to commute with $B$ is
$\sqrt{A_a}$, which follows from $[A_a, B] = 0$ by continuous functional calculus and not
formally. In the commuting-operator model --- where both players act on \emph{one} space and
merely commute, rather than on tensor factors --- that functional-calculus step is what keeps
the dilated PVMs commuting, so it cannot be skipped.

\begin{lemma}[The full-word levels, and that they are effectively enumerable]
\label{lem:npa-levels}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.2}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite}
For a finite Bell scenario, level $k$ of the full-word NPA hierarchy consists of the normalized
positive semidefinite moment matrices on words of length at most $k$, subject to the
contextual projection, completeness and cross-commutation relations of degree at most $2k$,
with two-letter moments equal to the correlation coordinates. These constraints are finite and
effectively enumerable, every $C_{qc}$ correlation is feasible at every level, the levels are
nested, and this redundant presentation agrees with the standard reduced one by
degree-preserving linear substitutions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Moment entries are bounded, so the levels are compact]
\label{lem:npa-moment-bound}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.3}
\uses{lem:npa-levels}
Every feasible level-$k$ moment satisfies $\abs{L_k(w)} \leq 1$ for $\abs{w} \leq 2k$: the
identity $\sum_a \Gamma[e_a v, e_a v] = \Gamma[v,v]$ with positivity bounds each
leading-generator diagonal by its suffix diagonal, and induction together with the positive
semidefinite $2 \times 2$ minors bounds every entry. Hence each finite-level feasible set, and
its correlation projection, is compact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[A diagonal subsequence, from certificates that need not be compatible]
\label{lem:npa-diagonal}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.4}
\uses{lem:npa-moment-bound}
If a correlation is feasible at every level, a countable diagonal subsequence of the bounded
certificates --- which need \emph{not} be compatible across levels, and this is the step where
a paraphrase overclaims --- defines a normalized positive functional on the polynomial
$*$-algebra modulo the measurement relations. Every polynomial positivity constraint and every
finite contextual relation survives the limit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[GNS: a positive functional gives a commuting projective representation]
\label{lem:npa-gns}
\ledgernode{1.8.2.5}
\uses{lem:npa-diagonal}
A normalized positive functional on the measurement $*$-algebra yields a separable commuting
projective representation. Quotient by the zero-norm subspace and complete; left multiplication
by a projection generator $g$ is a contraction because
$\norm{u}^2 - \norm{gu}^2 = \norm{(\Id - g)u}^2$, so the null subspace is invariant, and the
PVM and cross-commutation identities extend from the dense polynomial quotient. The
distinguished unit vector is the class of $1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Decidability of the first-order theory of real closed fields]
\label{lem:rcf-decision}
\ledgernode{1.8.3}
A first-order sentence over real closed fields with rational coefficients is decidable; in
particular so is feasibility of a finite conjunction of polynomial equalities and weak or
strict inequalities over $\R$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Source.} Tarski; the effective form used here is Basu, Pollack and
Roy~\cite{BPR06}. This is an import: the companion ledger records it as \emph{admitted}, one
of only three assumptions in the campaign (Remark~\ref{rem:admitted-nodes}), and one of the
two imports carried by this chapter rather than by the main theorem. The other is
Lemma~\ref{lem:kirchberg-forward}.

\subsection{Upper semidecision through a Positivstellensatz}
\label{sec:ds-positivstellensatz}

The Lean proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} does not go through the NPA levels. It works
with one object: the quadratic module of the measurement relations in the free $*$-algebra,
with POVM generators. Its Archimedean property and a Riesz extension give a noncommutative
Positivstellensatz (Theorem~\ref{thm:nc-positivstellensatz}; compare~\cite{HM04}), which turns
$\omegaco(\game) < r$ into the existence of an exact sum-of-squares certificate with
Gaussian-integer coefficients (Lemma~\ref{lem:sos-certificates}). The same states and the same
GNS construction give compactness of $C_{qc}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact}). No real-closed-field
decision, no dilation to projections, and no semidefinite hierarchy is involved.

\begin{definition}[The measurement algebra and its cone]
\label{def:measurement-algebra}
\uses{def:game}
\lean{MIPRE.NCPoly, MIPRE.NCPoly.eval, MIPRE.NCPoly.relSpan, MIPRE.NCPoly.qmod,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.Gen, MIPRE.Tsirelson.rels, MIPRE.Tsirelson.cone, MIPRE.Tsirelson.gamePoly,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.IsConeState}
\leanok
Let $P$ be the complex polynomials in noncommuting variables $e_{xa}$ ($x \in \mX$,
$a \in \mA$) and $f_{yb}$ ($y \in \mY$, $b \in \mB$), with the involution that conjugates
coefficients, reverses words and fixes the variables. The \emph{relations} are
$\sum_a e_{xa} - 1$, $\sum_b f_{yb} - 1$ and $e_{xa} f_{yb} - f_{yb} e_{xa}$; $I$ is the
complex span of $u \rho v$ over relations $\rho$ and $u, v \in P$. The \emph{cone} $M$ is the
set of finite nonnegative real combinations of the elements $s^* s$, $s^* e_{xa} s$,
$s^* f_{yb} s$ ($s \in P$), of the elements of $I$, and of the anti-hermitian elements of
$P$. The game polynomial of $\game$ is $W_\game = \sum_{x,y,a,b} \mu(x,y) D(x,y,a,b)\,
e_{xa} f_{yb}$. A \emph{cone state} is a complex-linear $L : P \to \C$ with $L(1) = 1$ and
$\mathrm{Re}\, L(m) \geq 0$ for every $m \in M$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Comments.} The Lean $P$ is \texttt{MonoidAlgebra} over the free monoid, under a
type synonym: Mathlib's star on \texttt{FreeAlgebra} is complex-linear, and the synonym keeps
the conjugate-linear star from becoming a global instance on \texttt{MonoidAlgebra}.

\begin{lemma}[The cone is Archimedean and positive on strategies]
\label{lem:cone-archimedean}
\uses{def:measurement-algebra,def:co-value-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.NCPoly.star_mul_mul_mem_qmod_of_mem, MIPRE.NCPoly.archimedean_of_gen,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.cone_archimedean, MIPRE.NCPoly.re_inner_eval_nonneg,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.strategy_nonneg, MIPRE.Tsirelson.value_le_of_mem}
\leanok
$a^* M a \subseteq M$ for every $a \in P$, and for every $y \in P$ some real $N$ has
$N \cdot 1 + y \in M$. Evaluating the variables at the effects of a commuting-operator
strategy gives $\mathrm{Re} \langle \psi, m(E, F) \psi \rangle \geq 0$ for every $m \in M$;
in particular $r \cdot 1 - W_\game \in M$ bounds the value of every strategy by $r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$1 - e_{xa} = \sum_{a' \neq a} e_{xa'} - (\sum_{a'} e_{xa'} - 1) \in M$, and
$e - e^2 = e(1 - e)e + (1 - e)e(1 - e) \in M$, so $1 - g^2 \in M$ for every variable $g$ and,
telescoping, $1 - w^* w \in M$ for every word $w$. Then
$(1 + \abs{\alpha}^2) \cdot 1 + \alpha w + (\alpha w)^* = (1 + \alpha w)^*(1 + \alpha w) +
\abs{\alpha}^2 (1 - w^* w)$, and the anti-hermitian part of a monomial lies in $M$. On a
strategy the relations evaluate to $0$, $\langle \psi, s^* g s \psi \rangle =
\langle s\psi, g\, s\psi \rangle \geq 0$ for positive $g$, and anti-hermitian operators have
imaginary expectations.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cone states are the states of commuting-operator strategies]
\label{lem:cone-state-gns}
\uses{lem:cone-archimedean,def:correlation-sets}
\lean{MIPRE.Tsirelson.isConeState_stateOf, MIPRE.GNS.GenData.strategy,
  MIPRE.GNS.GenData.strategy_correlation, MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateStrategy,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateStrategy_correlation, MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateStrategy_value,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.mem_Cqc_iff}
\leanok
For finite alphabets, the functional $z \mapsto \langle \psi, z(E, F) \psi \rangle$ of a
commuting-operator strategy is a cone state; conversely every cone state $L$ is that of a
commuting-operator strategy whose correlation is $\mathrm{Re}\, L(e_{xa} f_{yb})$. So $C_{qc}$
is the set of the correlations $\mathrm{Re}\, L(e_{xa} f_{yb})$ over cone states $L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A cone state is hermitian (since $z - z^*$ and $i(z + z^*)$ are anti-hermitian), vanishes on
$I$ (since $\pm j, \pm i j \in I \subseteq M$) and is positive on $s^* s$ and $s^* g s$. The
GNS construction completes $P$ for the seminorm $\mathrm{Re}\, L(a^* a)^{1/2}$; left
multiplication by a variable $g$ is a contraction because $a^*(1 - g^2) a \in M$, and extends
to a positive operator on the completion. The normalizations and the cross-commutation hold
because the corresponding vectors are null, and the class of $1$ is a unit vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$C_{qc}$ is compact]
\label{lem:cqc-compact}
\uses{lem:cone-state-gns,lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\lean{MIPRE.Tsirelson.stateSpace, MIPRE.Tsirelson.isCompact_stateSpace,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.image_correlationOf_stateSpace, MIPRE.isCompact_Cqc, MIPRE.isClosed_Cqc,
  MIPRE.Cqa_subset_Cqc}
\leanok
For finite alphabets $C_{qc}$ is compact, hence closed, and $C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A cone state is determined by its values on words. The set of those value families that are
cone states is closed in the product topology, since each condition involves finitely many
words, and lies in the product of closed unit disks, since $2 \cdot 1 + \alpha w +
(\alpha w)^* \in M$ for $\abs{\alpha} = 1$ gives $\abs{L(w)} \leq 1$. By Tychonoff it is
compact, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-state-gns} its continuous image under
$L \mapsto \mathrm{Re}\, L(e_{xa} f_{yb})$ is $C_{qc}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Positivstellensatz for the commuting-operator value]
\label{thm:nc-positivstellensatz}
\uses{lem:cone-state-gns}
\lean{MIPRE.exists_separating_functional_of_archimedean,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.exists_isConeState_of_not_mem, MIPRE.Tsirelson.sub_gamePoly_mem_cone_of_lt,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.commutingOperatorValue_lt_iff}
\leanok
If $\omegaco(\game) < r$ then $r \cdot 1 - W_\game \in M$. With nonempty answer sets,
$\omegaco(\game) < r$ holds exactly when $r' \cdot 1 - W_\game \in M$ for some $r' < r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\leanok
If $h = r \cdot 1 - W_\game \notin M$, the Riesz extension theorem applied to the cone
$M + \R_{\geq 0}(-h)$, with the functional $t \cdot 1 \mapsto t$ on the span of $1$, gives a
real functional $L_0 \geq 0$ on $M$ with $L_0(1) = 1$ and $L_0(h) \leq 0$; the Archimedean
property supplies both hypotheses. Its complexification $L_0(z) - i L_0(iz)$ is a cone state,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-state-gns} turns it into a strategy of value $L_0(W_\game) \geq r >
\omegaco(\game)$, a contradiction. The converse is Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-archimedean}, with a
strategy supplied by the deterministic one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact sum-of-squares certificates]
\label{lem:sos-certificates}
\uses{thm:nc-positivstellensatz}
\lean{MIPRE.Tsirelson.Dominant, MIPRE.Tsirelson.re_inner_eval_ge,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.commutingOperatorValue_lt_of_certificate,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.exists_certificate_of_lt, MIPRE.Tsirelson.exists_certificate_iff}
\leanok
Call $z \in P$ \emph{dominant} if the real part of its constant coefficient exceeds the sum of
$\abs{\mathrm{Re}\, z_w} + \abs{\mathrm{Im}\, z_w}$ over the nonempty words $w$. Let
$\kappa > 0$ and suppose the answer sets are nonempty. Then $\omegaco(\game) < r$ if and only
if there are $N \geq 1$, finitely many pairs $(g_i, s_i)$ with $g_i \in \{1, e_{xa},
f_{yb}\}$ and $s_i \in P$, and finitely many $(c_t, w_t, \rho_t, w'_t)$ with $c_t \in \C$,
words $w_t, w'_t$ and relations $\rho_t$, all coefficients Gaussian integers, such that
$T + T^*$ is dominant, where $T = N^2 \kappa (r \cdot 1 - W_\game) - \sum_i s_i^* g_i s_i -
\sum_t c_t w_t \rho_t w'_t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Soundness: every word acts as a contraction, so on every strategy
$2 N^2 \kappa (r - \text{value}) \geq \mathrm{Re} \langle \psi, (T + T^*)\psi \rangle$ is at
least the dominance margin of $T + T^*$, uniformly over strategies. Completeness: take
$\omegaco(\game) < r' < r$; Theorem~\ref{thm:nc-positivstellensatz} writes
$\kappa(r \cdot 1 - W_\game) = \varepsilon \cdot 1 + \sum_i \sigma_i^* g_i \sigma_i +
\sum_t c_t w_t \rho_t w'_t + a$ with $\varepsilon = \kappa(r - r') > 0$ and $a$
anti-hermitian, which symmetrization removes. Rounding the coefficients of the $\sigma_i$ at
scale $N$ and the $c_t$ at scale $N^2$ changes $T + T^*$ by $O(N)$ in the $\ell^1$ norm of
coefficients (submultiplicative and star-invariant), against a constant term
$2N^2\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Upper semidecision of the commuting-operator value]
\label{lem:valco-upper-re}
\ledgernode{1.8.4, 1.8.4.1}
\uses{def:game-description,def:co-value-bipartite,lem:sos-certificates,def:commuting-upper-re}
\lean{MIPRE.commutingUpperRE, MIPRE.Tsirelson.Coded.CheckUpper,
  MIPRE.Tsirelson.Coded.primrecRel_checkUpper, MIPRE.Tsirelson.checkUpper_iff}
\leanok
Write $\game_g$ for the game described by a game description $g$
(Definition~\ref{def:game-description}). The set
\[
  \{ (g, p, q) : g \text{ a game description},\ p, q \in \N,\ \omegaco(\game_g) < p/q \}
\]
is recursively enumerable, with $p/0 = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:value-lower-approx}
\leanok
The certificates of Lemma~\ref{lem:sos-certificates} at $\kappa = q\, W_g$, where $W_g$ is
the total weight of $g$, have integer data: $\kappa(p/q \cdot 1 - W_{\game_g}) = p W_g \cdot 1 -
q \sum \mathrm{wt}(x,y)[D]\, e_{xa} f_{yb}$. The checker computes $T + T^*$ on lists of
monomials with coded Gaussian-integer coefficients, merges equal words, tests dominance, and
rejects $q = 0$ and malformed letters or relation indices; it is primitive recursive, and a
certificate passes exactly when $\omegaco(\game_g) < p/q$. Enumerating certificates gives the
semidecider.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof route.} The correspondence with nodes $1.8.4$ and $1.8.4.1$ is at the level
of the conclusion. The paper runs the semidefinite hierarchy for upper bounds; the ledger makes
that exact through the admitted real-closed-field decision of node $1.8.3$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:rcf-decision}). The proof here uses neither, and so does not rest on $1.8.3$;
\texttt{scripts/ledger-sync.py}, which walks the ledger's declared dependencies, still lists
$1.8.3$ among the imports reached from this lemma, as expected. The ledger statement is also
broader: it covers games with rational data and rational $r$, and the dovetailed form that
halts exactly when $\omegaco(\game) < 1$, on succinct descriptions. The paper's construction
of Remark~\ref{rem:separation-premises} consumes those forms; Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} need only the form above.

The value here is the \emph{bipartite} commuting-operator value of
Definition~\ref{def:co-value-bipartite}, not the tracial $\valco$ of
Definition~\ref{def:commuting-value}: the polynomial algebra has both players' variables and
its states give the bipartite set $C_{qc}$. The two must not be interchanged ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:tracial-le-co} gives $\valco \leq \omegaco$ and the reverse comparison is false
in general --- so this lemma yields a \emph{sound} semidecider for $\valco(\game) < r$ and not
a complete one. That is all the arguments below need, since they consume the correlation sets
rather than the tracial value.

\subsection{Correlation sets and the conditional separation}

\begin{definition}[Correlation sets]
\label{def:correlation-sets}
\uses{def:tensor-strategy,def:co-strategy-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.Game.payoff, MIPRE.TensorProductStrategy.correlation, MIPRE.Cq, MIPRE.Cqa,
  MIPRE.Cqc}
\leanok
Fix finite sets $\mX, \mY, \mA, \mB$. A \emph{correlation} is a function
$p : \mX \times \mY \times \mA \times \mB \to \R$, written $p(a,b|x,y)$, and its
\emph{payoff} in a game $\game$ on these alphabets is
$\sum_{x,y,a,b} \mu(x,y)\, D(x,y,a,b)\, p(a,b|x,y)$. The set $C_q$ consists of the
correlations $\bra{\psi} A^x_a \ot B^y_b \ket{\psi}$ of finite-dimensional
tensor-product strategies (Definition~\ref{def:tensor-strategy}); $C_{qa}$ is its closure
in $\R^{\mX \times \mY \times \mA \times \mB}$; and $C_{qc}$ consists of the correlations
$\bra{\psi} E^x_a F^y_b \ket{\psi}$ of commuting-operator strategies
(Definition~\ref{def:co-strategy-bipartite}). The value of a strategy of either kind is
the payoff of its correlation.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Comments.} The Lean $C_q$ uses the projective measurements of
Definition~\ref{def:tensor-strategy}, and $C_{qc}$ the POVMs of
Definition~\ref{def:co-strategy-bipartite} on a Hilbert space of any dimension. The
companion paper defines the finite-dimensional set both ways and $C_{qc}$ on separable
spaces~\cite{AuditSync26}. The closures of the projective and POVM finite-dimensional sets
agree by Naimark dilation, and a commuting-operator correlation is realized on the
separable subspace generated cyclically by $\psi$; neither identification is used below.

\begin{lemma}[Basic properties of the correlation sets]
\label{lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\uses{def:correlation-sets,def:co-value-bipartite,def:q-value}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_nonneg,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_sum,
  MIPRE.commutingOperatorValue_nonneg, MIPRE.commutingOperatorValue_le_one,
  MIPRE.TensorProductStrategy.correlation_toCommuting, MIPRE.Cq_subset_Cqc,
  MIPRE.quantumValue_le_commutingOperatorValue, MIPRE.payoff_le_quantumValue_of_mem_Cqa,
  MIPRE.payoff_le_commutingOperatorValue_of_mem_Cqc, MIPRE.Cqa_subset_Cqc_of_isClosed}
\leanok
Commuting-operator correlations are nonnegative and sum to one for each question pair, so
$0 \leq \omegaco(\game) \leq 1$. A tensor-product strategy is a commuting-operator strategy
with the same correlation, so $C_q \subseteq C_{qc}$ and
$\valstar(\game) \leq \omegaco(\game)$. The payoff of every correlation in $C_{qa}$ is at
most $\valstar(\game)$, and that of every correlation in $C_{qc}$ at most
$\omegaco(\game)$. If $C_{qc}$ is closed then $C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
$E^x_a F^y_b$ is the product of two commuting positive operators, hence positive, which
gives nonnegativity; the sum over $a, b$ is $\langle \psi, \psi \rangle = 1$. On
$\C^{d_A} \ot \C^{d_B}$ the operators $A^x_a \ot \Id$ and $\Id \ot B^y_b$ are commuting
projections with the same Born probabilities. The payoff is linear, hence continuous, so
the bound $\valstar(\game)$ on $C_q$ passes to its closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Upper semidecision of the commuting-operator value]
\label{def:commuting-upper-re}
\uses{def:game-description,def:co-value-bipartite,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingUpperRE}
\leanok
The proposition that the set of triples $(d, p, q)$ of a game description $d$ and
$p, q \in \N$ with $\omegaco(\game_d) < p/q$ is recursively enumerable, where $p/0 = 0$: the
threshold encoding of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}. Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}
proves it; Lemma~\ref{lem:tsirelson-conditional} takes it as a hypothesis.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional separation]
\label{lem:tsirelson-conditional}
\uses{lem:correlation-sets-basic,def:game-description,def:commuting-upper-re}
\lean{MIPRE.HaltingReductionQuantum,
  MIPRE.exists_quantumValue_lt_commutingOperatorValue, MIPRE.exists_mem_Cqc_not_mem_Cqa,
  MIPRE.exists_Cqa_ne_Cqc, MIPRE.tsirelson_of_upperRE_of_isClosed}
\leanok
Suppose that there is a computable map $g$ from machines to game descriptions with
$\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) = 1$ when $\machine$ halts on the empty input and
$\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) \leq \frac12$ otherwise (the conclusion of
Corollary~\ref{cor:main-quantum}), and that the relation $\omegaco(\game_d) < p/q$ on game
descriptions $d$ and $p, q \in \N$ is recursively enumerable, with $p/0 = 0$ (the form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} used here). Then some described game has
$\valstar(\game_d) < \omegaco(\game_d)$, and in its scenario some correlation lies in
$C_{qc}$ but not in $C_{qa}$. If moreover $C_{qc}$ is closed in every finite scenario, then
$C_{qa} \subsetneq C_{qc}$ in that scenario, whose two players share the question alphabet
and the answer alphabet.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
If $\omegaco(\game_d) \leq \valstar(\game_d)$ for every $d$, the two values agree on every
described game, since $\valstar \leq \omegaco$. Then $\machine$ fails to halt exactly when
$\omegaco(\game_{g(\machine)}) < \frac34$, so non-halting is the pullback of an r.e. relation
along a computable map, hence r.e., which it is not
(Theorem~\ref{thm:halting-undecidable}). So some $d$ has $\valstar(\game_d) <
\omegaco(\game_d)$, and some commuting-operator strategy has value above $\valstar(\game_d)$;
its correlation is in $C_{qc}$, and its payoff exceeds the bound $\valstar(\game_d)$ that
holds on $C_{qa}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}). With $C_{qc}$ closed,
$C_{qa} \subseteq C_{qc}$, and the inclusion is strict.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Failure of Tsirelson's problem}

\begin{corollary}[Tsirelson's problem, negative answer]
\label{cor:tsirelson}
\ledgernode{1.8.5}
\uses{def:correlation-sets,lem:tsirelson-conditional,cor:main-quantum,lem:valco-upper-re,
  lem:cqc-compact}
\lean{MIPRE.tsirelson, MIPRE.tsirelson_of_haltingReduction, MIPRE.tsirelson_of_upperRE}
\leanok
There exist finite question and answer sets for which the closure $C_{qa}$ of the set
of finite-dimensional quantum correlations is strictly contained in the set $C_{qc}$
of commuting-operator correlations.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Lemma~\ref{lem:tsirelson-conditional}, with the halting reduction of
Corollary~\ref{cor:main-quantum}, the upper semidecider of Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re},
and the closedness of $C_{qc}$ in every finite scenario (Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact}).
\end{proof}

The Lean proof uses no lower semidecider, no real-closed-field decision and no NPA levels. The witness scenario has one
question alphabet and one answer alphabet shared by the two players. This is the
non-explicit route; it does not use Theorem~\ref{thm:separation}, which follows from the same
two inputs and the recursion theorem. The ledger's proof of
node $1.8.5$ dovetails the lower semidecider of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx} against
the upper one into a promise decider. The argument here needs only the upper one, because
$\valstar \leq \omegaco$ always holds. Non-closure of the unclosed set $C_q$ itself is
Slofstra's earlier theorem~\cite{Slo19}, which is not formalized here.

\subsection{Separation of quantum and commuting values}

\begin{theorem}[Separation]
\label{thm:separation}
\ledgernode{1.8.8, 1.8.9, 1.8.10, 1.8.11}
\uses{def:correlation-sets,lem:correlation-sets-basic,def:commuting-upper-re,
  lem:tsirelson-conditional,lem:valco-upper-re,cor:main-quantum,lem:cqc-compact}
\lean{MIPRE.separation, MIPRE.separation_of_upperRE,
  MIPRE.exists_quantumValue_le_half_commutingOperatorValue_eq_one,
  MIPRE.exists_mem_Cqc_payoff_eq_commutingOperatorValue}
\leanok
There is an explicit finite bipartite game $\game^{\sep}$, given by a game description, with
$\valstar(\game^{\sep}) \leq \frac12$ and $\omegaco(\game^{\sep}) = 1$. Moreover, some
correlation $p \in C_{qc}$ in the scenario of $\game^{\sep}$ has payoff $1$ in $\game^{\sep}$
and lies outside $C_{qa}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Let $g$ be the computable map of Corollary~\ref{cor:main-quantum}, from machines to game
descriptions, with $\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) = 1$ when $\machine$ halts on the empty
input and $\valstar(\game_{g(\machine)}) \leq \frac12$ otherwise. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} at $p = q = 1$, the set of machines $\machine$ with
$\omegaco(\game_{g(\machine)}) < 1$ is recursively enumerable. Kleene's recursion theorem
therefore gives a machine $\machine_{\sep}$ that halts on the empty input exactly when
$\omegaco(\game_{g(\machine_{\sep})}) < 1$. Put $\game^{\sep} = \game_{g(\machine_{\sep})}$.

If $\machine_{\sep}$ halted, then $\valstar(\game^{\sep}) = 1$, so
$\omegaco(\game^{\sep}) = 1$ by $\valstar \leq \omegaco$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}), and $\machine_{\sep}$ would not halt. So
$\machine_{\sep}$ does not halt. Hence $\valstar(\game^{\sep}) \leq \frac12$, and
$\omegaco(\game^{\sep})$ is not below $1$, so it equals $1$.

For the correlation: the payoff is continuous, and $C_{qc}$ is compact
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cqc-compact}) and nonempty, so the payoff attains its maximum on $C_{qc}$ at
some $p$. That maximum is $\omegaco(\game^{\sep}) = 1$, since every commuting-operator
strategy has value equal to the payoff of its correlation. Payoffs on $C_{qa}$ are at most
$\valstar(\game^{\sep}) \leq \frac12$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}), so
$p \notin C_{qa}$.
\end{proof}

The recursion theorem used in Lean is the two-argument form of Mathlib's
\texttt{Nat.Partrec.Code.fixed\_point}, for partial recursive codes. No running-time bound is
needed, so the efficient form of Lemma~\ref{lem:kleene} is not used.

\paragraph{Proof route.} The correspondence with nodes $1.8.8$--$1.8.11$ is at the level of the
conclusion. The game here is not the paper's $\game^{\sep} = \verifier^{\sep}_{C_0}$: it is the
halting-reduction game of a machine that runs the commuting-value semidecider on its own
game. It is explicit in the same sense as the paper's. It is obtained from self-reference,
uniformly from the codes of $g$ and of the semidecider, and neither proof exhibits a
value-$1$ commuting-operator strategy. The paper's proof needs, in the case
$\omegaco = 1$, that $\valstar \leq \frac12$ at a level of its recursion where no soundness is
otherwise known, and gets it from divergence of a Schmidt-rank entanglement requirement
(Remark~\ref{rem:separation-premises}). Here that case is the non-halting case of the
halting reduction, whose soundness is already the value-form compression of the main theorem.
The paper observes, in a footnote to its halting theorem, that the gap
$\valstar < \omegaco$ holds for infinitely many non-halting machines~\cite{AuditSync26}; the
fixed point picks one of them out.
\texttt{scripts/ledger-sync.py}, which walks the ledger's declared dependencies, still lists
the Schmidt-rank clauses of stage $1.5$ among the imports reached from this theorem, as
expected.

\begin{remark}[The paper's construction and its premises]
\label{rem:separation-premises}
\uses{rem:entanglement-form,lem:valco-upper-re,rem:pcc-representation-bridge,
  rem:runtime-contract-bridge}
The paper's proof builds $\game^{\sep}$ as the compressed verifier at a fixed level
$C_0 \geq 2$ of a self-referential family: its decider runs the upper semidecider on its own
succinct description, accepts early if the semidecider halts, and otherwise
compresses~\cite{AuditSync26}. Formalizing \emph{that} game, rather than the one of
Theorem~\ref{thm:separation}, needs the \emph{bipartite Schmidt-rank entanglement variant} of
compression, together with its effective bounded self-reference construction. Write
$\Ent_{\mathrm{bi}}(\game,\nu)$ for the least Schmidt rank of a finite-dimensional
tensor-product strategy attaining value at least $\nu$, or $\infty$ if there is none. This is
the paper's measure; it is distinct from the synchronous dimension requirement $\Ent$ of
Definition~\ref{def:ent}. The extra compression clauses must force $\Ent_{\mathrm{bi}}$ to
diverge along the recursive levels, and the construction must identify the game at $C_0$ with
the game supplied to the upper semidecider, with all specialization and timeout bounds
justified.

These are premises of the paper's construction, not of Theorem~\ref{thm:separation}, and none
of them is formalized. The value-form Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} and its current Lean
interface do not supply the Schmidt-rank clauses. If the source completeness steps are
implemented through the synchronous PCC interface, the representation transfers of
Remark~\ref{rem:pcc-representation-bridge} must also be supplied. An implementation through
the ambient verifier interfaces additionally requires the runtime and answer-cut normalization
of Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-contract-bridge}; the source construction's absolute bounds cannot
simply be read as relative cost coefficients. \texttt{planning/explicit-separation.md} records
the cost.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Which commuting value this is, and why it is not the tracial one]
\label{rem:separation-which-value}
\uses{thm:separation,lem:valco-upper-re,lem:tracial-le-co,def:commuting-value,def:co-value-bipartite}
Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} is stated for the \emph{bipartite} commuting-operator value
$\omegaco$ (Definition~\ref{def:co-value-bipartite}), not for the tracial $\valco$
(Definition~\ref{def:commuting-value}) that this blueprint otherwise carries. That is not a
slip, and the distinction is forced by the proof rather than cosmetic.

The proof turns on the semidecider of Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}, which is complete for
the event $\omegaco < 1$. For the tracial value, Lemma~\ref{lem:tracial-le-co} gives only
$\valco \leq \omegaco$, so the same procedure is a sound semidecider for $\valco < 1$ and not
a complete one, and the fixed point would conclude nothing. The semidecision event is that
the commuting value of the very game being defined is below $1$. No search for a proof of
non-halting, and no preservation theorem for arbitrary commuting-operator strategies, is
used, and the argument constructs no explicit perfect commuting-operator strategy: the
correlation of payoff $1$ is obtained by compactness.

No synchronous decision predicate is asserted: $\game^{\sep}$ is whatever game the halting
reduction produces. Even for synchronous games, Lemma~\ref{lem:tracial-le-co} only gives
$\valco\leq\omegaco$, with the reverse comparison false in general. Thus this route provides
neither a synchronous perfect correlation nor a tracial value-$1$ witness. Any such
formulation would require additional representation and value-transport results.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Refutation of Connes' Embedding Problem}

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[Kirchberg's forward implication]
\label{lem:kirchberg-forward}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.1}
If CEP has an affirmative answer then the minimal and maximal C$^*$-tensor norms coincide on
$C^*(F_\infty) \ot_{\mathrm{alg}} C^*(F_\infty)$, where $F_\infty$ is the free group on
countably many generators and $C^*$ denotes the full unital group algebra.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Source.} Kirchberg; see~\cite{Oza13}. This is the second of the two imports this
chapter carries: the companion ledger records it as \emph{admitted} (node $1.8.6.1$), and it is
the only step of the chain below that is not proved there.

\begin{lemma}[Finitely many generators]
\label{lem:cep-free-group}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.2}
\uses{lem:kirchberg-forward}
Conditional on CEP, $\min = \max$ on
$C^*(F_k) \ot_{\mathrm{alg}} C^*(F_k)$ for every finite $k \geq 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[A completely positive section onto the Bell algebra]
\label{lem:bell-algebra-section}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.3}
For $A = \ast_{x=1}^{k} \C^m$ and $B = C^*(F_k)$ with $k \geq 1$ and $m \geq 2$, the
canonical quotient $q : B \to A$ has a unital completely positive section $\Psi$, obtained as
the $(0,0)$ corner of an explicitly constructed unital $*$-homomorphism
$\gamma : A \to M_m(B)$. In particular $q \Psi = \mathrm{id}$ on all of $A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Transport to the Bell algebra]
\label{lem:cep-bell-algebra}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.4}
\uses{lem:cep-free-group,lem:bell-algebra-section}
Conditional on CEP, the minimal and maximal tensor norms coincide on
$A \ot_{\mathrm{alg}} A$ for every finite Bell algebra $A = \ast_{x=1}^{k} \C^m$,
$k \geq 1$, $m \geq 2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Commuting correlations restrict states on the maximal tensor product]
\label{lem:commuting-restricts-state}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.5}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,lem:cep-bell-algebra}
Every commuting-operator correlation in a finite bipartite scenario with common parameters
$(k,m)$ is the restriction to the coordinate projections of a state on
$A \ot_{\max} A$. Under CEP that same functional is a state on $A \ot_{\min} A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Vector states are dense on finitely many coordinates]
\label{lem:spatial-coordinates}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.6}
On any finite collection of self-adjoint coordinate observables in a faithfully represented
spatial C$^*$-tensor product, every state is approximable by finite convex combinations of
unit-vector states in that same spatial representation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[States on the spatial product give $C_{qa}$ correlations]
\label{lem:spatial-values-cqa}
\ledgernode{1.8.6.7}
\uses{lem:spatial-coordinates}
The coordinate values of any state on a spatial tensor product of finite Bell algebras belong
to $C_{qa}$, the closure of the finite-dimensional tensor-product POVM correlations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[CEP forces equality of the correlation sets]
\label{lem:cep-implies-equal}
\ledgernode{1.8.6, 1.8.6.8}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,lem:commuting-restricts-state,lem:spatial-values-cqa}
If every separable II$_1$ factor embeds into an ultrapower of the hyperfinite II$_1$ factor,
then $C_{qa} = C_{qc}$ in every finite bipartite scenario, including unequal alphabet sizes.
Consequently exhibiting one finite-scenario separation of $C_{qa}$ from $C_{qc}$ refutes CEP.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Connes' Embedding Problem, negative answer]
\label{cor:cep}
\ledgernode{1.8.7}
\uses{cor:tsirelson,lem:cep-implies-equal}
There is a separable II$_1$ factor that does not embed into an ultrapower
$\cal{R}^{\omega}$ of the hyperfinite II$_1$ factor.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Source for the implication.} The correlation-set connection is developed by
Fritz~\cite{Fri12}, Junge et al.~\cite{JNPPSW11} and Ozawa~\cite{Oza13}. The route here
uses only the forward implication in Lemma~\ref{lem:cep-implies-equal}: under CEP the
two bipartite correlation sets coincide, contradicting Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson}.
It does not require a converse equivalence, a synchronous perfect correlation for the
separating game, or a tracial value-$1$ strategy for that game.

\paragraph{Comments.} The formalization plan is the forward chain displayed above:
formulate CEP for separable II$_1$ factors and the ultrapower $\cal{R}^\omega$, then
combine the admitted Kirchberg implication with the finite-generator, Bell-algebra and
state-approximation lemmas to obtain $C_{qc}\subseteq C_{qa}$. The non-embeddable factor
follows by contraposition. A synchronous-correlation construction or a witness generated
by finitely many projections would require additional results and is not a conclusion of
this dependency chain.

\subsection{The commuting-operator class, conditionally}
\label{sec:ds-mipco}

Lin proved that the commuting-operator analogue of $\MIPstar$ is $\coRE$~\cite{Lin25}, by
the compression architecture of this blueprint with the soundness of every stage carried in
the commuting-operator value. This section states his theorem in the vocabulary of
Section~\ref{ch:foundations} and proves it from one hypothesis, the commuting-operator
soundness of gap compression (Definition~\ref{def:compression-co-sound}); the inclusion
$\MIPco \subseteq \coRE$ is proved outright. Nothing else is proved twice: the halting
reduction, its classes, semideciders and class transfer, and the classes $\MIPstar$ and
$\MIPco$ themselves are written once against a \emph{value model}
(Definition~\ref{def:value-model}), an interface that $\valstar$ and $\omegaco$ both satisfy,
and the two theorems differ only in the direction in which the value is semidecidable. The
plan for discharging the hypothesis is \texttt{planning/mipco-track.md}. The companion proof
repository has no $\coRE$ chapter: the paper of record for this section is Lin's, and its
nodes carry no ledger correspondence.

\begin{definition}[Value models]
\label{def:value-model}
\uses{def:q-value,def:co-value-bipartite,def:game-description,def:normal-verifier,
  lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\lean{MIPRE.ValueModel, MIPRE.ValueModel.tensor, MIPRE.ValueModel.commuting,
  MIPRE.ValueModel.LowerRE, MIPRE.ValueModel.UpperRE, MIPRE.ValueModel.HaltingReductionRE,
  MIPRE.ValueModel.HaltingReductionCoRE, MIPRE.Verifier.val, MIPRE.MIPClass}
\leanok
A \emph{value model} $\omega$ assigns to every finite game $\game$ a real number
$\omega(\game)$ such that $\valstar(\game) \leq \omega(\game) \leq 1$, and such that
$\omega$ is invariant under relabeling of the four alphabets, monotone in the decision
predicate, zero on a game that rejects everything, unchanged by padding the answer alphabets
with always-rejected answers, and unchanged by the doubling of the question set. The two
models are $\valstar$ (Definition~\ref{def:q-value}) and $\omegaco$
(Definition~\ref{def:co-value-bipartite}), and every statement below in a model $\omega$ is
one theorem in Lean, instantiated twice. The value of $\verifier_n$ in the model is
$\omega(\verifier_n)$, at an answer bound. The model is \emph{r.e.\ from below} if the set of
triples $(d, p, q)$ with $p/q < \omega(\game_d)$ is recursively enumerable, in the threshold
encoding of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}, and \emph{r.e.\ from above} if the set with
$\omega(\game_d) < p/q$ is: $\valstar$ is r.e.\ from below (Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx})
and $\omegaco$ from above (Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}), and neither has the other
direction, by the two theorems themselves. A \emph{halting reduction of $\RE$ shape} to
$\omega$ is a computable map from machines to game descriptions with $\omega = 1$ on the
machines that halt on the empty input and $\omega \leq \frac12$ on the others; the
\emph{$\coRE$ shape} is the reverse. The class $\MIPClass(\omega)$ is the set of languages
$L$ for which some computable map $g$ from strings to game descriptions has
$\omega(\game_{g(z)}) = 1$ for $z \in L$ and $\omega(\game_{g(z)}) \leq \frac12$ for
$z \notin L$; $\MIPstar$ of Definition~\ref{def:mipstar-computable} is
$\MIPClass(\valstar)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Co-recursively enumerable]
\label{def:core}
\uses{def:re}
\lean{MIPRE.IsCoRE, MIPRE.isCoRE_iff}
\leanok
A language $L \subseteq \{0,1\}^*$ is in $\coRE$ if its complement is in $\RE$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The class $\MIPco$]
\label{def:mipco}
\uses{def:co-value-bipartite,def:game-description,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.MIPCo}
\leanok
A language $L \subseteq \{0,1\}^*$ is in $\MIPco$ if there is a computable map $g$ from bit
strings to game descriptions (Definition~\ref{def:game-description}) such that for every
string $z$,
\begin{equation*}
  z \in L\ \Longrightarrow\ \omegaco(\game_{g(z)}) = 1,
  \qquad
  z \notin L\ \Longrightarrow\ \omegaco(\game_{g(z)}) \leq \tfrac12,
\end{equation*}
in the bipartite commuting-operator value of Definition~\ref{def:co-value-bipartite}: the
class $\MIPClass(\omegaco)$ of Definition~\ref{def:value-model}. As
Definition~\ref{def:mipstar-computable}, this is the computable class, with no
polynomial-time bound on $g$; the polynomial-time class of Lin's paper is contained in it, as
Lemma~\ref{lem:mipstar-poly-sub} shows for $\MIPstar$, and is not formalized.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[$\MIPco \subseteq \coRE$]
\label{lem:mipco-sub-core}
\uses{def:mipco,def:core,lem:valco-upper-re,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.MIPCo.isCoRE, MIPRE.MIPCo.exists_cosemidecider, MIPRE.MIPClass.isCoRE,
  MIPRE.MIPClass.exists_cosemidecider, MIPRE.ValueModel.commuting_upperRE}
\leanok
Every language in $\MIPco$ is co-recursively enumerable. More generally
$\MIPClass(\omega) \subseteq \coRE$ for every value model $\omega$ that is r.e.\ from above.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Let $z \mapsto \game_z$ witness $L \in \MIPClass(\omega)$. Under the promise, $z \notin L$ if
and only if $\omega(\game_z) < 1$. The set of game descriptions $d$ with $\omega(\game_d) < 1$
is recursively enumerable, at $p = q = 1$, and the complement of $L$ is its preimage under
the computable map $z \mapsto \game_z$. For $\omegaco$ the semidecider is
Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re}; Lin's proof runs the NPA hierarchy here~\cite{Lin25}, and the
Lean runs the sum-of-squares certificates of Lemma~\ref{lem:sos-certificates}. The same
argument at the threshold $\frac12$, for a model r.e.\ from below, is
Lemma~\ref{lem:mipstar-sub-re}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Soundness of compression in a value model]
\label{def:compression-co-sound}
\uses{thm:compression,def:value-model,def:lambda-bounded}
\lean{MIPRE.GapCompression.Sound, MIPRE.GapCompression.sound_tensor}
\leanok

$n \geq C_0$,
\begin{equation*}
  \omega(\verifier_{2^n}) \leq \tfrac12 \ \Longrightarrow\
  \omega(\verifier^{\mathrm{compr}}_n) \leq \tfrac12 ,
\end{equation*}
at the answer bounds of the soundness clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}. That clause
is soundness in the model $\valstar$. Soundness in the model $\omegaco$ --- the
\emph{commuting-operator soundness of compression} --- is the model-$co$ case of the
soundness clause of Lin's gap compression theorem~\cite{Lin25}, and the one hypothesis of
Theorems~\ref{thm:halting-co} and~\ref{thm:mipco-eq-core}. No completeness clause in the
commuting-operator model is asked for.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The soundness chain of compression, in a value model]
\label{lem:compress-sound-in}
\uses{def:stage-sound-in,lem:compress-margin,lem:compress-tau}
\lean{MIPRE.Pipeline.output_val_le}
\leanok
If the three stages are sound in the value model $\omega$ (Definition~\ref{def:stage-sound-in}),
then for every $\lambda$-bounded verifier $\verifier$ and every $n \geq C_0$,
$\omega(\verifier_{2^n}) \leq \frac12$ implies $\omega(\vcompr_n) \leq \frac12$, for the verifier

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok

decider rejects every answer beyond a bound, which is the same at every larger bound in every

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Halting reduces to the commuting-operator value, from co-soundness]
\label{thm:halting-co}

\lean{MIPRE.Halting.halting_reduction_upper, MIPRE.Halting.halting_reduction_upper_strings,

  MIPRE.Halting.classA_subset_classOne, MIPRE.Halting.val_tab_eq_one_of_mem_classOne}
\leanok
Assume the gap compression of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-target} is sound in the model
$\omegaco$ (Definition~\ref{def:compression-co-sound}). Then there is a computable map
$\machine \mapsto \game_\machine$ from machines to game descriptions such that
$\omegaco(\game_\machine) \leq \frac12$ if $\machine$ halts on the empty input and
$\omegaco(\game_\machine) = 1$ otherwise. More generally, every value model that is
r.e.\ from above and in which compression is sound has a halting reduction of $\coRE$ shape.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:mipco-sub-core}
\leanok
The halting reduction of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} in a value model $\omega$, with the
direction of the semidecider reversed. Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion-nested} is run
with three classes of strings, each read as a normal form verifier as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} and with the same answer bounds:
\begin{itemize}
\item $A(n)$: the verifier is $n$-bounded, rejects every answer longer than the bound, and
  $\omega(\verifier_n) \leq \frac12$ --- the class $B$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} in the
  model $\omega$. It contains the trivially rejecting string $y_{\mathrm{no}}$ of that
  theorem, whose game has value $0$ in every model, and the compressor of that theorem
  preserves it by soundness in the model, through the class transfer of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} read in $\omega$: the described verifier rejects beyond its
  answer bound, so raising the bound keeps the value; freezing keeps it; soundness carries it
  through compression; and the output's decider accepts what the compressed decider accepts.
\item $B_0(n)$: the class $A$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} --- $n$-bounded, rejecting long
  answers, with a perfect PCC strategy. It contains $y_{\mathrm{yes}}$, and the compressor
  preserves it by the \emph{tensor} completeness of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}.
\item $B_1(n)$: the strings whose tabulated game at level $n$ (the game description of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:halting}, item 2) does not have $\omega < 1$. It contains $B_0(n)$,
  because a perfect PCC strategy gives $\valstar = 1$ and every model dominates $\valstar$;
  its complement is recursively enumerable by the upper semidecider of the model, at
  $p = q = 1$, composed with the computable tabulation.
\end{itemize}
The criterion gives a polynomial-time $g$ with $g(\machine) \in A$ at the start level when
$\machine$ halts and $g(\machine) \in B_1$ otherwise. The game of $\machine$ is the
tabulation of $g(\machine)$ at that level. In the first case it has
$\omega(\verifier_n) \leq \frac12$, the tabulation having the value of the verifier's game in
every model at every bounded string, which is item 2 of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} read in
$\omega$; in the second it does not have $\omega < 1$, so $\omega = 1$. No perfect strategy
of the model is constructed. At $\omega = \omegaco$ this is Lin's verifier
sequence~\cite{Lin25}, with the compressor's preservation of the accepting game read as
tensor completeness, and with Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} in place of the NPA hierarchy.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The projective commuting-operator model}
\label{sec:ds-mipco-projective}

The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:mipco-eq-core} is to be discharged by generalizing the
stage analyses of compression, not by writing them a second time
(\texttt{planning/mipco-track.md}, Phases 1--6). Those analyses are bipartite, vector-state and
projective: a unit vector on a tensor product, the first player's operators acting on the first
factor and the second's on the second, every measurement projective, and the tracial state never
used (\texttt{reports/co-generalization-audit.md}). Their common generalization is a Hilbert
space with a unit vector and two commuting families of projective measurements, of which a
tensor-product strategy is an instance. That model reaches the whole commuting-operator value by
the dilation below. The dilation is elementary: it compares no projections and uses no von
Neumann algebra theory, where the finite-dimensional dilation extends an isometry to a unitary by
counting dimensions.

\begin{definition}[Projective commuting-operator strategy]
\label{def:co-projective}
\uses{def:co-strategy-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.IsProjective}
\leanok
A commuting-operator strategy $\strategy = (\mH, \psi, E, F)$ is \emph{projective} if every
$E^x_a$ and every $F^y_b$ is idempotent. Being positive, they are then orthogonal projections,
and every measurement of the strategy is projective.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Commutation-preserving dilation]
\label{lem:co-dilation}
\uses{def:co-strategy-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.OperatorMatrix.toCLMStarAlgHom, MIPRE.OperatorMatrix.amplify, MIPRE.OperatorMatrix.emb,
  MIPRE.OperatorMatrix.inner_emb_toCLM_emb, MIPRE.OperatorMatrix.toCLM_diagonal_emb,
  MIPRE.Halmos.extension, MIPRE.Halmos.conjTranspose_mul_extension,
  MIPRE.Halmos.extension_mul_conjTranspose, MIPRE.Halmos.naimark,
  MIPRE.Halmos.naimark_mul_conjTranspose_mul, MIPRE.Halmos.proj, MIPRE.Halmos.isStarProjection_proj,
  MIPRE.Halmos.sum_proj, MIPRE.Halmos.proj_naimark_inl_inl,
  MIPRE.Halmos.commute_diagonal_proj_naimark, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.dilateLeft,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_dilateLeft, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.swap,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_swap, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.dilateRight,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_dilateRight}
\leanok
Let $\{E_a\}_{a \in \mA}$ be a POVM on a Hilbert space $\mH$, let $a_0 \in \mA$, and let
$\iota : \mH \to \mH \otimes (\C^{\mA} \oplus \C^{\mA})$ be the embedding $\xi \mapsto \xi \otimes
e_{a_0}$ at the first copy of $a_0$. There is a projective measurement $\{P_a\}_{a \in \mA}$ on
$\mH \otimes (\C^{\mA} \oplus \C^{\mA})$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\iota^* P_a \iota = E_a$ for every $a$, and
  \item $P_a$ commutes with $c \otimes \Id$ for every bounded operator $c$ on $\mH$ that commutes
    with every $E_b$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, for a commuting-operator strategy $\strategy = (\mH, \psi, E, F)$ and a question
$x$ of the first player, the strategy on $\mH \otimes (\C^{\mA} \oplus \C^{\mA})$ with state
$\iota(\psi)$, in which the first player measures the $\{P_a\}$ of $\{E^x_a\}_a$ at $x$ and
$E^{x'}_a \otimes \Id$ at $x' \neq x$ and the second player measures $F^y_b \otimes \Id$, is a
commuting-operator strategy with the correlation of $\strategy$. Its measurement at $x$ is
projective, and so is every measurement that was projective in $\strategy$. The same holds for a
question of the second player.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Let $s_a = \sqrt{E_a}$, by the continuous functional calculus: it is self-adjoint, $\sum_a s_a^2
= \Id$, and it commutes with every operator that commutes with $E_a$. Write operators on $\mH
\otimes \C^n$ as $n \times n$ matrices of operators on $\mH$, so that composition and the adjoint
are the matrix product and the conjugate transpose. Let $W$ be the $\mA \times \mA$ matrix whose
column $a_0$ is $(s_a)_a$ and whose other columns vanish. Then $W^* W$ is the matrix unit at
$(a_0, a_0)$, so $W W^* W = W$ and $W$ is a partial isometry. The block matrix
\begin{equation*}
  U = \begin{pmatrix} W & \Id - W W^* \\ \Id - W^* W & W^* \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
is unitary, by the identities $W(\Id - W^* W) = 0$ and $(\Id - W W^*) W = 0$ and their adjoints.
Let $Q_a$ be the diagonal projection onto the two copies of the coordinate $a$, and put $P_a =
U^* Q_a U$. These are projections summing to $\Id$, and the entry of $P_a$ at the first copy of
$(a_0, a_0)$ is $s_a s_a = E_a$, which is item~1. Every entry of $U$ is a polynomial in the
$s_b$, and so commutes with $c$ as soon as $c$ commutes with every $E_b$, which is item~2.

For the strategy, take $c = F^y_b$. The correlation at $(x, y)$ is $\bra{\iota\psi} P_a
(F^y_b \otimes \Id) \ket{\iota\psi} = \bra{\psi} \iota^* P_a \iota F^y_b \ket{\psi}$, because
$(F^y_b \otimes \Id) \iota = \iota F^y_b$, and item~1 makes it $\bra{\psi} E^x_a F^y_b
\ket{\psi}$. At $x' \neq x$ both operators are amplified, and $\iota$ is an isometry. The
amplification $c \mapsto c \otimes \Id$ is a unital $*$-homomorphism, so it preserves
positivity, sums to $\Id$, commutation and idempotence. For the second player, exchange the
players, which transposes the correlation because the two players' operators commute.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The commuting-operator value is attained on projective strategies]
\label{thm:co-value-projective}
\uses{lem:co-dilation,def:co-projective,def:co-value-bipartite,lem:correlation-sets-basic}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.exists_isProjective_correlation_eq,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.exists_isProjective_value_eq,
  MIPRE.commutingOperatorValue_eq_iSup_isProjective, MIPRE.exists_isProjective_lt_value,
  MIPRE.TensorProductStrategy.isProjective_toCommuting}
\leanok
Every commuting-operator strategy for $\game$ has a projective commuting-operator strategy with
the same correlation, hence the same value. Consequently $\omegaco(\game)$ is the supremum of the
values of the projective commuting-operator strategies, and for every $t \geq 0$ with $t <
\omegaco(\game)$ there is a projective commuting-operator strategy of value greater than $t$.
Every tensor-product strategy is a projective commuting-operator strategy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Dilate the first player's questions one at a time by Lemma~\ref{lem:co-dilation}, then the
second player's. Each step keeps the correlation and every projectivity already obtained, and
makes one more measurement projective. A question has an answer, since its POVM sums to $\Id$ on
a nonzero space, so the lemma applies. The value is the payoff of the correlation, and it
lies in $[0, 1]$, as does $\omegaco(\game)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:correlation-sets-basic}), which
gives the supremum and the strategy of value greater than $t$. A tensor-product strategy
(Definition~\ref{def:tensor-strategy}) measures $A^x_a \otimes \Id$ and $\Id \otimes B^y_b$ with
$A^x_a$ and $B^y_b$ projections.
\end{proof}

The operator calculus of the analyses is restated over the following model, in which a
tensor-product strategy and a commuting-operator strategy are both instances. The players'
operators are elements of two abstract $*$-algebras rather than operators on $\mH$, so that a
product, a sum or an adjoint of one player's operators is again one of that player's and commutes
with the other player's: this is what the matrix analyses get from the two factors of a tensor
product. The state calculus of the matrix analyses, $\|M\psi\|$, the bound $\|Mv\| \leq K\|v\|$
and $\mathrm{Re}\langle \psi, M\psi \rangle$, and every estimate built on it --- the consistency
and commutation analyses, the sandwich and pasting chains, coarse-graining, the adaptation of a
strategy to another game --- is proved once in the model, and the matrix statements of the
earlier chapters are its instances in the tensor-product model
(\texttt{planning/mipco-track.md}, Phase 1(b); Remark~\ref{rem:model-calculus}).

\begin{definition}[Bipartite model]
\label{def:bipartite-model}
\uses{def:co-strategy-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.tensor, MIPRE.StateModel, MIPRE.StateModel.mat,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.toModel, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.commute_of_mem}
\leanok
A \emph{bipartite model} consists of a complex Hilbert space $\mH$, a vector $\psi \in \mH$, a
unital $*$-algebra $\alg$ over $\C$ represented on $\mH$ by a unital $*$-homomorphism
$\pi : \alg \to B(\mH)$, and two unital $*$-algebras $\alg_A$ and $\alg_B$ over $\C$ with unital
$*$-homomorphisms $\pi_A : \alg_A \to \alg$ and $\pi_B : \alg_B \to \alg$ such that $\pi_A(a)$
and $\pi_B(b)$ commute for all $a \in \alg_A$ and $b \in \alg_B$. The first player's operators
are elements of $\alg_A$, the second player's elements of $\alg_B$, and a state quantity of $a$
is computed on the vector $\pi(\pi_A(a))\psi$. That $\psi$ is a unit vector is a hypothesis of
each statement that needs it, not part of the model. It has two instances.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The \emph{tensor-product model} of a vector $\psi \in \C^{d_A} \otimes \C^{d_B}$ has
    $\alg = M_{d_A d_B}(\C)$ acting on $\C^{d_A} \otimes \C^{d_B}$, $\alg_A = M_{d_A}(\C)$,
    $\alg_B = M_{d_B}(\C)$, $\pi_A(X) = X \otimes \Id$ and $\pi_B(Y) = \Id \otimes Y$. Its
    products and sums are the matrix ones, so the matrix calculus is the model's with nothing to
    translate.
  \item The \emph{commuting-operator model} of a commuting-operator strategy
    $(\mH, \psi, E, F)$ has $\alg = B(\mH)$, $\alg_A$ the commutant of the $F^y_b$ and $\alg_B$
    the commutant of $\alg_A$, with the inclusions. They commute by construction and contain the
    $E^x_a$ and the $F^y_b$. A commutant contains the square roots of its positive elements, so
    positivity in $\alg_A$ and in $\alg_B$ is positivity in $B(\mH)$, which is what the
    measurements of the analyses need.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The commuting-operator value is a supremum of model values]
\label{lem:co-value-model}
\uses{def:bipartite-model,def:co-value-bipartite}
\lean{MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.aliceMeas, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.bobMeas,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.aliceMeas_op, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.bobMeas_op,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.correlation_eq_bornProb,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.value_eq_povmValue,
  MIPRE.commutingOperatorValue_eq_iSup_povmValue}
\leanok
The measurements of a commuting-operator strategy are POVMs in the two algebras of its
commuting-operator model (Definition~\ref{def:bipartite-model}), its correlation is that model's
Born rule, and its value is the model's value of those POVMs. So $\omegaco(\game)$ is the
supremum, over commuting-operator strategies, of the value of their measurements in their own
model.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The $E^x_a$ lie in the commutant of the $F^y_b$, and are positive and sum to $\Id$ there because
they are and do in $B(\mH)$; the same holds for the $F^y_b$ in the commutant of that commutant.
The model's Born probability at $(E^x_a, F^y_b)$ is
$\mathrm{Re}\langle \psi, E^x_a F^y_b \psi\rangle$, the correlation, and the value is the same
sum of payoffs against the same probabilities.
\end{proof}

A finite ancilla is adjoined to a model without leaving it: the space becomes the $\ell^2$-sum of
finitely many copies of $\mH$, and the players' algebras become matrix algebras over theirs. This
is the expanded state of the Pauli basis test (Definition~\ref{def:expanded-state}) in any model,
and in the tensor-product model it is that expanded state.

\begin{definition}[Ancilla extension of a bipartite model]
\label{def:ancilla-extension}
\uses{def:bipartite-model,lem:co-dilation}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel.expand, MIPRE.smulKron, MIPRE.liftLeft, MIPRE.liftRight,

\leanok
Let $(\mH, \psi, \alg, \alg_A, \alg_B)$ be a bipartite model, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ finite sets
and $e \in \C^{\alpha \times \beta}$. The \emph{ancilla extension} of the model by $e$ has the
Hilbert space $\mH \otimes \C^{\alpha \times \beta}$, the $\ell^2$-sum of $\alpha \times \beta$
copies of $\mH$; the state $\psi \otimes e$; the algebra $M_{\alpha \times \beta}(\alg)$, acting
entrywise; the first player's algebra $M_\alpha(\alg_A)$, acting as $X \otimes \Id_\beta$; and
the second player's algebra $M_\beta(\alg_B)$, acting as $\Id_\alpha \otimes Y$. For $X \in \alg_A$
and $P \in M_\alpha(\C)$, $X \otimes P \in M_\alpha(\alg_A)$ is the matrix with entries
$P_{ab} X$, and likewise for the second player.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The ancilla extension]
\label{lem:ancilla-extension}
\uses{def:ancilla-extension}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel.bornProb_expand_smulKron,

\begin{lemma}[Strategies in a model, read in $\omegaco$]
\label{lem:model-strategy-co}
\uses{def:bipartite-model,lem:co-value-model,thm:co-value-projective,def:co-projective}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel.toCommuting, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.correlation_toCommuting,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.value_toCommuting,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.povmValue_le_commutingOperatorValue, MIPRE.IsPVMIn.of_map,
  MIPRE.IsPVMIn.of_isStarProjection, MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.isPVMIn_aliceMeas,
  MIPRE.CommutingOperatorStrategy.isPVMIn_bobMeas, MIPRE.exists_isPVMIn_lt_povmValue}
\leanok
Let $(\mH, \psi, \alg, \alg_A, \alg_B)$ be a bipartite model with a unit state and star-ordered
player algebras, and let $\{M^x_a\}$ and $\{N^y_b\}$ be families of POVMs in $\alg_A$ and
$\alg_B$. The operators $\pi(\pi_A(M^x_a))$ and $\pi(\pi_B(N^y_b))$ form a commuting-operator
strategy whose value in any game is the model's value of the two families, so that value is at
most $\omegaco$. Conversely, for every $t \geq 0$ with $t < \omegaco(\game)$ there is a
commuting-operator strategy whose measurements are projective measurements in the two algebras
of its own model and whose value there exceeds $t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
A $*$-homomorphism of star-ordered algebras preserves positivity, sums and commutation, and the
model's Born probability of $(M^x_a, N^y_b)$ is the correlation of the strategy at
$(x, y, a, b)$. For the converse, take a projective strategy of value above $t$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:co-value-projective}) and read its measurements in its model
(Lemma~\ref{lem:co-value-model}). Its operators are projections summing to $\Id$, hence
mutually orthogonal: conjugating $\sum_c P_c = \Id$ by $P_a$ makes the compressions
$P_a P_c P_a = (P_c P_a)^*(P_c P_a)$, $c \neq a$, positive operators summing to $0$, so each
vanishes, and then $P_c P_a = 0$ by the C$^*$-identity. The equations of a projective
measurement hold in the commutants because they hold in $B(\mH)$, which the inclusions reflect.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The repetition bound in $\omegaco$, for answers of bounded length]
\label{lem:repetition-sound-bound-co}
\uses{thm:direct-repetition-co,lem:repetition-sound-bound,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.Repetition.repConstCo, MIPRE.Repetition.commutingOperatorValue_repeat_le_soundBound,
  MIPRE.Repetition.GameSoundIn, MIPRE.Repetition.GameSoundIn.mono,
  MIPRE.Repetition.gameSoundIn_tensor, MIPRE.Repetition.gameSoundIn_commuting}
\leanok
There is a universal constant $c'' > 0$ such that for every game $\game$ on a nonempty question
set whose answer alphabets are the strings of length at most $B$, every $\eps > 0$ with
$\omegaco(\game) \leq 1 - \eps$ and every $k \geq 1$,
\begin{equation*}
  \omegaco\bigl( \game^{k} \bigr) \;\leq\; \exp\Bigl( - \frac{c''\, \eps^{13}\, k}{B + 1} \Bigr)\;.
\end{equation*}
Say that a value model has the \emph{repetition bound at the constant $c$} if this holds with
$\omegaco$ replaced by the model and $c''$ by $c$. The model $\valstar$ has it at the constant
$c'$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:repetition-sound-bound}, the model $\omegaco$ at $c''$, and a model that
has it at $c$ has it at every smaller constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-co} at $\eps' = 1 - \omegaco(\game) \geq \eps$, through the
arithmetic of Lemma~\ref{lem:repetition-sound-bound}: its exponent is $\eps'^7$, and
$\eps^{13} \leq \eps^7 \leq \eps'^7$ because $\eps \leq \eps' \leq 1$. Take
$c'' = c / (1 + 2 \log 3)$ for the constant $c$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-co}. The
bound decreases as the constant grows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Parallel repetition is sound in $\omegaco$]
\label{thm:parallel-repetition-co}
\uses{thm:parallel-repetition,lem:repetition-sound-bound-co,def:stage-sound-in,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.val_repVerifier, MIPRE.val_repVerifier_le, MIPRE.Repetition.soundBound_anti,
  MIPRE.Repetition.withConst, MIPRE.Repetition.SoundIn.withConst,
  MIPRE.repetition_withConst_soundIn, MIPRE.Repetition.repConstBoth, MIPRE.repetitionCo,
  MIPRE.repetitionCo_soundIn_commuting}
\leanok
The procedure of Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition}, at the constant
$\min(c', c'')$ of the two repetition bounds (Lemma~\ref{lem:repetition-sound-bound-co}), is
sound in the model $\valstar$ and in the model $\omegaco$ (Definition~\ref{def:stage-sound-in}):
for all $0 < \eps \leq 1$, $\omegaco(\verifier_n) \leq 1 - \eps$ at the parse length gives
$\omegaco(\vrep_n) \leq \exp(-\min(c', c'')\, \eps^{13} k(n) / (B(n) + 1))$. More generally, in
every value model the value of $\vrep_n$ is that of the $k(n)$-fold direct repetition of
$\verifier_n$ at the parse length, so the procedure is sound in every model with the repetition
bound at a constant at least its own.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The value of $\vrep_n$ is the value of the repeated game in every model: the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition} identifies the two games up to an equivalence of the
questions and the padding of the answers by always-rejected ones, and a value model is invariant
under both (Definition~\ref{def:value-model}). So soundness in a model is the repetition bound
in that model. Lowering the constant of the procedure keeps every clause, the bound being
antitone in the constant; the procedure at $\min(c', c'')$ is the same verifier. The main
theorem keeps the constant $c'$, so $\MIPstar = \RE$ does not depend on
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-co}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of oracularization, in a bipartite model]
\label{lem:oracular-soundness-model}
\uses{def:oracular-game,def:bipartite-model,def:cross-distance}
\lean{MIPRE.SeededGame.povmValue_sound_ge, MIPRE.StateModel.sum_snorm_sq_ge_of_close,
  MIPRE.StateModel.sum_snorm_sq_eq_one_of_isPVMIn, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.sum_snorm_sq_chain,
  MIPRE.SeededGame.oracleView, MIPRE.SeededGame.isPVMIn_oracleJoint,
  MIPRE.SeededGame.sum_snorm_sq_joint_le, MIPRE.SeededGame.condWin_oracle_le,
  MIPRE.SeededGame.condWin_sound_ge, MIPRE.SeededGame.sum_condFail_four_le}
\leanok
Let $S$ be a seeded game with nonempty seed space and nonempty answer alphabet, let
$0 \leq \eps \leq 1$, and let $\{N^q_u\}$ and $\{N'^q_u\}$, one for each question $q$ of the
oracularization of $S$, be projective measurements in the first and the second player's algebra
of a bipartite model with a unit state, of model value at least $1 - \eps$ in the
oracularization of $S$. Then the families in which the first player measures
$\{N^{(\alice,\, x)}_a\}$ and the second $\{N'^{(\bob,\, y)}_b\}$, each relabelled so that the
oracle-shaped answers are grouped into one fixed answer, have model value at least
$1 - 24\sqrt{\eps}$ in the game of $S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:qld-combined-points}
\leanok
Fix a seed $z$ and write $O$ for the first player's measurement at $(\oracle, z)$, $O^\alice_u$
and $O^\bob_v$ for its sums over the answers whose Alice, respectively Bob, component is $u$,
respectively $v$, $C$ for the first player's measurement at $(\alice, L^\alice(z))$, and $P$, $Q$
for the second player's at $(\alice, L^\alice(z))$ and $(\bob, L^\bob(z))$. In the model's
algebra, $\pi_A(C_u)\pi_B(Q_v) - \pi_A(O^\alice_u O^\bob_v) = X_1 + X_2 + X_3$, where
\begin{equation*}
  X_1 = \pi_B(Q_v)(\pi_A(C_u) - \pi_B(P_u)), \quad
  X_2 = \pi_B(Q_v)(\pi_B(P_u) - \pi_A(O^\alice_u)), \quad
  X_3 = \pi_A(O^\alice_u)(\pi_B(Q_v) - \pi_A(O^\bob_v)),
\end{equation*}
because the two players' operators commute. The front factors are projective measurements, so
summed over their outcome they cost nothing, and each of the three deviations is the cross-party
inconsistency of one role pair --- $(\alice, \alice)$, $(\oracle, \alice)$, $(\oracle, \bob)$ ---
at which acceptance forces the two answers compared to agree, so by the estimate of
Definition~\ref{def:cross-distance} each is at most twice that pair's conditional failure. So
$\delta_z = \sum_{u,v} \|\pi(\pi_A(C_u)\pi_B(Q_v) - \pi_A(O^\alice_u O^\bob_v))\psi\|^2$ is at
most $6$ times the sum of the three conditional failures. Both
$\{\pi_A(C_u)\pi_B(Q_v)\}$ and $\{\pi_A(O^\alice_u O^\bob_v)\}$ are projective measurements
indexed by pairs, the second because distinct outcomes of $O$ are orthogonal, and on the pairs of
isolated answers the input predicate accepts at $(L^\alice(z), L^\bob(z))$ the second carries at
least the oracle's acceptance probability at $(\oracle, \oracle)$. The vector form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:close-measurements-close-values} then puts the conditional value of the extracted
families at the seed at least one minus the conditional failure at $(\oracle, \oracle)$ minus
$2\sqrt{\delta_z}$. The four role pairs used are distinct and each carries a ninth of the
question weight, so their conditional failures average to at most $9\eps$ in total, and with
Cauchy--Schwarz taking the average inside the square root the value is at least
$1 - 9\eps - 2\sqrt{54\eps} \geq 1 - 24\sqrt\eps$. Nothing here uses a dimension: projectivity
is a hypothesis, and in the commuting-operator model it is supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:model-strategy-co}. The matrix analysis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:oracular-soundness-tensor} is this one in the tensor-product model.
\end{proof}

\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Answer reduction in the model}
\label{sec:ds-mipco-phase3}

Phase 3 of \texttt{planning/mipco-track.md} reads answer reduction in $\omegaco$. Its soundness
analysis (Lemmas~\ref{lem:ar-soundness-setup} to~\ref{lem:ar-error-assembly}) works with a
strategy: it restricts it to the copies of the low-individual-degree test, extracts polynomial
measurements from the pieces, and decodes from them a strategy for the typed oracularized game of
the input verifier. It is written once, for a projective strategy in a bipartite model, and the
one fact about the low-individual-degree test it uses, the soundness of the seeded test
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lidt-cl-soundness}), becomes a hypothesis on the model. That hypothesis holds in
the tensor-product model, which gives back the soundness clause of
Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}; in the commuting-operator model it is what
Corollary~\ref{cor:mipco-from-stages} now assumes in place of answer reduction's clause. The
analysis needs no dilation: its decoded measurements are coarse-grainings of the extracted
projective ones, where the matrix proof dilated a POVM strategy.

\begin{definition}[Projective strategy in a bipartite model]
\label{def:model-proj-strategy}
\uses{def:bipartite-model,def:value-model}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel.ProjStrat, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.ProjStrat.value,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.ProjStrat.failAt, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.ProjStrat.adapt,

  MIPRE.POVMIn.isPVMIn_map}
\leanok
A \emph{projective strategy} for a game $\game$ in a bipartite model $(\mH, \psi, \alg,
\alg_A, \alg_B)$ with a unit state (Definition~\ref{def:bipartite-model}) is a projective
measurement $\{A^x_a\}_a$ in $\alg_A$ for each question $x$ of the first player and a projective
measurement $\{B^y_b\}_b$ in $\alg_B$ for each question $y$ of the second. Its value is the model's
value of the two families, and its conditional failure at $(x, y)$ is the probability of rejection
given the question pair. It is played on another game through a map of the questions and a
question-dependent coarse-graining of the answers, relabelled along equivalences, or restricted to
other questions; a coarse-graining of a projective measurement is projective, so these are
projective strategies. The \emph{inconsistency} of a family $\{P^x\}$ in $\alg_A$ and a family
$\{Q^x\}$ in $\alg_B$ with a common outcome set, on an index weighted by $\mu$, is
$\sum_x \mu(x) \sum_{a \neq b} \mathrm{Re}\langle \psi, \pi(\pi_A(P^x_a)\pi_B(Q^x_b))\psi\rangle$,
Definition~\ref{def:inconsistency} in the model. A value model $\omega$ \emph{dominates} the model
if every projective strategy in it has value at most $\omega$ of its game.
\end{definition}

The failure of a projective
strategy is the average of its conditional failures, each in $[0, 1]$; its value is in $[0, 1]$;
played through maps under which every accepted tuple stays accepted, it fails no more often at

\end{lemma}

\leanok

strategy only its Born probabilities, their nonnegativity and their normalization, which hold in
the model (Lemma~\ref{lem:model-strategy-co}).
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Introspection in the model, given the Pauli basis test}
\label{sec:ds-mipco-phase4}

Phase 4 of \texttt{planning/mipco-track.md} reads introspection in $\omegaco$. Its soundness
analysis (Lemmas~\ref{lem:intro-pauli-restriction} to~\ref{lem:introspection-supply}) works with
a strategy: it restricts it to the Pauli block, extracts from the Pauli basis test local
isometries into an EPR register tensored with an auxiliary state, transports the strategy there,
replaces its two Introspect measurements stage by stage by projective ones dilated on fresh
ancillas, and reads a strategy for the original game off the result. It is written once, for a
projective strategy in a bipartite model, and the one fact about the Pauli basis test it uses, the
conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:qld}, becomes a hypothesis on the model. That hypothesis holds in
the tensor-product model, which gives back the soundness clause of
Theorem~\ref{thm:introspection}; in the commuting-operator model it is what
Corollary~\ref{cor:mipco-from-stages} now assumes in place of introspection's clause. Four things
change with the setting, each a definition below. The local isometries are one local isometry of
models, whose homomorphisms are not unital, so a transported measurement is completed by the
complement of the image. The EPR register tensored with an auxiliary state is the register model
of an auxiliary model. The Naimark dilations of the adaptive stages are Halmos dilations of a
Kraus family in the first player's algebra, whose ancilla has a size chosen along the way, so each
stage returns a new model; every strategy of it reduces to one of the original model, which is how
a value model dominating the auxiliary model dominates the model of the extracted strategy. And the
extracted strategy is a POVM strategy there, which the value model has to dominate.

\begin{definition}[The order of a matrix algebra over an ordered $*$-ring]
\label{def:block-order}
\uses{def:ancilla-extension}
\lean{MIPRE.MatrixStar.cone, MIPRE.MatrixStar.instPartialOrderStar,
  MIPRE.MatrixStar.instStarOrderedRing, MIPRE.MatrixStar.instStarProper,
  MIPRE.MatrixStar.instStarProperComplex, MIPRE.StarProper.of_cstarRing,
  MIPRE.StarSubalgebra.instStarProper}
\leanok
For a $*$-ring $R$ ordered by its cone of sums of elements $z^* z$, the $n \times n$ matrices over
$R$ are ordered in the same way: $X \leq Y$ when $Y - X$ is a sum of matrices $Z^* Z$. When $R$ is
\emph{proper}, $z^* z = 0$ only for $z = 0$, the relation is antisymmetric, since an element of the
cone has nonnegative diagonal entries and a vanishing diagonal entry kills its row and its column,
and the matrices over $R$ are proper again. $C^*$-algebras and their $*$-subalgebras are proper, and
so are the complex matrices; on those the order used is the positive semidefinite one. This is what
lets the players' algebras of an ancilla extension (Definition~\ref{def:ancilla-extension}) carry
POVMs, over and over as registers are adjoined.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Local isometries of bipartite models]
\label{def:local-isometry}
\uses{def:bipartite-model,def:model-proj-strategy}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.withState,
  MIPRE.StateModel.withState, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.comp,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.swap, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.transportOpA,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.transportA, MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.transportB,
  MIPRE.POVMIn.pushforward, MIPRE.IsPVMIn.toPOVMIn}
\leanok
A \emph{local isometry} from a bipartite model $M$ to a bipartite model $M'$ is a linear isometry
$W$ of their Hilbert spaces with, for each player, a non-unital $*$-homomorphism $\Phi$ of the
player's algebra of $M$ into that of $M'$, such that $W$ intertwines the two representations:
$\pi'(\pi'_A(\Phi_A(a)))\, W = W\, \pi(\pi_A(a))$, and likewise for the second player. In the
tensor-product model, $X \mapsto V_A X V_A^\dagger$ for an isometry $V_A$ is such a $\Phi_A$, not
unital since $\Phi_A(\Id) = V_A V_A^\dagger$. Local isometries compose and exchange with the
players. A projective measurement $\{P_a\}$ of the first player is \emph{transported} to the
projective measurement $\{\Phi_A(P_a) + [a = a_0](\Id - \Phi_A(\Id))\}$ of $M'$, the complement of
the image added at one fixed outcome $a_0$, and likewise for the second player. The model $M$ with
its state replaced by a vector $v$ of its Hilbert space is written $M^{v}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Local isometries carry the Born rule]
\label{lem:local-isometry}
\uses{def:local-isometry}
\lean{MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.intertwine,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.bornProb_withState,
  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.stateSqNorm_withState,

  MIPRE.BipartiteModel.LocalIsometry.povmValue_pushforward}
\leanok
Let $W, \Phi$ be a local isometry from $M$ to $M'$. On the transported state $W\psi$, the Born
probability of $(\Phi_A(a), \Phi_B(b))$ in $M'^{W\psi}$ is that of $(a, b)$ in $M$, and so is every
squared state norm and cross-party deviation; when $W\psi$ is the state of $M'$ these hold in $M'$.

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Every quantity is a quadratic form of a product of represented operators on the state, and
intertwining moves $W$ past each factor; $W$ preserves inner products. The transported effects are

projection $\Id - \Phi_A(\Id)$, and the complement annihilates the image of $W$.
\end{proof}

  MIPRE.QLD.ApproxSoundIn}
\leanok
The Pauli basis test is \emph{sound in the bipartite model $M$, with the value model $\omega$}, if
the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:qld} holds for the projective strategies in $M$, in its shape
and at its error: for every admissible $(q, m, d)$, a projective strategy in $M$ failing the test
with probability at most $\eps$ admits an ancilla model $N$ on a Hilbert space, a unit state and
proper ordered algebras, and a local isometry of $M$ into the register model of $N$ on
$\F_q^{\{0,1\}^m}$, carrying the state of $M$ and each player's coarse Pauli measurements within
the error of Theorem~\ref{thm:qld} of the state of the register model and of the honest Pauli
projectors, with $\omega$ dominating the POVM strategies of $N$. It is \emph{sound in the
commuting-operator model} if it is sound, with $\omegaco$, in the model of every
commuting-operator strategy. A value model is \emph{approached in models where the test is sound}
if, for every game $\game$ and every $0 \leq t < \omega(\game)$, some projective strategy for
$\game$ in a model on a Hilbert space in which the test is sound with $\omega$ has value greater
than $t$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The Pauli basis test is sound in the tensor-product model]
\label{lem:qld-sound-in-tensor}
\uses{def:qld-sound-in,thm:qld,lem:tensor-expand,lem:povm-domination,
  lem:model-proj-strategy-values}
\lean{MIPRE.QLD.soundIn_tensor, MIPRE.QLD.tensorAncilla, MIPRE.QLD.tensorPhi,
  MIPRE.QLD.tensorPhi_W_ψ, MIPRE.QLD.tensorPhi_alice, MIPRE.QLD.tensorPhi_bob,
  MIPRE.QLD.approxSoundIn_tensor, MIPRE.QLD.approxSoundIn_commuting}
\leanok
The Pauli basis test is sound, with $\valstar$, in the tensor-product model of every state, and
$\valstar$ is approached in models where the test is sound. If the test is sound in the
commuting-operator model, then $\omegaco$ is approached in models where it is sound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
The ancilla model is the tensor-product model of $|\mathrm{aux}\rangle$ and the local isometry is
$V_A \otimes V_B$ followed by the reading of the flat register state as the register model
(Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-expand}); every error of Theorem~\ref{thm:qld} is then the corresponding
error in the model, and $\valstar$ dominates the POVM strategies of a tensor-product model
(Lemma~\ref{lem:povm-domination}). The approximations are
Lemma~\ref{lem:model-proj-strategy-values}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Introspection is sound in $\omegaco$, given the Pauli basis test]
\label{thm:intro-sound-co}
\uses{lem:introspection-supply,def:qld-sound-in,lem:qld-sound-in-tensor,lem:povm-domination,
  def:stage-sound-in}
\lean{MIPRE.Introspection.seven_soundIn, MIPRE.Introspection.seven_soundIn_commuting}
\leanok
The introspection of Lemma~\ref{lem:introspection-supply} is sound
(Definition~\ref{def:stage-sound-in}) in every value model approached in models where the Pauli
basis test is sound and approached by projective strategies, at the constants $a$, $b$ of its
soundness clause in $\valstar$. In particular, if the Pauli basis test is sound in the
commuting-operator model, introspection is sound in $\omegaco$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Let the value of the output verifier exceed $1 - \eps$, with $\eps < 1$; otherwise the loss is at
least $1$. The output rejects answers beyond its cutoff and agrees with the reference verifier, so
the value of the reference game at the cutoff exceeds $1 - \eps$, and a projective strategy of
value above $1 - \eps$ for it exists in a model where the Pauli basis test is sound. Restricted to
the typed raw game, decoded to the canonical game and restricted to the Pauli block, it stays in
that model, where the test supplies the extraction; the analysis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:intro-valid-isometric-soundness}, stated for a strategy in any model, then bounds
the value of the padded source game in every value model dominating the ancilla model, and removing
the padding, which needs the approximation by projective strategies, bounds the source verifier's
value. The constants are those of the clause in $\valstar$, which is the case of $\valstar$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:qld-sound-in-tensor} and Lemma~\ref{lem:povm-domination}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Further directions}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:further}
Other candidate consequences to record as the project matures: undecidability of the
existence of \emph{perfect} strategies for synchronous games and its group-theoretic
reformulations (via the synchronous algebra of a game); quantitative non-closure of
$C_q$ and rates for the NPA hierarchy; uncomputability results for entanglement
requirements ($\Ent(\cdot, \frac12)$ grows faster than any computable function along
the halting games), which need the entanglement form of the pipeline
(Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}) rather than the value form adopted here; the
commuting-operator counterpart $\MIPco = \coRE$~\cite{Lin23,Lin25}, stated and proved
conditionally in Section~\ref{sec:ds-mipco} (Theorem~\ref{thm:mipco-eq-core}), where what
remains is the commuting-operator soundness of compression; and, more speculatively,
connections
to the undecidability of the spectral gap and to the existence of non-hyperlinear
groups, the latter being \emph{not} implied by CEP's failure and best recorded as an
open problem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Provenance of this chapter}

\begin{remark}[Stage 1.8 of the ledger, and how much of it has been challenged]
\label{rem:downstream-nodes}
\ledgernode{1.8}
\uses{thm:separation,cor:tsirelson,cor:cep,lem:cep-implies-equal,lem:valco-upper-re,lem:rcf-decision,rem:admitted-nodes}
The statements of this chapter were roadmap sketches until September 2026, when the companion
campaign opened a stage for them: stage $1.8$, twenty-seven nodes, which the statements above
now account for one for one. Three things about that stage are worth carrying here rather than
leaving in the other repository.

First, it is where two of the campaign's three remaining admissions live ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:rcf-decision} (node $1.8.3$) and the forward Kirchberg implication inside
Lemma~\ref{lem:cep-implies-equal} (node $1.8.6.1$). Neither is reached by the main theorem:
they are assumptions of the \emph{corollaries}, which is the right place for them, and
Remark~\ref{rem:admitted-nodes} keeps the main theorem's own surface to a single classical
algorithmic result. The Lean proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson} does not rest on node
$1.8.3$: it proves Lemma~\ref{lem:valco-upper-re} through
Theorem~\ref{thm:nc-positivstellensatz} instead. The ledger's own dependencies are unchanged,
so \texttt{scripts/ledger-sync.py} keeps reporting $1.8.3$ as reached from these statements.

Second, the ledger's separation route needs more than the main theorem. The construction needs
effective bounded self-reference at a fixed $C_0 \geq 2$ through one canonical
specialization-and-timeout compiler (node $1.8.8$); the commuting value is $1$ because halting
of the upper semidecider would supply one-dimensional perfect PCC strategies at sufficiently
large levels, and ordinary completeness would propagate perfect finite-dimensional strategies
to the initial level (node $1.8.9$); and $\valstar\leq\frac12$ uses divergence of the
\emph{bipartite Schmidt-rank} requirement along $n_{j+1}=2^{n_j}$ (node $1.8.10$). The Lean
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} needs none of this: it applies the recursion theorem to
the halting reduction and the upper semidecider, and its correspondence with these nodes is at
the level of the conclusion. The three obligations remain those of the paper's own game,
recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:separation-premises}.

Third, and this is the part to be honest about: these nodes have had far less adversarial
scrutiny than the rest of the ledger. Two of the twenty-seven carry a challenge, against $85\%$
of the nodes in the original campaign state, and the adversarial round of 2026-09-14 --- which
re-examined seventy-five nodes and found one real defect
(Remark~\ref{rem:sync-transfer-marginal}) --- worked entirely within stage $1.2$ and did not
touch stage $1.8$ at all. Node $1.8$'s own statement says its derivations ``remain pending
adversarial review''. So the annotations in this chapter record that a decomposition exists and
that it has been checked once, not that it has been attacked; treat the statements here as
better-specified sketches rather than as verified mathematics, and expect the numbered details
above to move.
\end{remark}
