\section{Structure of the proof}
\label{ch:proof-structure}

This chapter records the main transformations of~\cite{JNVWY20} at headline level: for
each, what it does, what it relies on, and one theorem statement so that the dependency
graph reflects the architecture. Working out each section --- restating it in the
synchronous framework, decomposing it into lemmas, and setting the constants --- is a
main task of the project. The paper factors several of these transformations through
\emph{typed} verifiers and a detyping step; that machinery is not
reproduced here and should be introduced locally where needed.

Following Lin~\cite{Lin25}, every soundness statement below is in \emph{value form}: a
transformation preserves the property ``value at most $1 - \eps$'' up to a quantitative
loss, and compression preserves ``value at most $\frac12$''. The three inner
transformations, repetition and compression all carry that property in $\valstar$, the
bipartite tensor-product value: it is the class the compressibility criterion is
instantiated with and the form Definition~\ref{def:mipstar} requires
(Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}), and no passage between the two values occurs
anywhere on the path. The synchronous value is not used, and neither is the
almost-synchronous transport of Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync}; see
Remark~\ref{rem:sync-invariant} for why that is the better arrangement and
Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route} for the evidence. No entanglement lower bound is tracked. Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored} explains why none is needed,
Section~\ref{sec:rr-compression} states the abstract lemma (Lin's compressibility
criterion) that turns value-form compression into the halting reduction, and the
entanglement clauses of~\cite{JNVWY20} are recorded for reference in
Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}.

\subsection{Conditionally linear functions and samplers}
\label{sec:ps-cl}

Question distributions must be structured enough that players can sample them
\emph{on their own} in a certifiable way (introspection), yet expressive enough to
implement all the verifiers in the pipeline. The right class is that of conditionally
linear distributions: linear maps applied in stages, where each stage may depend on
the output of the previous ones.

\begin{definition}[Conditionally linear function]
\label{def:cl-function}
\ledgernode{1.1.2}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun,
  MIPRE.CL.clDist}
\leanok
Let $V = \F^n$ for a finite field $\F$ and $n \geq 0$. A \emph{register subspace} of $V$ is
the span of a subset of the standard basis; for a register subspace $W$ and $x \in V$, $x^W$
denotes the projection of $x$ onto $W$ along the complementary register subspace. For
$\ell \geq 0$, the \emph{$\ell$-level conditionally linear (CL) functions} on $V$ are defined
inductively: the only $0$-level CL function is the zero function on $V$; for $\ell \geq 1$, an
$\ell$-level CL function is a function $L : V \to V$ of the form
\begin{equation*}
  L(x) = L_1\bigl(x^{V_1}\bigr) + L_{>1,\, L_1(x^{V_1})}\bigl(x^{V_{>1}}\bigr)
\end{equation*}
for complementary register subspaces $V = V_1 \oplus V_{>1}$, a linear map $L_1 : V_1 \to
V_1$, and, for each $v$ in the range of $L_1$, an $(\ell-1)$-level CL function $L_{>1,v}$ on
$V_{>1}$. A \emph{CL distribution} is the distribution of $(L^\alice(x), L^\bob(x))$ for
uniformly random $x \in V$ and CL functions $L^\alice, L^\bob$ on $V$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Comments.} This is the paper's definition (\texttt{linear.tex},
\texttt{def:cl-func}); the paraphrase this blueprint carried before started at level $1$ and
let $V_1 \oplus V_{>1}$ be any direct sum decomposition, both departures from the paper. Two
things about the formalization. Every consumer of the definition --- the structure lemma
below, the query interface of a sampler, the hiding tests of introspection --- works with the
decomposition data $(V_1, L_1, L_{>1,\cdot})$, which the function does not determine; so the
Lean object is a \emph{presentation}, \texttt{CLFun}, an inductive type indexed by the level,
with \texttt{CLFun.eval} the function it presents and \texttt{IsCLFun} the paper's notion
(some presentation on $V$ evaluates to the function). And presentations live on one ambient
space $\F^\iota$ whose register subspaces are sets of coordinates; ``a presentation on $V_T$''
is the predicate \texttt{CLFun.SupportedOn}, under which the paper's $V_{>1}$ is the
complement of $V_1$ inside $V_T$. The continuations $L_{>1,v}$ are indexed by all $v$ rather
than by the range of $L_1$; the extra ones are never evaluated. The distribution is
\texttt{clDist}, in the real-valued format of Definition~\ref{def:game}.

\begin{lemma}[Structure of a CL function]
\label{lem:cl-structure}
\uses{def:cl-function}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun.truncate, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorAt, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapAt,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorOfPrefix, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapOfPrefix, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.ExactlyOn,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.eval_truncate_eq_sum, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.disjoint_factorAt,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.factorAt_subset, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.ExactlyOn.biUnion_factorAt,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.factorOfPrefix_eval, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.mapOfPrefix_eval}
\leanok
Let $L$ be an $\ell$-level CL function on $V$, given with its decomposition data. For $k \in
\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$ there are a $k$-level CL function $L_{\leq k} : V \to V$ on $V$, the
\emph{$k$-th marginal} (with $L_{\le 0} = 0$ and $L_{<k} = L_{\le k-1}$), and, for every
prefix $u \in L_{<k}(V)$, a register subspace $V_{k,u}$ of $V$, the \emph{$k$-th factor space},
and a linear map $L_{k,u} : V_{k,u} \to V_{k,u}$, the \emph{$k$-th linear map}, such that for
all $x \in V$, writing $u_i = L_{<i}(x)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L_{\leq k}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} L_{i,u_i}\bigl(x^{V_{i,u_i}}\bigr)$, and
  $L = L_{\le \ell}$;
\item the factor spaces $V_{1,u_1}, \ldots, V_{\ell,u_\ell}$ are pairwise disjoint register
  subspaces of $V$, and $V = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell} V_{i,u_i}$ when the data are
  \emph{exact}, that is, when the last level of every branch leaves no coordinate over.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
This is \texttt{lem:cl-kth} of \texttt{linear.tex}, by induction on the level. In Lean the
marginal is \texttt{CLFun.truncate}; the factor space and the linear map of a stage are
computed both along a run (\texttt{factorAt}, \texttt{mapAt}) and from the prefix alone
(\texttt{factorOfPrefix}, \texttt{mapOfPrefix}), and the two agree
(\texttt{factorOfPrefix\_eval}, \texttt{mapOfPrefix\_eval}): that is what makes the paper's
indexing by prefixes well defined, and it is the fact the queries of a sampler rest on.
Exactness is \texttt{CLFun.ExactlyOn}. The paper states the lemma as an equivalence, with the
data normalized so that the factor spaces always exhaust $V$; the formalization keeps the
paper's own inductive definition, under which a level-$0$ remainder is allowed, and records
exactness as the separate predicate that samplers are required to satisfy.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closure of CL functions]
\label{lem:cl-closure}
\uses{def:cl-function,lem:cl-structure}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun.concat, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.directSum, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.succ,
  MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.concat, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.directSum, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.directSum'}
\leanok
Let $V = U \oplus W$ be a decomposition of $V$ into complementary register subspaces.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Levels.) Every $\ell$-level CL function is $(\ell+1)$-level.
\item (Concatenation.) If $L$ is a $k$-level CL function on $U$ and $R_u$ an $\ell$-level CL
  function on $W$ for each $u \in L(U)$, then $x \mapsto L(x^U) + R_{L(x^U)}(x^W)$ is a
  $(k+\ell)$-level CL function on $V$.
\item (Direct sums.) If $L$ is a $k$-level CL function on $U$ and $R$ an $\ell$-level CL
  function on $W$, then $x \mapsto L(x^U) + R(x^W)$ is a $\max(k,\ell)$-level CL function on
  $V$.
\end{enumerate}
Concatenation and direct sums preserve exactness in the sense of
Lemma~\ref{lem:cl-structure}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{rk:higher-level}, \texttt{lem:cl-concat} and \texttt{lem:cl-func-prod} of
\texttt{linear.tex}, the last for two summands, which iterates. The Lean constructions are on
presentations and carry no hypotheses; that the supports are disjoint enters in the evaluation
lemmas identifying the function presented.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Downsizing]
\label{lem:cl-downsize}
\uses{def:cl-function,lem:cl-structure}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.downsizeEquiv, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval_downsize,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval_truncate_downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorOfPrefix_downsize,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapOfPrefix_downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.ExactlyOn.downsize,
  MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.downsize, MIPRE.CL.clDist_downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.reindex,
  MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.reindex}
\leanok
Let $\F$ be a finite field, $\mathbb{K}$ a subfield, and $e_1, \ldots, e_t$ a basis of $\F$
over $\mathbb{K}$; let $\downsize : \F^n \to \mathbb{K}^{nt}$ be the $\mathbb{K}$-linear
bijection reading off the coordinates of each entry in this basis. If $L$ is an $\ell$-level CL
function on $V = \F^n$, then $L^\downsize = \downsize \circ L \circ \downsize^{-1}$ is an
$\ell$-level CL function on $\mathbb{K}^{nt}$, whose decomposition data are the downsized data
of $L$: for $j \in \{1, \ldots, \ell\}$ and $u \in L_{<j}(V)$,
\begin{equation*}
  L^\downsize_{\le j} = \downsize \circ L_{\le j} \circ \downsize^{-1}, \qquad
  V^\downsize_{j,\, \downsize(u)} = \downsize(V_{j,u}), \qquad
  L^\downsize_{j,\, \downsize(u)} = \downsize \circ L_{j,u} \circ \downsize^{-1},
\end{equation*}
where $\downsize(V_{j,u})$ is the register subspace of $\mathbb{K}^{nt}$ on the coordinates
$(i, k)$ with $i$ a coordinate of $V_{j,u}$. Exactness is preserved, and the CL distribution of
$(L^\downsize, R^\downsize)$ is the image under $\downsize$ of that of $(L, R)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{lem:cl-downsize} and \texttt{lem:downsize-cl-dist} of \texttt{linear.tex}, by
induction on the level; the one fact used is that $\downsize$ commutes with the coordinate
projections onto register subspaces (\texttt{lem:downsize\_subspace} of
\texttt{preliminaries.tex}, in the register case).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} The paper states the lemma for admissible $q = 2^t$, $t$ odd, with the
self-dual normal basis of Lemma~\ref{lem:self-dual-basis}; the formalization proves it for any
basis of any finite field over any subfield, the coordinates of $\mathbb{K}^{nt}$ being indexed
by pairs $(i, k)$ and identified with $\{1, \ldots, nt\}$ by a reindexing
(\texttt{CLFun.reindex}, the transport along any bijection of coordinates, which preserves
everything above). The self-dual normal basis matters elsewhere: for the arithmetic of
$\F_{2^t}$ to be computable from $\F_2$-data in polynomial time by the samplers and deciders
that use it, a matter for the efficient-computability toolkit rather than for this lemma. The
paper's downsized \emph{sampler} (\texttt{def:downsize\_sampler},
\texttt{lem:downsize\_sampler}), a Turing machine wrapping a sampler over $\F_{2^t}$, has no
counterpart here: samplers are over $\F_2$ by definition (Definition~\ref{def:sampler}), and a
construction native to $\F_{2^t}$, such as the introspection sampler, is to present the
downsized CL functions directly, this lemma supplying their decomposition data.

\begin{remark}[Why the pipeline is bipartite throughout]
\label{rem:bipartite-route}
\uses{def:q-value,def:sync-value,thm:almost-sync,rem:almost-sync-hypothesis,rem:sync-invariant,lem:sync-le-valstar}
Every soundness clause of this chapter is stated in $\valstar$ and none in $\synval$. The
choice is deliberate and rests on two things.

First, it is forced at the outer end. Applied to a game supported on the diagonal the
corrected transport (Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync}) forces its constant to satisfy $C \geq
2^{c-1}$, so the best it can conclude from $\synval \leq \frac12$ is $\valstar \leq 1 -
\kappa (2C)^{-1/c}$, which is never better than $\frac12$; and its diagonal-weight
hypothesis is in any case unavailable on a $k$-fold product, whose diagonal weight decays
as $\kappa^{k}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:almost-sync-hypothesis}). So the threshold has to come
from a $\valstar$ theorem, which Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q} is.

Second, the inner end does not need $\synval$ either. The adversarially verified
decomposition of the proof recorded in the companion repository
\texttt{vidick/mipre-proof} --- 123 nodes, 291 challenges all resolved --- states every
node in the bipartite tensor-product value, from the top claim to the conclusion; not one
of its nodes mentions the synchronous value. Stating this chapter's clauses in $\synval$
therefore buys nothing the sources provide and costs three things: the transport itself
(Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync}, whose printed form was false), its polynomial loss and the
factor $\kappa^{13}$ it contributed to
Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition}, and the invariant of
Remark~\ref{rem:sync-invariant}, which had to be established for three transformations and
never was. All three are now off the path. Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar} survives as the
free comparison it always was, used only to read a $\valstar$ conclusion in $\synval$ when
a synchronous statement is wanted (Theorem~\ref{thm:halting}, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}),
never in the other direction.

What this costs in turn is that the rigidity and approximate-measurement lemmas the inner
analyses consume must be stated bipartitely, as their sources state them, rather than
tracially. That is the arrangement of~\cite{JNVWY20} and of the companion repository's
paper, so it is the arrangement with the least to prove.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Question reduction: introspection}
\label{sec:ps-introspection}

Introspection removes the verifier's cost of sampling questions: instead of receiving
questions for $\verifier_{2^n}$, the players \emph{sample the questions themselves} by
measuring shared EPR pairs, and the new verifier uses the Pauli basis test
(Theorem~\ref{thm:qld}) to force honest sampling. The CL structure of the sampler is
what makes commitment to each stage of the sampled question enforceable (the ``hiding''
directions of the CL decomposition). The result is a verifier whose questions are
exponentially smaller, at the price of a fixed number of levels.

\begin{remark}[Typed verifiers, and why this blueprint has none]
\label{rem:typed-detyping}
\ledgernode{1.1.4}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc,thm:oracularization,thm:answer-reduction}
The transformations of this chapter are performed in~\cite{JNVWY20} on \emph{typed}
verifiers --- a type graph whose vertices label the roles a question can play, with the
sampler producing a pair of types and then a pair of questions --- and converted back by a
detyping lemma at the end of each. This blueprint states the untyped forms throughout,
which is why no type graph appears in Theorem~\ref{thm:oracularization} or
Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}.

That is a presentational choice with two costs a formalization will have to pay, both
recorded on ledger node $1.1.4$ so they are not forgotten. Detyping preserves the value
only up to a factor $16^{-\abs{T}}$ in the type graph's size, which is where the
``constant-factor loss'' in the soundness chains comes from; and it adds $2$ to the level
count, which is part of why the level budget is what it is. The lemma also needs the type
graph to have at least one edge --- a hypothesis every instantiation in the paper satisfies
but which the source leaves unstated --- and its soundness argument truncates over-long
answers, justified by the same answer-truncation lemma that the anchoring
construction of~\cite{BVY17} uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Turing machine conventions, and the cost model that replaces them]
\label{rem:tm-conventions}
\ledgernode{1.1.5, 1.1.5.1, 1.1.5.2, 1.1.5.3}
\uses{def:decider,def:lambda-bounded,lem:universal-tm}
The paper fixes a concrete Turing machine formalism and a \emph{timeout-counter} normal form
$B(n) = c_0 (c_1 n)^{c_2}$: a machine is wrapped so that it always halts within its bound,
and the wrapped description is canonical enough that the parameters can be read back off
it. Four ledger nodes are about that machinery, and this blueprint replaces all of it with
the cost model of Section~\ref{sec:rr-computability}
(Section~\ref{sec:departures} records the departure).

Three things in those nodes are worth keeping even so, because they are the kind of detail a
formalization rediscovers the hard way. The canonical form has to be genuinely injective in
the parameters for the timeout bound to be well defined at all, and the audit had to supply
an explicit parser to establish it --- in the source the parser's existence was asserted
rather than constructed. The overhead polynomial relating a machine's running time to its
bound needs the normalization $c_1 \geq 2$, without which the claim fails outright at
$c_1 = 1$, $n = 1$. And the whole scheme rests on the bound dominating $n$, which holds only
because the constants are positive integers. None of the three is deep; all three are the
sort of thing that stops a formalization dead.
\end{remark}


\subsubsection{The introspective verifier}

\begin{definition}[The introspective verifier]
\label{def:intro-verifier}
\ledgernode{1.2.3}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:cl-function,rem:typed-detyping,thm:qld}
Fix $\ell$ and a $\lambda$-bounded $\ell$-level verifier $\verifier$. The
\emph{introspective verifier} $\vint$ is the typed verifier whose players sample the
conditionally linear question distribution of $\verifier_{2^n}$ \emph{themselves}: the
question $y \in V = \F_q^s$ is not sent to them, but obtained by each player measuring its
half of $Q$ shared EPR pairs in a Pauli basis, and reported back. Its decider runs three
kinds of test.
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \textbf{Pauli test}, which is the game of Theorem~\ref{thm:qld} on the same EPR
  pairs, so that the measurements the players use to sample are forced to be genuine Pauli
  measurements in the low-degree-encoded basis;
\item a \textbf{sampling test}, which forces the reported $y$ to be distributed as the
  conditionally linear distribution of $\verifier_{2^n}$ rather than as anything else the
  players might prefer;
\item a \textbf{hiding test}, which forces each player not to learn the parts of the
  question that the conditional sampling should have hidden from it --- the types
  $\mathtt{Hide}_1, \ldots, \mathtt{Hide}_\ell$ exist for this, one per level.
\end{enumerate}
It is a $3$-level \emph{typed} verifier, hence $5$-level once detyped
(Remark~\ref{rem:typed-detyping}), and that $5$ is what the compression pipeline consumes.
\end{definition}

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[Complexity of the introspective verifier]
\label{lem:intro-complexity}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.1}
\uses{def:intro-verifier,def:lambda-bounded}
There are Turing machines computing the two halves of $\vint$ separately: one which on input
$(\lambda, \ell)$ outputs the sampler $\sint$ in time $\polylog(\lambda, \ell)$, with
$\TIME_{\sint}(n) = \poly(n, \lambda, \ell)$ and $\sint$ a $3$-level typed sampler; and one
which on input $(\verifier, \lambda, \ell)$ outputs the decider $\dint$ in standard
timeout-counter form, in polynomial time, with $\abs{\dint} = \poly(\lambda, \ell)$ and
timeout bound $B_{\dint}(n) = \poly(2^{\lambda n}, \ell)$. The sampler's description does not
depend on $\verifier$ at all.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[A false step in computing the hidden subspace]
\label{rem:intro-lperp}
\uses{lem:intro-complexity,def:cl-function}
The decider has to compute, for a level $k$ and a prefix $u$, the orthogonal complement
$(L^v_{k+1, u})^\perp$ of the conditionally linear map at that level. The printed procedure
computed it by taking a basis of $\ker(M)$ and forming its canonical complement --- and the
canonical complement of a basis of $\ker(M)$ is \emph{not} a basis of $\ker(M)^\perp$. Over
$\F_2^2$ the map $L(x_1, x_2) = (x_1 + x_2, 0)$ is a counterexample, found independently by
two checkers. Only the computation was wrong: the test itself is defined abstractly through
the complement, so the statement of Lemma~\ref{lem:intro-complexity} and its bound are
unaffected. It is recorded because a formalization must supply a procedure that is actually
correct, and this is the obvious place to get it wrong. The same audit closed a second seam
here, an answer-length bound of $8Q$ that only holds once the honest answer is truncated the
way oracularization and answer reduction already truncate theirs.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Completeness: two identities about Pauli measurements}

The honest strategy measures EPR pairs in the bases the sampled question prescribes, so the
completeness proof is not an approximation argument at all: it is two exact statements about
how Pauli operators interact with measurements of a linear map, and then a check that the
seven tests of the decider all pass with certainty. Both identities are finite-field linear
algebra, and both are plausible early Lean targets.

\effortMedium

\begin{lemma}[Pauli operators against a linear-map measurement]
\label{lem:pauli-linear-fourier}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.2}
\uses{def:cl-function}
Let $L : \F^k \to \F^k$ be linear, let $W \in \{X, Z\}$, and for $a$ in the range of $L$
pick $u_a$ with $L(u_a) = a$. Write $\sigma^W_{[L(\cdot) = a]}$ for the projector onto the
event that a $W$-basis measurement returns a preimage of $a$. Then for every
$v \in \ker(L)^\perp$,
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma^W(v) = \sum_{a \in \F^k} \omega^{\fieldtr(u_a \cdot v)}\, \sigma^W_{[L(\cdot) = a]},
\end{equation*}
and conversely, for every $a$ in the range of $L$,
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma^W_{[L(\cdot) = a]} = \E_{v \sim \ker(L)^\perp}\ \omega^{-\fieldtr(v \cdot u_a)}\,
    \sigma^W(v).
\end{equation*}
The choice of $u_a$ off the range of $L$ is immaterial, since there the projector vanishes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each identity is the Fourier expansion of the other over the group $\ker(L)^\perp$: the
character $v \mapsto \omega^{\fieldtr(u_a \cdot v)}$ depends only on $a = L(u_a)$, because two
preimages of $a$ differ by an element of $\ker(L)$, which the trace pairing annihilates
against $\ker(L)^\perp$.
\end{proof}

\effortEasy

\begin{lemma}[A commutation criterion for $X$ and $Z$ measurements]
\label{lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.2}
\uses{lem:pauli-linear-fourier}
Let $L, R : \F^k \to \F^k$ be linear with $\ker(R)^\perp \subseteq \ker(L)$. Then the
measurements $\{\sigma^Z_{[L(\cdot) = b]}\}_b$ and $\{\sigma^X_{[R(\cdot) = d]}\}_d$ commute.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand both families by Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-linear-fourier}. The $Z$-side expansion runs
over $v \in \ker(L)^\perp$ and the $X$-side over $w \in \ker(R)^\perp$, and
$\sigma^Z(v)$ and $\sigma^X(w)$ commute exactly when $\fieldtr(v \cdot w) = 0$. The
hypothesis gives $w \in \ker(L)$ for every such $w$, and $v \in \ker(L)^\perp$, so the pairing
vanishes term by term.
\end{proof}

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[Completeness of introspection]
\label{lem:intro-completeness}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.2}
\uses{def:intro-verifier,def:pcc,lem:pauli-linear-fourier,lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
If $\verifier_{2^n}$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, then $\vint_n$ has one: the players
measure their EPR halves in the bases the sampled question prescribes and then play the
value-$1$ strategy of $\verifier_{2^n}$ on the question they have obtained. Every test of the
decider accepts with certainty, and the resulting strategy is again projective, consistent
and commuting.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:pauli-linear-fourier,lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-linear-fourier} identifies the honest sampling measurement with a Pauli
measurement, which is what the Pauli test checks; Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
gives the commutation the hiding test needs, the hypothesis
$\ker(R)^\perp \subseteq \ker(L)$ being exactly the nesting of consecutive conditionally
linear levels. Consistency and projectivity pass through the construction because each
player's measurement is a product of commuting projective measurements.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The soundness toolkit}

Soundness needs three groups of lemmas before the induction can start, and they are
separated here because they are independent of introspection and reusable: a Pauli twirl, a
conversion from sub-measurements to measurements, and a way to condition on part of a
question.

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[The Pauli twirl]
\label{lem:pauli-twirl}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.3}
\uses{def:distance,def:q-value}
Averaging a measurement against a Pauli operator, on a state that contains EPR pairs,
replaces it by one that commutes with the Pauli basis, at a controlled cost. The group of
statements comprises: commuting with the Pauli basis implies commuting with the Pauli
observables; a mixing lemma for a linear map and one for a unitary; and a lemma combining
the two sides. The error accumulates additively through the group.
\end{lemma}

\effortMedium

\begin{lemma}[Close to a sub-measurement implies close to a measurement]
\label{lem:close-submeasurement}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.3}
\uses{def:distance}
Let $\{A^x_a\}$ be a projective measurement, let each $B^x_a$ be positive semidefinite with
$\sum_a B^x_a \leq \Id$, and let $\{C^x_a\}$ be a POVM with $C^x_a \geq B^x_a$ for all $x$
and $a$. If $A^x_a \approx_\eps B^x_a$ on average over $x$, then
$A^x_a \approx_{\sqrt{\eps}} C^x_a$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The gap $\sum_a (C^x_a - B^x_a)$ is a positive operator of small expectation, because
$\{A^x_a\}$ sums to $\Id$ and is $\eps$-close to $B$; Cauchy--Schwarz turns that bound on the
expectation into a bound on the Hilbert--Schmidt distance, which is where the square root
comes from.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conditioning on part of a question]
\label{lem:conditional-measurement}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.4}
\uses{def:distance,def:inconsistency}
On a state that factors as a question part times an answer part, a projective measurement of
the question part induces a distribution on it, and consistency or commutation statements
about the unconditioned measurements transfer to the measurements conditioned on the sampled
question. In particular, if
$\sum_{x, y, z} \bra{\psi} A^y_{x, z} \ot B_{x, y, z} \ket{\psi} \geq 1 - \delta$ with
$\{B_{x,y,z}\}$ projective, then
$\Id \ot B_{x, y, z} \approx_\delta A^y_{x, z} \ot B_{x, y}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Introspection]
\label{thm:introspection}
\ledgernode{1.2, 1.2.3.8}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:lambda-bounded,def:pcc,thm:qld,def:cl-function,def:intro-verifier,lem:intro-complexity,lem:intro-completeness,lem:intro-induction}

\subsection{Oracularization}
\label{sec:ps-oracularization}

Oracularization converts a normal form verifier into one where a single player (the
``oracle'') receives both original questions and answers both, while the other player
receives one of the two questions and must answer consistently. This makes the game's
predicate a pointwise function of one player's answer --- the format required by answer
reduction. This is probably the easiest transformation in the paper and could be attempted first to fix the semantics. 

\begin{theorem}[Oracularization]
\label{thm:oracularization}
\ledgernode{1.3.5}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc,def:sync-strategy}
There is a polynomial-time Turing machine $\ComputeOracleVerifier$ mapping a normal
form verifier $\verifier = (\sampler, \decider)$ to a normal form verifier $\vora$
such that, for some $\delta(\eps) = O(\sqrt{\eps})$ and all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy then
  $\vora_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, in which moreover both players measure with the
  same family of operators.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) If $\valstar(\vora_n) > 1 - \eps$ then
  $\valstar(\verifier_n) \geq 1 - \delta(\eps)$.
\item (\textbf{Complexity}) The sampler of $\vora$ depends only on $\sampler$, with
  $\TIME$ within a constant factor and the same number of levels.
\end{enumerate}
The oracularized decider's preamble performs a type check, a question-length check, and a
bounded parse of each answer component against the decider's timeout bound $B_\decider(n)$;
answers failing the parse are grouped into one distinguished outcome, so the oracle
families are projective measurements and not sub-measurements.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[What the audit changed in oracularization]
\label{rem:oracularization-repairs}
\ledgernode{1.3.5}
\uses{thm:oracularization,def:pcc,def:sync-strategy,rem:identical-operators}
Node $1.3.5$ was added late --- round 12 of the campaign found that
\texttt{oracularization.tex} was covered by no ledger node at all, although both of its
theorems are consumed by answer reduction --- and it carries four repairs that the
statement above has been written to respect.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The soundness constant $\delta_\ora$ carries \emph{one square root}: the step from a
  $1 - O(\eps)$ measurement-closeness to a $1 - O(\sqrt{\eps})$ statement about the value
  is an application of \cite[Fact 4.31]{NW19}, not an identity. An earlier revision of this
  blueprint wrote $\delta(\eps) = \poly(\eps)$, which is not merely imprecise but false as
  stated: $\sqrt{\eps}$ is not a polynomial in $\eps$.
\item The completeness witness's extra property is \emph{identical measurement operators},
  not symmetry of the state. The campaign found the claim that the constructed strategy is
  symmetric ``by construction'' unjustified --- the hypothesis supplies a value-$1$ PCC
  strategy whose state is reused unchanged, and no lemma in the paper symmetrizes such a
  strategy while preserving projectivity, consistency and value $1$. SPCC was withdrawn
  from the completeness clause in consequence. In the synchronous framework this is exactly
  what is free (Remark~\ref{rem:identical-operators}).
\item The oracle families are genuine projective measurements. Outcomes failing the
  decider's bounded parse are grouped into a distinguished outcome; leaving that implicit
  made them sub-measurements, which the later sandwiching argument may not use.
\item The truncation of answers to $B_\decider(n)$ symbols is justified by a corollary of
  the timeout-counter form, not by the assertion that a timeout-form decider inspects at
  most $B_\decider(n)$ symbols. Nothing forces $B_\decider(n)$ to exceed the time bound
  $T$ against which the PCP decodes, so the truncation is load-bearing and a formalization
  must carry it explicitly.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Answer reduction}
\label{sec:ps-answer-reduction}

After introspection the decider still runs in time exponential in $n$ (it simulates
$\decider$ at index $2^n$), and the players' answers are correspondingly long. Answer
reduction composes the game with a PCP: by Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} the
statement ``the original decider would have accepted our answers'' is a succinct 3SAT
instance; the players commit to a low-degree encoding of a satisfying assignment
(their original answers plus the computation tableau) and the new decider spot-checks
it, using the low individual degree test to certify the encoding and
Lemma~\ref{lem:schwartz-zippel} for the arithmetization. Answers and decider time
become polylogarithmic in the original.

\begin{lemma}[Succinct decoupled 5SAT]
\label{lem:decoupled-5sat}
\ledgernode{1.3.2, 1.3.2.1, 1.3.2.2, 1.3.2.3}
\uses{thm:succinct-sat,def:succinct,def:decider}
The succinct 3SAT instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} can be converted, in time
polynomial in $n$, $\log T$, $Q$ and $\sigma$, into a \emph{decoupled} 5SAT instance: the
variables are partitioned into five blocks, two of which are the players' answer strings
$a$ and $b$, and every clause touches each block at most once. The conversion preserves
satisfiability in both directions, and both the explicit and the padded succinct
descriptions of the result are computable within the same bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Decoupling is a syntactic transformation: a clause touching one block twice is split by
introducing a fresh variable in a new block together with an equality clause. Satisfiability
in both directions and the block discipline for every clause of the image are checked
clause by clause. The two description layers are separate obligations --- the explicit
description fixes the circuit sizes and input formats, and the padded one fixes the padding
conventions and index arithmetic that the PCP layer and the composition consume.
\end{proof}

\effortHard

\begin{theorem}[The bespoke PCP for deciders]
\label{thm:pcp-decider}
\ledgernode{1.3.3}
\uses{lem:decoupled-5sat,lem:schwartz-zippel,thm:qld,def:decider}
There is a verifier $\pcpverifier$ which, given a valid decider specification $(\decider,
n, T, Q, \sigma, x, y)$ --- validity requiring $\abs{\decider} \leq \sigma$, $\max\{Q,
2\lceil \log n \rceil\} \leq T$ \emph{and} $\abs{x}, \abs{y} \leq Q$, as exact inequalities
--- together with a PCP view $(z, \Xi)$, runs in time $\poly(\log T, \log n, Q, \sigma)$
and satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) if $\decider$ accepts $(n, x, y, a, b)$ within $T$ steps,
  then the low-degree proof with $g_1, g_2$ the low-degree encodings of the padded answers
  is accepted with probability $1$ over uniform $z$;
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) any low-degree proof accepted with probability greater than
  $\frac12$ decodes to strings whose prefixes $\decider$ accepts within $T$ steps.
\end{enumerate}
Here the number of variables $m$ of the arithmetization must be a power of two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Low-degree tests on the committed polynomials, sum-check-style zero-testing of the
constraint polynomial against a zero basis, and Lemma~\ref{lem:schwartz-zippel} for the
arithmetization. Four obligations are separate in the source and stay separate here: the
proof format and query structure, completeness, the zero-testing argument with its error
bounds, and the complexity bound the composition consumes.
\end{proof}

The theorem has four obligations, and they are separate in the source because they fail
separately.

\begin{remark}[Why $m$ is a power of two]
\label{rem:pcp-power-of-two}
\uses{thm:pcp-decider,thm:qld}
The hypothesis is not cosmetic and was a confirmed defect in the source. The $m$-variate
low-degree test needs $m \mid q$, and $q$ is a power of two, so $m$ must be one as well.
The padded description guarantees this only for the \emph{outer} variable count $m' = 5m +
5 + s$; the inner $m$, inherited from the succinct-SAT reduction, carries no such
guarantee, which leaves the conditionally linear functions of the axis-parallel and
diagonal lines ill defined and the applications of low-degree soundness with an unverified
hypothesis. The repair is to round $m$ up to a power of two in the padding step, which
costs nothing asymptotically. A formalization must state it as a hypothesis, as
Theorem~\ref{thm:pcp-decider} does.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Answer reduction]
\label{thm:answer-reduction}
\ledgernode{1.3, 1.3.4}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc,thm:succinct-sat,lem:decoupled-5sat,thm:pcp-decider,lem:schwartz-zippel,thm:qld,lem:self-dual-basis,thm:oracularization}

\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy

\end{enumerate}

This is the most technically heterogeneous section of the paper

\begin{remark}[The composition, and what the value form does not inherit]
\label{rem:ar-composition}
\ledgernode{1.3.4}
\uses{thm:answer-reduction,thm:oracularization,thm:pcp-decider,def:ent,rem:compression-chain}
The composition of the oracularized verifier with $\pcpverifier$ --- questions append
conditionally-linearly sampled PCP evaluation points to the oracularized questions, so the
sampler stays conditionally linear, and answers are PCP evaluations --- is ledger node

local: misnamed objects, a wrong exponent, an averaging omitted from a display, an
undefined sampler symbol. Four are structural, and one of those does not reach this
blueprint at all.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The ambient space of the answer-reduced sampler is declared as a direct sum of the

  conditionally linear functions are $\Fq$-linear, so the decomposition needs stating over

\item The success probability fed into low-degree soundness omits the consistency subtest,

\item Claim ar-4 omits the word \emph{projective} for the simultaneous family, and the

\subsection{Gap amplification: parallel repetition}
\label{sec:ps-parallel-repetition}

Answer reduction leaves a shrunken gap ($1$ vs.\ $1 - 1/\poly(n)$). Direct parallel
repetition (Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}, formalized end to end) restores
soundness
$\frac12$ with polynomially many repetitions, at polynomial cost in complexity and without
anchoring; the anchored, entanglement-form theorem used in~\cite{JNVWY20}
(\cite{BVY17}) is not needed (Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored}).

\begin{theorem}[Parallel repetition of normal form verifiers]
\label{thm:parallel-repetition}
\ledgernode{1.4, 1.4.3, 1.4.3.1}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc,def:direct-repetition,thm:direct-repetition-q,def:mipstar}
There are universal constants $b, c > 0$ such that the following holds for every
$\kappa \in (0,1]$. There are a constant $c' > 0$, depending only on $\kappa$, and a
polynomial-time Turing machine $\ComputeParrepVerifier$ that on input
$(\verifier, \lambda, \ell, \tau)$, for $\verifier$ an $\ell$-level normal form verifier,
outputs an $\ell$-level normal form verifier $\vrep$ whose game $\vrep_n$ is the
$k(n)$-fold direct repetition (Definition~\ref{def:direct-repetition}) of $\verifier_n$
--- up to the encoding of answer tuples as strings, the decider parsing a self-delimiting
$k(n)$-tuple and rejecting malformed answers --- with $k(n) = (\lambda n)^{(1 + c')\tau}$,
$\TIME_{\srep}(n) = O(k(n) \TIME_\sampler(n))$ and $\TIME_{\drep}(n) = O\bigl(k(n)
\max(\TIME_\decider(n), (\lambda n)^\tau)\bigr)$, the sampler of $\vrep$ depending only on
$(\sampler, \lambda, \ell, \tau)$, and for all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy and
  $\TIME_\decider(n) \leq (\lambda n)^\tau$, then $\vrep_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC
  strategy.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) For all $\eps > 0$, if $\valstar(\verifier_n) \leq 1 - \eps$
  and $\TIME_\decider(n) \leq (\lambda n)^\tau$, then
  $\valstar(\vrep_n) \leq \exp\bigl( -c\, \eps^{13}\, k(n) / (\lambda n)^{\tau} \bigr)$;
  in particular $\valstar(\vrep_n) \leq \frac12$ whenever $\eps \geq (\lambda
  n)^{-\tau}$, for $c'$ large enough and $n \geq 2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The game $\vrep_n$ is $\verifier_n^{k(n)}$: its sampler runs $k(n)$ independent copies of
$\sampler$ (a product of conditionally linear functions of the same level) and its
decider runs $\decider$ on every coordinate. Completeness: the $k(n)$-fold tensor power of
a value-$1$ PCC strategy is a value-$1$ PCC strategy for the repeated game. Soundness is
now a direct application of Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}: the hypothesis is
already a $\valstar$ bound, so nothing has to be transported first. That theorem bounds
$\valstar(\verifier_n^{k(n)})$ by $\exp\bigl( -c\, k(n)\, \eps^{13} / (\eps +
\log(\abs{\mA}\abs{\mB})) \bigr)$, and the answer alphabets of $\verifier_n$ have size at
most $2^{\TIME_\decider(n)}$, so $\log(\abs{\mA}\abs{\mB}) \leq 2 (\lambda n)^\tau$ and
the denominator is $O((\lambda n)^\tau)$; with $\eps \geq (\lambda n)^{-\tau}$ and $k(n) =
(\lambda n)^{(1 + c')\tau}$ the exponent is at least $(\lambda n)^{(c' - 13)\tau}$, so
$c' > 13$ suffices. The exponent $13$ is the one of
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q} itself, with no further loss: carrying the
hypothesis in $\valstar$ rather than $\synval$ removes both the transport's polynomial
loss and the factor $\kappa^{13}$ that its diagonal-weight hypothesis used to contribute.
The conclusion is in $\valstar$, as is the hypothesis: that is the form
Definition~\ref{def:mipstar} asks of the final game, and it is available at every step
because nothing in the chain ever leaves $\valstar$ (Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}).
The exponent $b$ and the constants are provisional (Section~\ref{sec:conventions}).

\subsection{The compression theorem}
\label{sec:ps-compression}

Composing the four transformations --- introspection, oracularization, answer
reduction, repetition --- yields gap-preserving compression: the paper-level form of
the compression hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}. The soundness
clause is the value-form clause that~\cite{JNVWY20} state first and then strengthen to
an entanglement bound (Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}).

\begin{theorem}[Gap-preserving compression]
\label{thm:compression}
\ledgernode{1.5, 1.5.7}
\uses{thm:introspection,thm:oracularization,thm:answer-reduction,thm:parallel-repetition,def:lambda-bounded,def:pcc,def:normal-verifier}
\lean{MIPRE.GapCompression}
\leanok
There is a universal constant $C_0 > 0$ and a polynomial-time Turing machine
$\Compress$ that on input $(\verifier, \lambda)$ outputs a $7$-level normal form
verifier $\vcompr = (\scompr, \dcompr)$ with $\TIME_{\scompr}(n),
\TIME_{\dcompr}(n) = \poly(n, \lambda)$, where $\scompr$ is independent of
$\verifier$ and computable from $\lambda$ in time $\polylog(\lambda)$. If moreover
$\verifier$ is a $\lambda$-bounded $7$-level normal form verifier, then for all $n
\geq C_0$ and $N = 2^n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_N$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, then so
  does $\vcompr_n$.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) If $\valstar(\verifier_N) \leq \tfrac12$ then
  $\valstar(\vcompr_n) \leq \tfrac12$: the class ``$\valstar \leq \tfrac12$'' is
  preserved, in the same value at both ends.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The level count is forced by the chain rather than chosen: introspection outputs a
$5$-level verifier whatever the level of its input (Theorem~\ref{thm:introspection}),
oracularization preserves the level (Theorem~\ref{thm:oracularization}), answer reduction
takes $\ell$ to $\max\{\ell + 2, 5\}$, here $7$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}), and
direct repetition preserves it (Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition}); so $\Compress$
outputs a $7$-level verifier on every input, and $7$ is consequently the level at which
the recursion closes. A verifier of fewer levels is presented at that level by padding its
decomposition with trivial factors, which an $\ell$-level conditionally linear function
(Definition~\ref{def:cl-function}) admits. In~\cite{JNVWY20} the count is $9$, anchored
repetition adding two levels (Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored}).

\paragraph{Lean statement.} The theorem is the structure \texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression}: a
record of the constant $C_0$; the compressed sampler $\lambda \mapsto \scompr_\lambda$, a
valid $7$-level sampler for every $\lambda$, with a polynomial-time program computing its
description from the binary representation of $\lambda$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-sampler-indep});
the polynomial-time map $\Compress$ from pairs of programs and $\lambda$ to the program of
the compressed decider; the packaging of every output, well-formed input or not, as a
$7$-level normal form verifier with that sampler and that decider; the bound $\poly(n,
\lambda)$ on the running times of the output at index $n$, at the degree \texttt{deg} in the
size of the input that Definition~\ref{def:decider} carries, the same bound on the dimension of the compressed
sampler (in the paper a consequence of its running time) and on the length of the answers
the compressed decider can accept (\texttt{output\_rejects\_long}: the paper's repeated
decider accepts only after fully parsing the answers, so an answer longer than its time bound
forces a timeout, the property the amended proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dhalt-values} relies on;
in the ambient model, where running times scale with the input, it is a demand on the
construction, an explicit length check); and the completeness and soundness clauses for
$\lambda$-bounded $7$-level inputs at $n \geq C_0$. An instance of the structure is the
theorem; the pipeline of this chapter is its proof, and the halting reduction consumes an
instance (Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}). Two readings are fixed there.
$\verifier_N$ is taken with answers of length at most $N^\lambda$, the bound that
$\lambda$-boundedness puts on the running time of its decider, and $\vcompr_n$ with answers
of length at most the stated running-time bound --- beyond which its decider rejects, so
that its value is the same with any larger alphabet
(\texttt{Verifier.valStar\_eq\_of\_rejects}) --- since the exact running times of
Definition~\ref{def:normal-verifier} are not functions of the verifier in the
formalization. And ``a value-$1$ PCC strategy'' is a PCC synchronous strategy of value $1$
for the game read as synchronous (\texttt{Verifier.HasPerfectPCC}), which presupposes that
the decider rejects unequal answers to equal questions at that index, as the deciders of
the pipeline do.

The four stages are chained through a parameter budget rather than composed blindly:
each stage's soundness clause is applied contrapositively, and each application needs a
strict margin between the loss it suffers and the gap it is handed. Those margins are
arithmetic facts about the imported constants, isolated here as
Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-margin} and Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-tau}. The provenance notes
name the corresponding nodes of the companion repository's ledger
(\texttt{vidick/mipre-proof}); see \texttt{planning/ledger-informed-plan.md}.

\begin{lemma}[The compressed sampler does not depend on the verifier]
\label{lem:compress-sampler-indep}
\ledgernode{1.5.1}
\uses{thm:compression,def:normal-verifier,def:cl-function}
\lean{MIPRE.GapCompression}
\leanok
For all inputs $(\verifier, \lambda)$ --- $\lambda$-bounded or not, well-formed or not
--- the sampler $\scompr$ of $\Compress(\verifier, \lambda)$ depends only on $\lambda$,
and there is a Turing machine $\mathtt{ComputeSampler}$ that outputs its description from
$\lambda$ in time $\polylog(\lambda)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Chain the verifier-independence of the sampler through the three stages: introspection's
sampler depends only on $(\lambda, \ell)$, answer reduction's only on its input sampler
and the parameters, and repetition's only on its input sampler and $(\lambda, \ell,
\tau)$. $\mathtt{ComputeSampler}$ is then realized by running $\Compress$ on the trivial
verifier. It is this lemma that makes the recursion of Section~\ref{sec:ps-halting}
possible at all: a fixed point has to quote its own sampler, so the sampler must not
depend on what is being compressed. In Lean the lemma is built into the statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}: the fields \texttt{sampler} and \texttt{samplerProg} of
\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression} are the map $\lambda \mapsto \scompr_\lambda$ and its
polynomial-time description, and \texttt{output\_sampler} says that every output carries it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The margin claim]
\label{lem:compress-margin}
\ledgernode{1.5.3}
\uses{thm:introspection,thm:answer-reduction}
Write $(a_1, b_1)$ for the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:introspection} and $(a_2, b_2)$
for those of Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}, normalized so that $a_1, a_2 \geq 1$.
Put $\sigma = \lceil C_{\mathrm{intro}} \lambda^{C_{\mathrm{intro}}} \rceil$ and
\begin{equation*}
  \mu = \Bigl\lceil \max\Bigl\{ C_{\mathrm{intro}},\ \frac{9 a_1 + 2 a_2
  C_{\mathrm{intro}}}{b_1 b_2} \Bigr\} \Bigr\rceil .
\end{equation*}
Then $\mu$ is a universal constant, $\mu \geq C_{\mathrm{intro}}$, and
\begin{equation*}
  (\lambda n)^{-b_2 \mu} \;<\; \frac{\eps_1}{2 \sigma^{a_2}}
  \qquad \text{for all } n \geq 2 \text{ and all } \lambda \geq 1,
\end{equation*}
where $\eps_1 = \bigl( 8 a_1 (\lambda n)^{a_1} \bigr)^{-1/b_1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[A universal repetition exponent]
\label{lem:compress-tau}
\ledgernode{1.5.5}
\uses{thm:parallel-repetition,thm:answer-reduction}
There is a universal integer $\tau$ such that, with $k(n) = (\lambda n)^{\tau}$ and
$\eps_2$ the function of $(n, \lambda)$ fixed by the answer-reduction stage, the
repetition bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition} is below $\frac12$ for all $n
\geq \tau$ and all integers $\lambda \geq 1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$1/\eps_2$ is bounded by a fixed polynomial in $\lambda n$, because $\eps_1$, $\sigma$
and $\mu$ are, so $\eps_2^{13} \geq (\lambda n)^{-P}$ for a universal $P$; choosing $\tau$
larger than $P$ plus the answer-reduction exponent, with slack for the constants, makes
the exponent of the bound grow as a positive fixed power of $\lambda n$. Purely
arithmetic, and computationally testable --- the companion repository tests it.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[How the value chain composes, and what it avoids]
\label{rem:compression-chain}
\ledgernode{1.5.8, 1.5.2, 1.5.4, 1.5.6}
\uses{thm:compression,thm:introspection,thm:oracularization,thm:answer-reduction,thm:parallel-repetition,def:ent,lem:compress-margin,lem:compress-tau}
The soundness clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} is obtained by applying each
transformation's soundness clause contrapositively, with a margin at every step. From
$\valstar(\verifier_N) \leq \frac12$ and $\delta_1(\eps_1, n) < \frac12$: were
$\valstar(\verifier^{(1)}_n) > 1 - \eps_1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:introspection} would give
$\valstar(\verifier_N) \geq 1 - \delta_1 > \frac12$, so
$\valstar(\verifier^{(1)}_n) \leq 1 - \eps_1$. The same step through
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracularization} and then Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}, using
$\delta_2(\eps_2, n) < \eps_1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-margin}), gives
$\valstar(\verifier^{(2)}_n) \leq 1 - \eps_2$; and
Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition} with Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-tau} brings that below
$\frac12$.

Every step of that chain is an implication between $\valstar$ bounds, which is worth
recording because the route of~\cite{JNVWY20} cannot take it. There the repetition step's
soundness hypothesis is an \emph{entanglement} bound, and $\Ent(\game, 1 - \eps) = \infty$
needs $\valstar(\game) < 1 - \eps$ \emph{strictly} --- a non-strict $\leq$ leaves the value
possibly attained and the entanglement requirement finite. So the paper runs the whole
chain in $\Ent$, starting it at the threshold $1 - \delta_1$ rather than at $\frac12$; the
value-form route would force it to apply repetition at $\eps_2/2$ instead of $\eps_2$, at a
cost of $2^{17}$ in the exponent of its repetition parameter. Direct repetition
(Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}) takes a value hypothesis and so needs no
strictness and pays no such factor: this is the second place, after
Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored}, where the value-form pipeline is cheaper than the
entanglement-form one rather than merely equivalent to it. It is also why no entanglement
lower bound has to be tracked anywhere in this chapter.
\end{remark}

Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} instantiates the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}: take $A$ to be the set of (descriptions of) normal
form verifier games with a value-$1$ PCC strategy and $B$ the set of those with $\valstar
\leq \frac12$, with the trivially accepting and the trivially rejecting game as the
distinguished elements; the complement of $B$ is semidecided by enumerating tensor-product
strategies, the $\valstar$ half of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}; and a description
$(\sampler, \decider, n)$ with $n$ in binary is a succinct description of the game
$\verifier_{2^n}$ played by a $\lambda$-bounded verifier. Completeness of compression is
the preservation of $A$; preservation of $B$ is the soundness clause of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} verbatim, hypothesis and conclusion being in the same value.
Taking $B$ in $\valstar$ throughout is what keeps Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync} out of the
pipeline altogether --- at the outer end its hypothesis is unavailable
(Remark~\ref{rem:almost-sync-hypothesis}) and at the inner end it is simply not needed
(Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}); it is also the form in which the semidecision
procedure is available, the rounding argument for the $\synval$ half not being the same
one. Making this dictionary precise is a
formalization task of this section. The four obligations recorded here from the referee
report (R1) --- the universe of the criterion's strings, compression from a
\emph{succinct} input, preservation on inputs that are not $\lambda$-bounded descriptions,
and the choice of $\lambda$ along the recursion --- are settled as follows, against the
per-level form of the lemma (\texttt{compressibility\_criterion\_levels},
Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}), whose classes $A_n, B_n$ are indexed by the level
of the recursion.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Strings are descriptions, never games.} If they were games, $\compr$ could not
  be polynomial-time, a game of $\verifier_{2^n}$ having $2^{\poly(n, \lambda)}$
  questions. A string is a pair $(\lambda, \decider)$ of a parameter and a decider
  program, read as the verifier $(\scompr_\lambda, \decider)$ with the compressed sampler
  of Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-sampler-indep}, the length check of
  Definition~\ref{def:normal-verifier} being enforced by a wrapper around $\decider$; so
  every string names a normal form verifier, and the interpretation sits outside every
  time bound. $A_n$ is the set of strings whose verifier is $n$-bounded and whose $n$-th
  game has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, $B_n$ the set of those that are $n$-bounded with
  $\valstar \leq \frac12$ at index $n$ (\texttt{Verifier.InClassA},
  \texttt{InClassB}; disjoint, since a perfect PCC strategy gives $\valstar = 1$). Only the
  length check has to go into the wrapper: synchronicity at an index and the answer-length
  bound are conditions on membership in these classes, not on well-formedness, and the
  paper's timeout counter is not needed either --- a decider that fails to halt simply fails
  to be $n$-bounded, and its string then lies in neither class. Tying the bound $\lambda = n$ to the level is what
  replaces the choice of $\lambda$ along the recursion (Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda}): the levels
  $r(e) = 2^{K + 1 + \abs{e}}$ at which the recursion runs are exponential in $\abs{e}$,
  above every polynomial in $\abs{e}$ that the paper's $\lambda$ has to dominate.
\item \emph{Compression from a succinct input.} The compressor cannot read the string it
  is handed: its time is polynomial in the length of $(\hat x, n)$, hence polylogarithmic
  in $n$, and a single bit query to $\hat x$ costs up to $n$. On $(\hat x, n)$ it
  therefore outputs the string $(\lambda(n), \decider_{\hat x})$, where $\lambda(n) =
  \poly(n)$ is a fixed formula and $\decider_{\hat x}$ is the decider that, at its own
  index $N$, reads $x = (\lambda', \decider')$ from $\hat x$ by bit queries (at most $2n +
  O(1)$ of them, rejecting otherwise), forms the verifier $\verifier'$ of $x$ \emph{frozen
  at index $2n + 1$} (\texttt{Verifier.freeze}: a program that replaces the index in its
  input by the frozen one, so that its sampler and decider answer at every index as
  $\verifier'$ does at index $2n + 1$, and its game at index $2^n$ is the game of $x$ at
  level $2n + 1$ --- \texttt{freeze\_valStar}, \texttt{freeze\_hasPerfectPCC},
  with $\lambda$-boundedness transferred by \texttt{freeze\_isBounded}; the answer-length
  bound is immaterial above the length the decider rejects,
  \texttt{Verifier.valStar\_eq\_of\_rejects}, \texttt{hasPerfectPCC\_of\_le}) --- runs
  $\Compress(\verifier', \lambda(n))$, and finally the compressed decider at index $N$. The
  freezing reconciles the recursion's levels $n \to 2n + 1$ with the compression theorem's
  $n \leftarrow 2^n$, at the cost of a logarithmic factor in $\lambda(n)$.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} at index $n \geq C_0$ then transfers the class of $x$ at
  level $2n + 1$ to the class of the output at level $n$, and the output is $n$-bounded
  for $n$ above a universal threshold: its size is $\abs{\hat x} + O(\log n) \leq n$ by the
  slack $2\abs{\hat x} \leq n$ of the lemma, and its time at index $N$ is $\poly(N, n) \leq
  N^n$.
\item \emph{Junk inputs.} Nothing is demanded of the compressor off strings in $A_{2n+1}
  \cup B_{2n+1}$, every string names a verifier, and $\lambda$-boundedness is decided
  nowhere. What the lemma does demand is that the complement of $B_n$ be semidecidable:
  ``not $n$-bounded'' is witnessed by a run exceeding its budget --- a search over inputs, so
  $\Sigma_1$ --- and ``$\valstar > \frac12$ at index $n$'' is the $\valstar$ half of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx} applied to the tabulated game, which is computable from
  an $n$-bounded verifier. Neither disjunct decides $n$-boundedness, which is $\Pi_1$; the
  point of taking the disjunction is that it does not have to
  (\texttt{Verifier.not\_inClassB\_iff}). The tabulation itself is read with the answer
  alphabet enumerated by \texttt{Verifier.answerEquiv} and the questions by
  \texttt{questionEquiv}, and a description matching $\verifier_n$ along those indexings has
  the same value (\texttt{quantumValue\_toGame\_eq\_valStar}), which is what carries the
  verdict of a procedure that runs on game descriptions back to the verifier.
\item \emph{The choice of $\lambda$.} Absorbed into item 1.
\end{enumerate}
Of this dictionary the per-level lemma, the Lean statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} as a hypothesis (\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression}) and the
value bookkeeping are in Lean: the quantum value is invariant under relabelings of the
alphabets (\texttt{quantumValue\_eq\_of\_equiv}, the tool for the tabulation) and under
always-rejected extra answers (\texttt{quantumValue\_extendAnswers}, by zero extension one

acceptance at an index determines $\valstar$ and the perfect PCC strategies
(\texttt{Verifier.valStar\_congr}, \texttt{hasPerfectPCC\_congr}), which survive answer

\begin{remark}[The entanglement form of~\cite{JNVWY20}]
\label{rem:entanglement-form}
\uses{def:ent,lem:recursive-compression}
The paper states the soundness of each transformation with an additional entanglement
clause, obtained from the same proofs by tracking the dimension of the extracted
strategies: $\Ent(\vint_n, 1 - \eps) \geq \max\{\Ent(\verifier_{2^n}, 1 -
\delta(\eps, n)),\ (1 - \delta(\eps, n))\, 2^{2^{\lambda n}}\}$ for introspection;
$\Ent(\vora_n, 1 - \eps) \geq \Ent(\verifier_n, 1 - \delta(\eps))$ for
oracularization; $\Ent(\vans_n, 1 - \eps) \geq \frac12 \Ent(\verifier_n, 1 - \delta(\eps, n))$
for answer reduction, the factor $\frac12$ being the Schmidt-rank doubling of the
symmetrization step, which the synchronous framework of this blueprint does not perform
(symmetry being definitional there) and therefore does not pay; the anchored repetition theorem of~\cite{BVY17}; and, composed,
$\Ent(\vcompr_n, \frac12) \geq \frac12 \max\{\Ent(\verifier_N, \frac12),\
2^{N^\lambda - 1}\}$ for compression. Fed into the variant of
Lemma~\ref{lem:recursive-compression} tolerating the factor $\frac12$ (harmless, since
the second term grows much faster than the levels of the recursion), with $f =
\Ent(\cdot, \frac12)$, this yields Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} with the stronger
conclusion $\Ent(\game_\machine, \frac12) = \infty$ for non-halting $\machine$. The
value form adopted here needs none of it; the entanglement clauses remain of interest for
the corollaries of Remark~\ref{rem:further}.

One proviso on the measure: the clauses above are transcribed from~\cite{JNVWY20}, where
the quantity is the least local dimension of a \emph{bipartite} tensor-product strategy,
whereas $\Ent$ of Definition~\ref{def:ent} is the least dimension of a \emph{synchronous}
one. Relating the two is the same transport as
Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync} and carries its hypothesis
(Remark~\ref{rem:almost-sync-hypothesis}), so the clauses should be read in the paper's
measure until that is done.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The self-referential decider is well defined]
\label{lem:halt-construction}

\begin{lemma}[A uniform bound on the verifier's parameter]
\label{lem:lambda-bound}

\begin{lemma}[The parameter of the halting verifier]
\label{lem:lambda}
\ledgernode{1.6.5}
\uses{lem:halt-construction,lem:lambda-bound,def:lambda-bounded}
There is a $\lambda$, computable from the description of $\machine$ in polynomial time and
of size $\poly(\abs{\machine})$, such that $\vhalt$ is $\lambda$-bounded and
$\TIME_{\dhalt}(n), \TIME_{\shalt}(n) = \poly(n, \abs{\machine})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Values of the halting verifier]
\label{lem:dhalt-values}
\ledgernode{1.6.2, 1.6.3}
\uses{lem:halt-construction,lem:lambda,lem:compress-sampler-indep,def:pcc,def:q-value}
For every $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $\machine$ halts within $n$ steps then $\valstar(\vhalt_n) = 1$, witnessed by a
  value-$1$ PCC strategy;
\item if $\machine$ does not halt within $n$ steps then $\vhalt_n$ and $\vcompr_n$ are the
  same game up to an identification of the answer alphabets; in particular
  $\valstar(\vhalt_n) = \valstar(\vcompr_n)$, and $\vhalt_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy
  if and only if $\vcompr_n$ does.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Item 1 is the fixed-answer strategy: when $\machine$ halts within $n$ steps, $\dhalt$
accepts every answer pair on every question pair in the support, so any strategy has value
$1$ and the constant-$0$ strategy is PCC. For item 2, $\dhalt$ at index $n$ performs the
same question-length check as $\dcompr$ and then runs $\dcompr(n, x, y, a, b)$ verbatim,
with $\mathtt{TIMEOUT}$ rejecting in both, so the two deciders accept on exactly the same
inputs; and the two verifiers share the sampler $\shalt = \mathtt{ComputeSampler}(\lambda)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-sampler-indep}), so a strategy for either is a strategy for the
other with the same winning probability. The timeout counter of $\dhalt$ does not
interfere: its bound is chosen in Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda} so that the computations above
finish first.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Item 2 is a transfer, not a bound]
\label{rem:dhalt-transfer}
\uses{lem:dhalt-values,thm:halting,thm:compression}
Item 2 is deliberately not ``$\valstar(\vhalt_n) \leq \frac12$''. That per-level bound is
false: if $\machine$ halts for the first time at step $T$, then for every $n < T$ it does
not halt within $n$ steps, yet $\valstar(\vhalt_n) = 1$ --- item 1 gives value $1$ at every
level $\geq T$, and item 2 together with the completeness clause of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} carries it \emph{downward} from level $2^n$ to level $n$,
which is exactly how the completeness half of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} is proved. The
absolute bound needs $\machine$ never to halt, and is then the conclusion of the recursion
in Theorem~\ref{thm:halting}, not an ingredient of it. Recorded here because an earlier
revision of this chapter did state item 2 as the bound, and because the ledger node
`1.6.3' states it that way too; the formalization should follow the paper.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Halting reduction]
\label{thm:halting}
\ledgernode{1.6, 1.6.6}
\uses{thm:compression,rem:compression-abstract,lem:compressible-criterion,lem:value-lower-approx,lem:kleene,def:mipstar,def:game-description,lem:sync-le-valstar,lem:dhalt-values,lem:lambda}
\lean{MIPRE.Halting.halting_reduction}
There is a computable (indeed polynomial-time) map $\machine \mapsto
\game_{\machine}$ from Turing machines to synchronous game descriptions, with games
given by normal form verifiers satisfying the efficiency requirements of
Definition~\ref{def:mipstar}, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $\machine$ halts on the empty input then $\game_\machine$ has a value-$1$ PCC
  strategy, so $\synval(\game_\machine) = \valstar(\game_\machine) = 1$;
\item if $\machine$ does not halt on the empty input then
  $\valstar(\game_\machine) \leq \tfrac12$, and hence also
  $\synval(\game_\machine) \leq \tfrac12$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof is Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion} applied to the instantiation of
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}; the semidecidability hypothesis of the lemma is the
$\valstar$ half of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}. The obligations of that
instantiation that are open are inherited here: the four listed in
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}. The synchronization invariant that an earlier
revision also required of the games produced inside $\Compress$ is no longer among them
(Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}).

In Lean the assembly is \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.halting\_reduction}. It is complete and free
of \texttt{sorry}, and it takes three hypotheses: a \texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression} (which is
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}), a universal machine, and an inhabitant of
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.Obligations}, whose four fields are exactly what
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract} leaves owed: the two distinguished strings, the
tabulation with its value agreement, the semidecider, and the compressor. So what is open is
a structure to be inhabited rather than an argument to be found, and
\texttt{\#print axioms} cannot hide any of it.
Two things are deliberately not in that statement, and neither belongs to the instantiation.
It concludes in $\valstar$ only: item 1's $\synval = 1$ needs a \emph{synchronous} strategy to
transport along the relabelings of the tabulation, for which there is no counterpart of
\texttt{quantumValue\_eq\_of\_equiv} yet, and the passage to
\texttt{HaltingGameValue.halting\_reduces\_to\_gameValue} additionally needs
\texttt{MIPRE.syncValue} related to \texttt{HaltingGameValue.gameValue}, those being parallel
developments (Section~\ref{ch:main-theorem}). Soundness needs neither, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar}.

\begin{remark}[Identical measurement operators]
\label{rem:identical-operators}
\ledgernode{1.6.7}
\uses{thm:halting,def:sync-strategy,def:pcc,thm:separation}
One property the synchronous framework gets for nothing is worth recording. The value-$1$
witness of item 1 is a PCC, hence synchronous, strategy: a \emph{single} family of
measurements used by both players. In the bipartite framework of~\cite{JNVWY20} that the
two players measure identically is an extra property, established by tracking it through
oracularization, answer reduction, detyping and repetition, and the verification
campaign~\cite{Audit26} reports that the chain is delicate, since the completeness clause
of compression carries only PCC and the property has to be read off the last application
rather than carried by the induction. Here it holds by definition
(Definition~\ref{def:sync-strategy}), so no such chain is needed --- which is also what
makes the commuting completeness of Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} a question about one
transformation at a time rather than about a provenance argument. The entanglement form of the statement, with the
stronger conclusion $\Ent(\game_\machine, \frac12) = \infty$ for non-halting
$\machine$, is discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}.
\end{remark}
