\section{Structure of the proof}
\label{ch:proof-structure}

This chapter records the main transformations of~\cite{JNVWY20} at headline level: for
each, what it does, what it relies on, and one theorem statement so that the dependency
graph reflects the architecture. The conditional assembly and its parameter arithmetic
are formalized; supplying the introspection and answer-reduction constructions remains
a main task of the project. Their soundness analyses use the bipartite framework.
The paper factors these transformations through \emph{typed} verifiers and detypes at
specified boundaries (Remark~\ref{rem:typed-detyping}); the ambient Lean hypotheses
also require the runtime adaptation of Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-normalization}.

Following Lin~\cite{Lin25}, every soundness statement below is in \emph{value form}: a
transformation preserves the property ``value at most $1 - \eps$'' up to a quantitative
loss, and compression preserves ``value at most $\frac12$''. The three inner
transformations, repetition and compression all carry that property in $\valstar$, the
bipartite tensor-product value: it is the class the compressibility criterion is
instantiated with and the form Definition~\ref{def:mipstar} requires
(Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}), and no passage between the two values occurs
anywhere on the path. The synchronous value is not used, and neither is the
almost-synchronous transport of Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync}; see
Remark~\ref{rem:sync-invariant} for why that is the better arrangement and
Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route} for the evidence. No entanglement lower bound is tracked. Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored} explains why none is needed,
Section~\ref{sec:rr-compression} states the abstract lemma (Lin's compressibility
criterion) that turns value-form compression into the halting reduction, and the
entanglement clauses of~\cite{JNVWY20} are recorded for reference in
Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}.

\subsection{Conditionally linear functions and samplers}
\label{sec:ps-cl}

Question distributions must be structured enough that players can sample them
\emph{on their own} in a certifiable way (introspection), yet expressive enough to
implement all the verifiers in the pipeline. The right class is that of conditionally
linear distributions: linear maps applied in stages, where each stage may depend on
the output of the previous ones.

\begin{definition}[Conditionally linear function]
\label{def:cl-function}
\ledgernode{1.1.2}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun,
  MIPRE.CL.clDist}
\leanok
Let $V = \F^n$ for a finite field $\F$ and $n \geq 0$. A \emph{register subspace} of $V$ is
the span of a subset of the standard basis; for a register subspace $W$ and $x \in V$, $x^W$
denotes the projection of $x$ onto $W$ along the complementary register subspace. For
$\ell \geq 0$, the \emph{$\ell$-level conditionally linear (CL) functions} on $V$ are defined
inductively: the only $0$-level CL function is the zero function on $V$; for $\ell \geq 1$, an
$\ell$-level CL function is a function $L : V \to V$ of the form
\begin{equation*}
  L(x) = L_1\bigl(x^{V_1}\bigr) + L_{>1,\, L_1(x^{V_1})}\bigl(x^{V_{>1}}\bigr)
\end{equation*}
for complementary register subspaces $V = V_1 \oplus V_{>1}$, a linear map $L_1 : V_1 \to
V_1$, and, for each $v$ in the range of $L_1$, an $(\ell-1)$-level CL function $L_{>1,v}$ on
$V_{>1}$. A \emph{CL distribution} is the distribution of $(L^\alice(x), L^\bob(x))$ for
uniformly random $x \in V$ and CL functions $L^\alice, L^\bob$ on $V$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Comments.} This is the paper's definition (\texttt{linear.tex},
\texttt{def:cl-func}); the paraphrase this blueprint carried before started at level $1$ and
let $V_1 \oplus V_{>1}$ be any direct sum decomposition, both departures from the paper. Two
things about the formalization. Every consumer of the definition --- the structure lemma
below, the query interface of a sampler, the hiding tests of introspection --- works with the
decomposition data $(V_1, L_1, L_{>1,\cdot})$, which the function does not determine; so the
Lean object is a \emph{presentation}, \texttt{CLFun}, an inductive type indexed by the level,
with \texttt{CLFun.eval} the function it presents and \texttt{IsCLFun} the paper's notion
(some presentation on $V$ evaluates to the function). And presentations live on one ambient
space $\F^\iota$ whose register subspaces are sets of coordinates; ``a presentation on $V_T$''
is the predicate \texttt{CLFun.SupportedOn}, under which the paper's $V_{>1}$ is the
complement of $V_1$ inside $V_T$. The continuations $L_{>1,v}$ are indexed by all $v$ rather
than by the range of $L_1$; the extra ones are never evaluated. The distribution is
\texttt{clDist}, in the real-valued format of Definition~\ref{def:game}.

\begin{lemma}[Structure of a CL function]
\label{lem:cl-structure}
\uses{def:cl-function}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun.truncate, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorAt, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapAt,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorOfPrefix, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapOfPrefix, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.ExactlyOn,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.eval_truncate_eq_sum, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.disjoint_factorAt,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.factorAt_subset, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.ExactlyOn.biUnion_factorAt,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.factorOfPrefix_eval, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.SupportedOn.mapOfPrefix_eval}
\leanok
Let $L$ be an $\ell$-level CL function on $V$, given with its decomposition data. For $k \in
\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$ there are a $k$-level CL function $L_{\leq k} : V \to V$ on $V$, the
\emph{$k$-th marginal} (with $L_{\le 0} = 0$ and $L_{<k} = L_{\le k-1}$), and, for every
prefix $u \in L_{<k}(V)$, a register subspace $V_{k,u}$ of $V$, the \emph{$k$-th factor space},
and a linear map $L_{k,u} : V_{k,u} \to V_{k,u}$, the \emph{$k$-th linear map}, such that for
all $x \in V$, writing $u_i = L_{<i}(x)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L_{\leq k}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} L_{i,u_i}\bigl(x^{V_{i,u_i}}\bigr)$, and
  $L = L_{\le \ell}$;
\item the factor spaces $V_{1,u_1}, \ldots, V_{\ell,u_\ell}$ are pairwise disjoint register
  subspaces of $V$, and $V = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell} V_{i,u_i}$ when the data are
  \emph{exact}, that is, when the last level of every branch leaves no coordinate over.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
This is \texttt{lem:cl-kth} of \texttt{linear.tex}, by induction on the level. In Lean the
marginal is \texttt{CLFun.truncate}; the factor space and the linear map of a stage are
computed both along a run (\texttt{factorAt}, \texttt{mapAt}) and from the prefix alone
(\texttt{factorOfPrefix}, \texttt{mapOfPrefix}), and the two agree
(\texttt{factorOfPrefix\_eval}, \texttt{mapOfPrefix\_eval}): that is what makes the paper's
indexing by prefixes well defined, and it is the fact the queries of a sampler rest on.
Exactness is \texttt{CLFun.ExactlyOn}. The paper states the lemma as an equivalence, with the
data normalized so that the factor spaces always exhaust $V$; the formalization keeps the
paper's own inductive definition, under which a level-$0$ remainder is allowed, and records
exactness as the separate predicate that samplers are required to satisfy.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closure of CL functions]
\label{lem:cl-closure}
\uses{def:cl-function,lem:cl-structure}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun.concat, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.directSum, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.succ,
  MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.concat, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.directSum, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.directSum'}
\leanok
Let $V = U \oplus W$ be a decomposition of $V$ into complementary register subspaces.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Levels.) Every $\ell$-level CL function is $(\ell+1)$-level.
\item (Concatenation.) If $L$ is a $k$-level CL function on $U$ and $R_u$ an $\ell$-level CL
  function on $W$ for each $u \in L(U)$, then $x \mapsto L(x^U) + R_{L(x^U)}(x^W)$ is a
  $(k+\ell)$-level CL function on $V$.
\item (Direct sums.) If $L$ is a $k$-level CL function on $U$ and $R$ an $\ell$-level CL
  function on $W$, then $x \mapsto L(x^U) + R(x^W)$ is a $\max(k,\ell)$-level CL function on
  $V$.
\end{enumerate}
Concatenation and direct sums preserve exactness in the sense of
Lemma~\ref{lem:cl-structure}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{rk:higher-level}, \texttt{lem:cl-concat} and \texttt{lem:cl-func-prod} of
\texttt{linear.tex}, the last for two summands, which iterates. The Lean constructions are on
presentations and carry no hypotheses; that the supports are disjoint enters in the evaluation
lemmas identifying the function presented.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Direct sums of families, and their distributions]
\label{lem:cl-famsum}
\uses{lem:cl-closure,lem:cl-structure}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.CLFun.famSum, MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.famSum, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.exactlyOn_famSum,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval_famSum, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval_truncate_famSum,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorOfPrefix_famSum, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapOfPrefix_famSum,
  MIPRE.CL.clDist_famSum}
\leanok
Let $V = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} V^{(i)}$ with every $V^{(i)}$ a copy of $F^s$, and let $L^{(i)}$
be an $\ell$-level CL function on $V^{(i)}$ for each $i$. Then $x \mapsto (L^{(i)}(x^{(i)}))_i$
is an $\ell$-level CL function on $V$, exact if every $L^{(i)}$ is, whose marginals, factor
spaces and stage maps read off a prefix are those of the $L^{(i)}$ on the blocks. For two such
families $(L^{(i)})$, $(R^{(i)})$, the conditionally linear distribution of the pair of direct
sums is the product of the distributions of the pairs $(L^{(i)}, R^{(i)})$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Item (3) of Lemma~\ref{lem:cl-closure} iterated over the $k$ summands, done in one induction
on the level with the $k$ presentations side by side: at every stage the register subspace is
the union of the blocks' and the map their direct sum, and the continuations are selected
blockwise. The Lean presentation lives on any $F^\kappa$ with $\kappa \cong \{1, \ldots, k\}
\times \{1, \ldots, s\}$, so that $\kappa = \{1, \ldots, ks\}$ needs no reindexing. For the
distribution: a uniform vector of $V$ is a $k$-tuple of independent uniform vectors of the
blocks, and the event that the direct sums take prescribed values is the conjunction of the
blockwise events.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Downsizing]
\label{lem:cl-downsize}
\uses{def:cl-function,lem:cl-structure}
\lean{MIPRE.CL.downsizeEquiv, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval_downsize,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.eval_truncate_downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.factorOfPrefix_downsize,
  MIPRE.CL.CLFun.mapOfPrefix_downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.ExactlyOn.downsize,
  MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.downsize, MIPRE.CL.clDist_downsize, MIPRE.CL.CLFun.reindex,
  MIPRE.CL.IsCLFun.reindex}
\leanok
Let $\F$ be a finite field, $\mathbb{K}$ a subfield, and $e_1, \ldots, e_t$ a basis of $\F$
over $\mathbb{K}$; let $\downsize : \F^n \to \mathbb{K}^{nt}$ be the $\mathbb{K}$-linear
bijection reading off the coordinates of each entry in this basis. If $L$ is an $\ell$-level CL
function on $V = \F^n$, then $L^\downsize = \downsize \circ L \circ \downsize^{-1}$ is an
$\ell$-level CL function on $\mathbb{K}^{nt}$, whose decomposition data are the downsized data
of $L$: for $j \in \{1, \ldots, \ell\}$ and $u \in L_{<j}(V)$,
\begin{equation*}
  L^\downsize_{\le j} = \downsize \circ L_{\le j} \circ \downsize^{-1}, \qquad
  V^\downsize_{j,\, \downsize(u)} = \downsize(V_{j,u}), \qquad
  L^\downsize_{j,\, \downsize(u)} = \downsize \circ L_{j,u} \circ \downsize^{-1},
\end{equation*}
where $\downsize(V_{j,u})$ is the register subspace of $\mathbb{K}^{nt}$ on the coordinates
$(i, k)$ with $i$ a coordinate of $V_{j,u}$. Exactness is preserved, and the CL distribution of
$(L^\downsize, R^\downsize)$ is the image under $\downsize$ of that of $(L, R)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{lem:cl-downsize} and \texttt{lem:downsize-cl-dist} of \texttt{linear.tex}, by
induction on the level; the one fact used is that $\downsize$ commutes with the coordinate
projections onto register subspaces (\texttt{lem:downsize\_subspace} of
\texttt{preliminaries.tex}, in the register case).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} The paper states the lemma for admissible $q = 2^t$, $t$ odd, with the
self-dual normal basis of Lemma~\ref{lem:self-dual-basis}; the formalization proves it for any
basis of any finite field over any subfield, the coordinates of $\mathbb{K}^{nt}$ being indexed
by pairs $(i, k)$ and identified with $\{1, \ldots, nt\}$ by a reindexing
(\texttt{CLFun.reindex}, the transport along any bijection of coordinates, which preserves
everything above). The self-dual normal basis matters elsewhere: for the arithmetic of
$\F_{2^t}$ to be computable from $\F_2$-data in polynomial time by the samplers and deciders
that use it, a matter for the efficient-computability toolkit rather than for this lemma. The
paper's downsized \emph{sampler} (\texttt{def:downsize\_sampler},
\texttt{lem:downsize\_sampler}), a Turing machine wrapping a sampler over $\F_{2^t}$, has no
counterpart here: samplers are over $\F_2$ by definition (Definition~\ref{def:sampler}), and a
construction native to $\F_{2^t}$, such as the introspection sampler, is to present the
downsized CL functions directly, this lemma supplying their decomposition data.

\begin{remark}[Why the pipeline is bipartite throughout]
\label{rem:bipartite-route}
\uses{def:q-value,def:sync-value,thm:almost-sync,rem:almost-sync-hypothesis,rem:sync-invariant,lem:sync-le-valstar}
Every soundness clause of this chapter is stated in $\valstar$ and none in $\synval$. The
choice follows the source transformation contracts and the direct repetition theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}), all of which already concern $\valstar$.
No scalar transport from a synchronous value bound is needed. In particular, neither
the synchronization statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync} nor the additional
hypotheses discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:almost-sync-hypothesis} enter the composition.
The source rigidity analyses can therefore be formalized in their bipartite setting,
without the synchronization invariant of Remark~\ref{rem:sync-invariant}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar} is used only to read a $\valstar$ conclusion in $\synval$ when
a synchronous statement is wanted (Theorem~\ref{thm:halting}, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}),
never in the other direction.

What this costs in turn is that the rigidity and approximate-measurement lemmas the inner
analyses consume must be stated bipartitely, as their sources state them, rather than
tracially. That is the arrangement of~\cite{JNVWY20} and of the companion repository's
paper, so it is the arrangement with the least to prove.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Question reduction: introspection}
\label{sec:ps-introspection}

Introspection removes the verifier's cost of sampling questions: instead of receiving
questions for $\verifier_{2^n}$, the players \emph{sample the questions themselves} by
measuring shared EPR pairs, and the new verifier uses the Pauli basis test
(Theorem~\ref{thm:qld}) to force honest sampling. The CL structure of the sampler is
what makes commitment to each stage of the sampled question enforceable (the ``hiding''
directions of the CL decomposition). The result is a verifier whose questions are
exponentially smaller, at the price of a fixed number of levels.

\begin{remark}[Typed verifiers and the exterior transformation boundaries]
\label{rem:typed-detyping}
\ledgernode{1.1.4}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:tensor-strategy}
The source constructions of~\cite{JNVWY20} use \emph{typed} verifiers: a type graph
labels question roles, and the sampler first selects types and then questions.
Introspection is detyped at its exterior boundary. Oracularization stays typed inside
answer reduction, and only the final answer-reduced verifier is detyped. Thus the
exterior chain is introspection, full answer reduction, then direct repetition, with
levels $5 \to 7 \to 7$. There is no separate untyped oracle step in that count.

Detyping has two costs a formalization must retain. It preserves the value
only up to a factor $16^{-\abs{T}}$ in the type graph's size, which is where the
``constant-factor loss'' in the soundness chains comes from; and it adds $2$ to the level
count, which is part of why the level budget is what it is. The lemma also needs the type
graph to have at least one edge --- a hypothesis every instantiation in the paper satisfies
and which the source now states explicitly after its repair --- and its soundness argument truncates over-long
answers, justified by the same answer-truncation lemma that the anchoring
construction of~\cite{BVY17} uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Turing machine conventions and the ambient cost model]
\label{rem:tm-conventions}
\ledgernode{1.1.5, 1.1.5.1, 1.1.5.2, 1.1.5.3}
\uses{def:decider,def:lambda-bounded,lem:universal-tm}
The paper fixes a concrete Turing machine formalism and a \emph{timeout-counter} normal form
$B(n) = c_0 (c_1 n)^{c_2}$: a machine is wrapped so that it always halts within its bound,
and the wrapped description is canonical enough that the parameters can be read back off
it. The ambient Lean model instead uses input-size-dependent resource budgets
(Section~\ref{sec:rr-computability}). This changes the transformation hypotheses;
it does not establish the source's timeout or answer-truncation properties.
Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-normalization} records the required adaptation, and
Section~\ref{sec:departures} records the departure.

Three things in those nodes are worth keeping even so, because they are the kind of detail a
formalization rediscovers the hard way. The canonical form has to be genuinely injective in
the parameters for the timeout bound to be well defined at all, and the audit had to supply
an explicit parser to establish it --- in the source the parser's existence was asserted
rather than constructed. The overhead polynomial relating a machine's running time to its
bound needs the normalization $c_1 \geq 2$, without which the claim fails outright at
$c_1 = 1$, $n = 1$. And the whole scheme rests on the bound dominating $n$, which holds only
because the constants are positive integers. None of the three is deep; all three are the
sort of thing that stops a formalization dead.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Runtime normalization obligation]
\label{rem:runtime-normalization}
\uses{def:lambda-bounded,def:decider,rem:tm-conventions,rem:runtime-contract-bridge}
The model distinction is recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-contract-bridge}; here
are its consequences for the transformations.
The source introspection theorem uses an absolute bound $N^\lambda$ at index $N$;
its simulator rejects after that many steps. Definition~\ref{def:lambda-bounded}
instead bounds ambient cost on data $d$ by $N^\lambda(\abs{d}+1)^\lambda$.
Even on questions and answers of length at most $N^\lambda$, this supplies a bound
of order $N^{O(\lambda^2)}$, before simulation overhead, rather than $N^\lambda$.
It also gives no answer-prefix invariance for arbitrary longer strings.

To derive the ambient transformation hypotheses from the source, a normalization
construction must restrict the valid input formats, preserve the original answer cut,
and prove preservation of values and perfect PCC witnesses. It must provide the
absolute simulation budgets and the truncation properties required by the source,
including bounded checks on malformed inputs. If this requires a reparameterization
$\lambda' = p(\lambda)$, its effect on the introspection bound
$\poly(2^{p(\lambda)n},\ell)$ and on the soundness margins must be propagated.
Enlarging the answer alphabet to $N^{\lambda'}$ without rejecting answers beyond the
original cut can change the game. A direct proof in the ambient model is another route.

This is an open obligation, not a normalization theorem established here.
\texttt{Budget} and \texttt{Verifier.Within} record coefficients, degrees, question
dimensions and explicit rejection bounds; \texttt{GapCompression.output\_rejects\_long}
constrains the output. None of these supplies normalization for arbitrary
$\lambda$-bounded inputs. The introspection, oracularization and answer-reduction
source displays below use the paper's absolute-time convention and its bipartite PCC
notion. Separate ambient contract definitions carry the Lean tags and statement marks;
their inhabitants require the supply targets stated below. In particular, identifying
source PCC witnesses with ambient completeness witnesses requires the two directions
of Remark~\ref{rem:pcc-representation-bridge}. The comparison is with \texttt{introspection.tex},
\texttt{oracularization.tex} and \texttt{ld\_compiler.tex} in the companion paper
pinned by~\cite{AuditSync26}.
\end{remark}


\subsubsection{The introspective verifier}

\begin{definition}[The introspective verifier]
\label{def:intro-verifier}
\ledgernode{1.2.3}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:cl-function,rem:typed-detyping,thm:qld}
Fix $\ell$ and an $\ell$-level verifier $\verifier$ satisfying the paper's absolute
$\lambda$-bound, as distinguished in Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-normalization}. The
\emph{introspective verifier} $\vint$ is the typed verifier whose players sample the
conditionally linear question distribution of $\verifier_{2^n}$ \emph{themselves}: the
question $y \in V = \F_q^s$ is not sent to them, but obtained by each player measuring its
half of $Q$ shared EPR pairs in a Pauli basis, and reported back. Its decider runs three
kinds of test.
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \textbf{Pauli test}, which is the game of Theorem~\ref{thm:qld} on the same EPR
  pairs, so that the measurements the players use to sample are forced to be genuine Pauli
  measurements in the low-degree-encoded basis;
\item a \textbf{sampling test}, which forces the reported $y$ to be distributed as the
  conditionally linear distribution of $\verifier_{2^n}$ rather than as anything else the
  players might prefer;
\item a \textbf{hiding test}, which forces each player not to learn the parts of the
  question that the conditional sampling should have hidden from it --- the types
  $\mathtt{Hide}_1, \ldots, \mathtt{Hide}_\ell$ exist for this, one per level.
\end{enumerate}
It is a $3$-level \emph{typed} verifier, hence $5$-level once detyped
(Remark~\ref{rem:typed-detyping}), and that $5$ is what the compression pipeline consumes.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Complexity of the introspective verifier]
\label{lem:intro-complexity}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.1}
\uses{def:intro-verifier,def:lambda-bounded}
There are Turing machines computing the two halves of $\vint$ separately: one which on input
$(\lambda, \ell)$ outputs the sampler $\sint$ in time $\polylog(\lambda, \ell)$, with
$\TIME_{\sint}(n) = \poly(n, \lambda, \ell)$ and $\sint$ a $3$-level typed sampler; and one
which on input $(\verifier, \lambda, \ell)$ outputs the decider $\dint$ in standard
timeout-counter form, in polynomial time, with $\abs{\dint} = \poly(\lambda, \ell)$ and
timeout bound $B_{\dint}(n) = \poly(2^{\lambda n}, \ell)$. The sampler's description does not
depend on $\verifier$ at all.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[A false step in computing the hidden subspace]
\label{rem:intro-lperp}
\uses{lem:intro-complexity,def:cl-function}
The decider has to compute, for a level $k$ and a prefix $u$, the orthogonal complement
$(L^v_{k+1, u})^\perp$ of the conditionally linear map at that level. The printed procedure
computed it by taking a basis of $\ker(M)$ and forming its canonical complement --- and the
canonical complement of a basis of $\ker(M)$ is \emph{not} a basis of $\ker(M)^\perp$. Over
$\F_2^2$ the map $L(x_1, x_2) = (x_1 + x_2, 0)$ is a counterexample, found independently by
two checkers. Only the computation was wrong: the test itself is defined abstractly through
the complement, so the statement of Lemma~\ref{lem:intro-complexity} and its bound are
unaffected. It is recorded because a formalization must supply a procedure that is actually
correct, and this is the obvious place to get it wrong. The same audit closed a second seam
here, an answer-length bound of $8Q$ that only holds once the honest answer is truncated the
way oracularization and answer reduction already truncate theirs.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Completeness: two identities about Pauli measurements}

The honest strategy measures EPR pairs in the bases the sampled question prescribes, so the
completeness proof is not an approximation argument at all: it is two exact statements about
how Pauli operators interact with measurements of a linear map, and then a check that the
seven tests of the decider all pass with certainty. Both identities are finite-field linear
algebra, and both are plausible early Lean targets.

\effortMedium

\begin{lemma}[Pauli operators against a linear-map measurement]
\label{lem:pauli-linear-fourier}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.2}
\uses{def:cl-function}
Let $L : \F^k \to \F^k$ be linear, let $W \in \{X, Z\}$, and for $a$ in the range of $L$
pick $u_a$ with $L(u_a) = a$. Write $\sigma^W_{[L(\cdot) = a]}$ for the projector onto the
event that a $W$-basis measurement returns a preimage of $a$. Then for every
$v \in \ker(L)^\perp$,
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma^W(v) = \sum_{a \in \F^k} \omega^{\fieldtr(u_a \cdot v)}\, \sigma^W_{[L(\cdot) = a]},
\end{equation*}
and conversely, for every $a$ in the range of $L$,
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma^W_{[L(\cdot) = a]} = \E_{v \sim \ker(L)^\perp}\ \omega^{-\fieldtr(v \cdot u_a)}\,
    \sigma^W(v).
\end{equation*}
The choice of $u_a$ off the range of $L$ is immaterial, since there the projector vanishes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each identity is the Fourier expansion of the other over the group $\ker(L)^\perp$: the
character $v \mapsto \omega^{\fieldtr(u_a \cdot v)}$ depends only on $a = L(u_a)$, because two
preimages of $a$ differ by an element of $\ker(L)$, which the trace pairing annihilates
against $\ker(L)^\perp$.
\end{proof}

\effortEasy

\begin{lemma}[A commutation criterion for $X$ and $Z$ measurements]
\label{lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.2}
\uses{lem:pauli-linear-fourier}
Let $L, R : \F^k \to \F^k$ be linear with $\ker(R)^\perp \subseteq \ker(L)$. Then the
measurements $\{\sigma^Z_{[L(\cdot) = b]}\}_b$ and $\{\sigma^X_{[R(\cdot) = d]}\}_d$ commute.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand both families by Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-linear-fourier}. The $Z$-side expansion runs
over $v \in \ker(L)^\perp$ and the $X$-side over $w \in \ker(R)^\perp$, and
$\sigma^Z(v)$ and $\sigma^X(w)$ commute exactly when $\fieldtr(v \cdot w) = 0$. The
hypothesis gives $w \in \ker(L)$ for every such $w$, and $v \in \ker(L)^\perp$, so the pairing
vanishes term by term.
\end{proof}

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[Completeness of introspection]
\label{lem:intro-completeness}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.2}
\uses{def:intro-verifier,def:pcc,lem:pauli-linear-fourier,lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
If $\verifier_{2^n}$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, then $\vint_n$ has one: the players
measure their EPR halves in the bases the sampled question prescribes and then play the
value-$1$ strategy of $\verifier_{2^n}$ on the question they have obtained. Every test of the
decider accepts with certainty, and the resulting strategy is again projective, consistent
and commuting.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\uses{lem:pauli-linear-fourier,lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-linear-fourier} identifies the honest sampling measurement with a Pauli
measurement, which is what the Pauli test checks; Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-commute-criterion}
gives the commutation the hiding test needs, the hypothesis
$\ker(R)^\perp \subseteq \ker(L)$ being exactly the nesting of consecutive conditionally
linear levels. Consistency and projectivity pass through the construction because each
player's measurement is a product of commuting projective measurements.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The soundness toolkit}

Soundness needs three groups of lemmas before the induction can start, and they are
separated here because they are independent of introspection and reusable: a Pauli twirl, a
conversion from sub-measurements to measurements, and a way to condition on part of a
question.

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[The Pauli twirl]
\label{lem:pauli-twirl}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.3}
\uses{def:distance,def:q-value}
Averaging a measurement against a Pauli operator, on a state that contains EPR pairs,
replaces it by one that commutes with the Pauli basis, at a controlled cost. The group of
statements comprises: commuting with the Pauli basis implies commuting with the Pauli
observables; a mixing lemma for a linear map and one for a unitary; and a lemma combining
the two sides. The error accumulates additively through the group.
\end{lemma}

\effortMedium

\begin{lemma}[Close to a sub-measurement implies close to a measurement]
\label{lem:close-submeasurement}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.3}
\uses{def:distance}
Let $\{A^x_a\}$ be a projective measurement, let each $B^x_a$ be positive semidefinite with
$\sum_a B^x_a \leq \Id$, and let $\{C^x_a\}$ be a POVM with $C^x_a \geq B^x_a$ for all $x$
and $a$. If $A^x_a \approx_\eps B^x_a$ on average over $x$, then
$A^x_a \approx_{\sqrt{\eps}} C^x_a$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The gap $\sum_a (C^x_a - B^x_a)$ is a positive operator of small expectation, because
$\{A^x_a\}$ sums to $\Id$ and is $\eps$-close to $B$; Cauchy--Schwarz turns that bound on the
expectation into a bound on the Hilbert--Schmidt distance, which is where the square root
comes from.
\end{proof}

\effortHard

\begin{lemma}[Conditioning on part of a question]
\label{lem:conditional-measurement}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.4}
\uses{def:distance,def:inconsistency}
On a state that factors as a question part times an answer part, a projective measurement of
the question part induces a distribution on it, and consistency or commutation statements
about the unconditioned measurements transfer to the measurements conditioned on the sampled
question. In particular, if
$\sum_{x, y, z} \bra{\psi} A^y_{x, z} \ot B_{x, y, z} \ket{\psi} \geq 1 - \delta$ with
$\{B_{x,y,z}\}$ projective, then
$\Id \ot B_{x, y, z} \approx_\delta A^y_{x, z} \ot B_{x, y}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Extracting a Pauli strategy]

\ledgernode{1.2.3.5}
\uses{def:intro-verifier,thm:qld,lem:pauli-twirl,def:distance}
There is $\delta_1(\eps, R) = a\bigl( (\log R)^a \eps^b + (\log R)^{-b} \bigr)$, with $a > 1$

\begin{lemma}[The hiding test]
\label{lem:intro-hiding-test}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.6}
\uses{lem:intro-sampling-test,lem:conditional-measurement,def:distance}

\begin{lemma}[The soundness induction]
\label{lem:intro-induction}
\ledgernode{1.2.3.7}
\uses{lem:intro-sampling-test,lem:intro-hiding-test,lem:conditional-measurement,lem:close-submeasurement,def:ent}
For each level $k \in \{1, \ldots, \ell + 1\}$ there are ancilla states and projective

\begin{remark}[One step of the induction is documented rather than repaired]
\label{rem:intro-commutation-gap}
\uses{lem:intro-induction,lem:conditional-measurement}
The induction's $Z$-step applies a commutation analysis to \emph{fine} operators where the
surrounding chain supplies only the \emph{coarse} ones. The audit recorded the repair recipe
in the source at the point of use --- re-run the commutation analysis with the coarse

Lemma~\ref{lem:intro-induction} is the intended one, and a formalization will have to carry

open step in the section; everything else was verified line by line, including the whole
twirl toolkit and the final constant arithmetic.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Introspection]
\label{thm:introspection}
\ledgernode{1.2, 1.2.3.8}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:lambda-bounded,def:pcc,thm:qld,def:cl-function,def:intro-verifier,lem:intro-complexity,lem:intro-completeness,lem:intro-induction}
In the source model, a Turing machine $\ComputeIntroVerifier$ runs in time
$\poly(\abs{\verifier},\log\lambda,\ell)$, polynomial in its input length for each
fixed $\ell$. On input $(\verifier, \lambda, \ell)$ it returns a $5$-level normal form verifier $\vint =
(\sint, \dint)$ with
$\TIME_{\sint}(n) = \poly(n, \lambda, \ell)$,
$\TIME_{\dint}(n) = \poly(2^{\lambda n}, \ell)$ and $\abs{\dint} = \poly(\lambda,
\ell)$, with its decider in standard timeout-counter form, such that for every
$\ell$-level verifier $\verifier$ satisfying the source absolute $\lambda$-bound there
is a function $\delta(\eps, n) = a\bigl( (\lambda n)^a \eps^b + (\lambda n)^{-b}
\bigr)$ (constants $a, b$ depending only on $\ell$) with, for all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_{2^n}$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, so
  does $\vint_n$.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) If $\valstar(\vint_n) > 1 - \eps$ then
  $\valstar(\verifier_{2^n}) \geq 1 - \delta(\eps, n)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Ambient introspection contract]
\label{def:introspection-contract}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:lambda-bounded,def:pcc}
\lean{MIPRE.Introspection}
\leanok
The structure \texttt{MIPRE.Introspection} is an ambient contract,
parametrized by $\ell$: the introspective sampler as a function of $\lambda$ alone with its
program computable in polynomial time, the introspective decider's program as a
polynomial-time function of the input programs and $\lambda$, the packaging as a $5$-level
normal form verifier on every input, the complexity clauses for every input and index ---
sampler and decider runtime coefficients at most $(\lambda n + 1)^C$ and
$2^{C(\lambda n + 1)}$, respectively, both at input-size degree $C$, and
$\abs{\dint} \leq C(\lambda + 1)^C$ for a constant $C$, the ambient analogue of
$C_{\mathrm{intro}}$, with
the decider rejecting answers longer than $2^{C(\lambda n+1)}$. For ambient
$\lambda$-bounded inputs, completeness transfers \texttt{HasPerfectPCC} from index
$2^n$ at answer cut $(2^n)^\lambda$ to index $n$ at cut $2^{C(\lambda n+1)}$.
Soundness transfers a value greater than $1-\eps$, for $\eps>0$, at the output cut
to a value at least $1-\delta(\eps,n)$ at the input cut, where
$\delta(\eps,n)=a((\lambda n)^a\eps^b+(\lambda n)^{-b})$, $a\geq1$ and $0<b\leq1$.
The complexity clauses are stated in the
vocabulary of resource budgets (\texttt{MIPRE.Budget}, \texttt{Verifier.Within}: the running-time
coefficients of sampler and decider and their degree, the question dimension, and the answer
length beyond which the decider rejects), which lets the exterior transformations be
chained. The composition \texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression.ofPipeline} consumes an instance at
$\ell = 7$. Here $\lambda$-boundedness is the ambient definition, not the source
absolute-time hypothesis. This definition specifies the contract, without constructing
an inhabitant. Since $\ell$ is fixed by the structure, its polynomial-time construction
field does not assert a bound polynomial in binary $\ell$.
\end{definition}

\label{lem:introspection-supply}
\uses{thm:introspection,def:introspection-contract,rem:runtime-normalization,rem:pcc-representation-bridge}
The ambient contract \texttt{MIPRE.Introspection 7} is inhabited.
\emph{Open target.} A derivation from the source must normalize the input runtimes

\end{lemma}

(twirling, commutation, sandwiching). For this project, those lemmas should be stated and proved in the bipartite setting, as in the sources: the pipeline carries $\valstar$ throughout, so no tracial restatement is needed and none should be attempted (Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}).

\subsection{Oracularization}
\label{sec:ps-oracularization}

Oracularization converts a normal form verifier into one where a single player (the
``oracle'') receives both original questions and answers both, while the other player
receives one of the two questions and must answer consistently. This makes the game's
predicate a pointwise function of one player's answer --- the format required by answer
reduction. This is probably the easiest transformation in the paper and could be attempted first to fix the semantics. 

\begin{theorem}[Oracularization]
\label{thm:oracularization}
\ledgernode{1.3.5}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc,def:sync-strategy}
The source theorem supplies a polynomial-time Turing machine $\ComputeOracleVerifier$
mapping an $\ell$-level normal form verifier $\verifier = (\sampler, \decider)$,
whose decider is in standard timeout-counter form, to a \emph{typed} verifier $\vora$
such that, for some $\delta(\eps) = O(\sqrt{\eps})$ and all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy then
  $\vora_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, in which moreover both players measure with the
  same family of operators.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) If $\valstar(\vora_n) > 1 - \eps$ then
  $\valstar(\verifier_n) \geq 1 - \delta(\eps)$.
\item (\textbf{Complexity}) The sampler of $\vora$ depends only on $\sampler$, with
  $\TIME_{\sampler^{\mathrm{ora}}}(n) = \poly(\TIME_\sampler(n))$ and $\ell$ typed levels.
  The decider has time $\poly(\TIME_\sampler(n),B_\decider(n))$.
\end{enumerate}
The oracularized decider's preamble performs a type check, a question-length check, and a
bounded parse of each answer component against the decider's timeout bound $B_\decider(n)$;
answers failing the parse are grouped into one distinguished outcome, so the oracle
families are projective measurements and not sub-measurements.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Ambient oracularization contract]
\label{def:oracularization-contract}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc}
\lean{MIPRE.Oracularization}
\leanok
The structure \texttt{MIPRE.Oracularization}, parametrized by $\ell$, is an ambient hypothesis
structure with \emph{untyped}, level-preserving output. It takes an explicit parse
bound $(\lambda n+1)^\beta$; its sampler depends only on the input sampler, and its
sampler and decider descriptions are computable in polynomial time. Completeness
transfers \texttt{HasPerfectPCC} at that input cut to the declared output answer bound,
a fixed polynomial in the parse bound. Soundness, for $\eps>0$, transfers output value
greater than $1-\eps$ to input value at least $1-c\sqrt{\eps}$, for a fixed $c>0$.
Its resource clause is polynomial in the parse bound, the input budget and the input
description size, at a degree affine in the input's degree. These are specified fields;
the declaration does not construct such a verifier.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Typed oracle interface obligation]
\label{rem:oracularization-contract}
\uses{thm:oracularization,def:oracularization-contract,rem:typed-detyping,rem:runtime-normalization,rem:pcc-representation-bridge}
The source typed theorem does not directly inhabit this structure. Generic detyping
adds two levels, so a level-preserving untyped implementation or a suitable typed
interface remains an obligation, together with the runtime adaptation of
Remark~\ref{rem:runtime-normalization}. In the paper, oracularization is internal to
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}; its analysis uses the typed verifier
and detypes only the final answer-reduced verifier. The checked composition
\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression.ofPipeline} therefore does not consume
\texttt{MIPRE.Oracularization}. Inhabiting the existing untyped oracle structure
would require a separate justification before using it in the source answer-reduction
proof; it is not a fourth exterior stage or an additional soundness loss in compression.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What the audit changed in oracularization]
\label{rem:oracularization-repairs}
\ledgernode{1.3.5}
\uses{thm:oracularization,def:pcc,def:sync-strategy,rem:identical-operators}
Node $1.3.5$ was added late --- round 12 of the campaign found that
\texttt{oracularization.tex} was covered by no ledger node at all, although both of its
theorems are consumed by answer reduction --- and it carries four repairs that the
statement above has been written to respect.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The soundness constant $\delta_\ora$ carries \emph{one square root}: the step from a
  $1 - O(\eps)$ measurement-closeness to a $1 - O(\sqrt{\eps})$ statement about the value
  is an application of \cite[Fact 4.31]{NW19}, not an identity. An earlier revision of this
  blueprint wrote $\delta(\eps) = \poly(\eps)$, which is not merely imprecise but false as
  stated: $\sqrt{\eps}$ is not a polynomial in $\eps$.
\item The completeness witness's extra property is \emph{identical measurement operators},
  not symmetry of the state. The campaign found the claim that the constructed strategy is
  symmetric ``by construction'' unjustified --- the hypothesis supplies a value-$1$ PCC
  strategy whose state is reused unchanged, and no lemma in the paper symmetrizes such a
  strategy while preserving projectivity, consistency and value $1$. SPCC was withdrawn
  from the completeness clause in consequence. The ambient interface starts from a
  different strategy representation; transferring this source witness requires
  Remark~\ref{rem:pcc-representation-bridge}.
\item The oracle families are genuine projective measurements. Outcomes failing the
  decider's bounded parse are grouped into a distinguished outcome; leaving that implicit
  made them sub-measurements, which the later sandwiching argument may not use.
\item The truncation of answers to $B_\decider(n)$ symbols is justified by a corollary of
  the timeout-counter form, not by the assertion that a timeout-form decider inspects at
  most $B_\decider(n)$ symbols. Nothing forces $B_\decider(n)$ to exceed the time bound
  $T$ against which the PCP decodes, so the truncation is load-bearing and a formalization
  must carry it explicitly.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Answer reduction}
\label{sec:ps-answer-reduction}

After introspection the decider still runs in time exponential in $n$ (it simulates
$\decider$ at index $2^n$), and the players' answers are correspondingly long. Answer
reduction composes the game with a PCP: by Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} the
statement ``the original decider would have accepted our answers'' is a succinct 3SAT
instance; the players commit to a low-degree encoding of a satisfying assignment
(their original answers plus the computation tableau) and the new decider spot-checks
it, using the low individual degree test to certify the encoding and
Lemma~\ref{lem:schwartz-zippel} for the arithmetization. Answers and decider time
become polylogarithmic in the original.

\begin{lemma}[Succinct decoupled 5SAT]
\label{lem:decoupled-5sat}
\ledgernode{1.3.2, 1.3.2.1, 1.3.2.2, 1.3.2.3}
\uses{thm:succinct-sat,def:succinct-3sat,def:decider}
\lean{MIPRE.SAT.DecoupledDescriber, MIPRE.SAT.decoupledDescriber}
\leanok
There is a polynomial-time algorithm that, on the inputs of
Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} under the same hypotheses, outputs a circuit with two inputs
of length $\ell_0 = \lceil \log 2T \rceil$, three of length $r_0 = O(\log T + \log
\sigma)$ and five single-bit inputs, with at most $s_0 = \poly(\log T, \log n, Q, \sigma)$
gates, which describes the decider $\decider$ on $(n, x, y)$ at time $T$ in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:succinct-3sat}: the variables of the decoupled 5SAT formula fall into
five blocks, the first two being the players' answer strings, and every clause touches
each block once. The parameters $\ell_0, r_0, s_0$ are computable in time polynomial in
the bit lengths of $n, T, Q, \sigma$. (The padded description, with the outer variable
count a power of two, is the padding step of the PCP, Remark~\ref{rem:pcp-power-of-two}.)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{thm:succinct-sat}
Run the describer of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} to get a circuit $C_3$ on
$3 r_0 + 3$ inputs, $r_0$ being that theorem's index width, and set
$\ell_0 = \lceil \log 2T \rceil$. Since $2^{r_0} \geq 4T$ and $2^{\ell_0} < 4T$ we have
$\ell_0 \leq r_0$, so an $\ell_0$-bit index compared with an $r_0$-bit one is zero-padded
and $i_2 + 2T < 4T \leq 2^{r_0}$ does not overflow. The output circuit accepts
$(i_1, \ldots, i_5, o_1, \ldots, o_5)$ when any of nine conditions holds: the first is
$C_3(i_3, i_4, i_5, o_3, o_4, o_5) = 1$, and the other eight are
$i_1 < 2T \land i_1 = i_3 \land o_1 \neq o_3$; \;
$i_2 < 2T \land i_3 = i_2 + 2T \land o_2 \neq o_3$; \;
$i_1 \geq 2T$ with $i_1$ even and $o_1 = 1$, or $i_1$ odd and $o_1 = 0$; \; the same two for
$i_2, o_2$; \; $i_3 = i_4 \land o_3 \neq o_4$; and $i_4 = i_5 \land o_4 \neq o_5$.

For a fixed triple of indices the signs of the blocks a condition does not constrain range
over all of $\{0,1\}^3$, so a decoupled clause is satisfied exactly when the literals of the
blocks it does constrain are. The last two conditions therefore say $w_1 = w_2 = w_3$, the
first two that the common assignment agrees with $a$ below $2T$ and with $b$ on $[2T, 4T)$,
and the middle four that $a$ and $b$ carry the blank padding above $2T$. So a satisfying
five-tuple is exactly a satisfying assignment of $\varphi_{3}$ extending the answer blocks,
and item~1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:succinct-sat} finishes it. Sizes and running times are those
of $C_3$ plus $O(r_0)$ gates for the eight conditions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two repairs this proof carries]
\label{rem:decoupled-5sat-repairs}
\uses{lem:decoupled-5sat}
An earlier sketch here described decoupling as a generic syntactic transformation --- split a
clause that touches a block twice by adding a fresh variable and an equality clause --- which
is not the construction the source gives and does not produce the promised index lengths:
$\ell_0$ is pinned to $\lceil \log 2T \rceil$ by the answer blocks, and a generic splitting
fixes neither that nor $r_0$. The proof above is the source's.

The parities of the four padding conditions are a repair of the verification campaign, and
the one place where a careless transcription breaks completeness rather than soundness.
Blocks are $0$-indexed and the blank encodes as $10$, so the honest padding begins at the
even index $2T$ and puts $1$ at even indices and $0$ at odd ones; the pre-repair text had it
the other way round, which the honest assignment violates. The formalization's tape encoding
agrees with the repaired parity, so nothing had to be adapted --- but nothing was taken on
trust either.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The low-degree code]
\label{def:ld-encoding}
\lean{MIPRE.LowDegree.ofBool, MIPRE.LowDegree.pt, MIPRE.LowDegree.ind,

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The bespoke PCP for deciders]
\label{thm:pcp-decider}
\ledgernode{1.3.3}
\uses{lem:decoupled-5sat,lem:schwartz-zippel,thm:qld,def:decider,def:ld-encoding}
\lean{MIPRE.SAT.BinField, MIPRE.SAT.PcpParams, MIPRE.SAT.PcpProof, MIPRE.SAT.PcpProof.ev,

\begin{remark}[Why $m$ is a power of two]
\label{rem:pcp-power-of-two}
\uses{thm:pcp-decider,thm:qld}
The hypothesis is not cosmetic and was a confirmed defect in the source. The $m$-variate
low-degree test needs $m \mid q$, and $q$ is a power of two, so $m$ must be one as well.
The padded description guarantees this only for the \emph{outer} variable count $m' = 5m +
5 + s$; the inner $m$, inherited from the succinct-SAT reduction, carries no such
guarantee, which leaves the conditionally linear functions of the axis-parallel and
diagonal lines ill defined and the applications of low-degree soundness with an unverified
hypothesis. The repair is to round $m$ up to a power of two in the padding step, which
costs nothing asymptotically. A formalization must state it as a hypothesis, as
Theorem~\ref{thm:pcp-decider} does.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The composition, and what the value form does not inherit]
\label{rem:ar-composition}
\ledgernode{1.3.4}
\uses{thm:answer-reduction,thm:oracularization,thm:pcp-decider,def:ent,rem:compression-chain}
The composition of the oracularized verifier with $\pcpverifier$ --- questions append

\item The ambient space of the answer-reduced sampler is declared as a direct sum of the

The one that does not reach us is the \emph{entanglement} clause. In~\cite{JNVWY20} answer

\subsection{Gap amplification: parallel repetition}
\label{sec:ps-parallel-repetition}

Answer reduction leaves a shrunken gap ($1$ vs.\ $1 - 1/\poly(n)$). Direct parallel
repetition (Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}, formalized end to end) restores
soundness
$\frac12$ with polynomially many repetitions, at polynomial cost in complexity and without
anchoring; the anchored, entanglement-form theorem used in~\cite{JNVWY20}
(\cite{BVY17}) is not needed (Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored}).

\begin{theorem}[Parallel repetition of normal form verifiers]
\label{thm:parallel-repetition}
\ledgernode{1.4, 1.4.3, 1.4.3.1}
\uses{def:normal-verifier,def:pcc,def:direct-repetition,thm:direct-repetition-q,def:mipstar,lem:tensor-power-pcc,lem:repetition-sound-bound}
\lean{MIPRE.Repetition, MIPRE.Repetition.soundBound, MIPRE.repetition}
\leanok
There are a universal constant $c > 0$ and a polynomial-time Turing machine
$\ComputeParrepVerifier$ that on input $(\verifier, \ell, \lambda, \tau, \beta)$, for
$\verifier$ an $\ell$-level normal form verifier, outputs an $\ell$-level normal form
verifier $\vrep$ whose game $\vrep_n$ is the $k(n)$-fold direct repetition
(Definition~\ref{def:direct-repetition}) of $\verifier_n$ with answers of length at most
$B(n) = 2^{\beta(|\lambda| + |n|)}$, with $|\cdot|$ the bit length --- up to the encoding of
answer tuples as strings, the decider parsing a self-delimiting $k(n)$-tuple, each coordinate
against $B(n)$, and rejecting malformed answers --- with $k(n) = 2^{\tau(|\lambda| + |n|)}$,
$\TIME_{\srep}(n) = \poly\bigl(k(n),
\TIME_\sampler(n)\bigr)$ and $\TIME_{\drep}(n) = \poly\bigl(k(n), B(n), \TIME_\decider(n),
\abs{\verifier}\bigr)$, the sampler of $\vrep$ depending only on $(\sampler, \lambda, \ell,
\tau)$, and for all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, then $\vrep_n$
  has a value-$1$ PCC strategy.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) For all $0 < \eps \leq 1$, if $\valstar(\verifier_n) \leq 1 -
  \eps$ then
  \begin{equation*}
    \valstar(\vrep_n) \;\leq\; \exp\Bigl( - \frac{c\, \eps^{13}\, k(n)}{B(n) + 1} \Bigr)\;.
  \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:direct-repetition,thm:direct-repetition-q,lem:tensor-power-pcc,lem:repetition-sound-bound,lem:cl-famsum}
The game $\vrep_n$ is $\verifier_n^{k(n)}$ at the truncated alphabet: its sampler runs
$k(n)$ independent copies of $\sampler$ (a product of conditionally linear functions of the
same level) and its decider runs $\decider$ on every coordinate. Completeness: the
$k(n)$-fold tensor power of a value-$1$ PCC strategy is a value-$1$ PCC strategy for the
repeated game. Soundness is a direct application of
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}: the hypothesis is already a $\valstar$ bound, so
nothing has to be transported first. That theorem bounds $\valstar(\verifier_n^{k(n)})$ by
$\exp\bigl( -c\, k(n)\, \eps^{13} / (\eps + \log(\abs{\mA}\abs{\mB})) \bigr)$, and the answer
alphabets are the strings of length at most $B(n)$, so $\log(\abs{\mA}\abs{\mB}) \leq 2(B(n)
+ 1)$ and the denominator is at most $3(B(n) + 1)$, the factor absorbed into $c$. The
exponent $13$ is the one of Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q} itself, with no further
loss: carrying the hypothesis in $\valstar$ rather than $\synval$ removes both the
transport's polynomial loss and the factor $\kappa^{13}$ that its diagonal-weight hypothesis
used to contribute. The conclusion is in $\valstar$, as is the hypothesis: that is the form
Definition~\ref{def:mipstar} asks of the final game, and it is available at every step
because nothing in the chain ever leaves $\valstar$ (Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}).
In~\cite{JNVWY20} the answer length is the timeout bound $B_\decider(n)$ read off the
decider and $\tau$ is chosen against it; the ambient model has no timeout-counter form, so
the parse length is the explicit parameter $\beta$, and the choice of $\tau$ against it is
Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-tau}. The counts are powers of two rather than the paper's
$(\lambda n)^\tau$ because a program of the ambient model writes $2^{\tau(|\lambda| + |n|)}$
down by a walk over the bits of $\lambda$ and $n$, where $(\lambda n)^\tau$ would need
multiplication; $(\lambda n + 1)^\tau \leq 2^{\tau(|\lambda| + |n|)} \leq (\lambda n +
1)^{6\tau}$ for $\lambda, n \geq 1$, so nothing downstream can tell the difference.

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Lean statement.} The theorem is the structure \texttt{MIPRE.Repetition},
parametrized by $\ell$: the repeated sampler as a function of the input sampler and
$(\lambda, \tau)$ with its program computable in polynomial time, the repeated decider's
program as a polynomial-time function of the input programs and $(\lambda, \tau, \beta)$,
the packaging as an $\ell$-level normal form verifier, the complexity clause in the
vocabulary of budgets (\texttt{Verifier.Within}) at a degree affine in the input's --- the
repeated decider runs the input decider through the universal machine, whose overhead is
polynomial --- and the two value clauses at the answer bounds stated, with $\lambda n + 1$ in
place of $\lambda n$ so that every quantity is meaningful at every index. An earlier
statement of this theorem coupled the answer length to a hypothesis $\TIME_\decider(n) \leq
(\lambda n)^\tau$ and took $k(n) = (\lambda n)^{(1 + c')\tau}$ for a constant $c'$ depending
on a parameter $\kappa$ left over from the transport through $\synval$; writing the consumer
showed that the repetition count and the answer length have to be independent parameters,
and the constant $c'$ is now the choice of $\tau$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-tau}. It is
inhabited by \texttt{MIPRE.repetition} (\texttt{MIPRE/Background/Repetition/Verifier.lean}):
the repeated sampler runs $k(n)$ copies of $\sampler$ through the universal machine, its CL
functions the direct sums of the input's (\texttt{MIPRE.Repeat.repSampler}); the repeated
decider sanitizes its input, parses the two answers as self-delimiting tuples and runs
$\decider$ on every coordinate (\texttt{MIPRE.Repeat.repDecider}); both are the s-m-n
construction on a fixed core, so their programs are polynomial-time functions of the inputs.
The game of the output at index $n$ is the direct repetition of $\verifier_n$ up to a
relabeling of the questions into blocks and the embedding of encoded answer tuples
(\texttt{valStar\_repVerifier}, by \texttt{quantumValue\_extendAnswers}); completeness is the
tensor power carried across that relabeling, soundness is
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q} on the repeated game, and the complexity clause is
the explicit running-time accounting of the two programs (\texttt{repSampler\_timeBound},
\texttt{repDecider\_timeBound}), each dominated by $c (W + 1)^m (|d| + 1)^{e (k + 1)}$ for
constants produced by the accounting itself, $W$ the argument of the polynomial.

\subsection{The compression theorem}
\label{sec:ps-compression}

The three exterior stages are introspection, full answer reduction (with its typed
oracle step), and repetition. Their ambient contracts yield gap-preserving compression,
the compression hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}. The soundness
clause is the value-form clause that~\cite{JNVWY20} state first and then strengthen to
an entanglement bound (Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}).

\begin{theorem}[Gap-preserving compression from ambient stage contracts]
\label{thm:compression}
\ledgernode{1.5, 1.5.7}
\uses{def:introspection-contract,def:answer-reduction-contract,thm:parallel-repetition,def:lambda-bounded,def:pcc,def:normal-verifier}
\lean{MIPRE.GapCompression, MIPRE.GapCompression.ofPipeline}
\leanok
Assume $I : \texttt{MIPRE.Introspection 7}$ and
$A : \texttt{MIPRE.AnswerReduction 5}$, the contracts of
Definitions~\ref{def:introspection-contract} and~\ref{def:answer-reduction-contract}.
Using the supplied repetition construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition},
there is a universal constant $C_0 > 0$ and a polynomial-time
procedure $\Compress$ that on input $(\verifier, \lambda)$ outputs a $7$-level normal
form verifier $\vcompr = (\scompr, \dcompr)$ with runtime coefficients bounded by
$\poly(n,\lambda)$ at a fixed input-size degree, where $\scompr$ is independent of
$\verifier$ and computable from $\lambda$ in time $\polylog(\lambda)$. If moreover
$\verifier$ is a $\lambda$-bounded $7$-level normal form verifier, then for all $n
\geq C_0$ and $N = 2^n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\textbf{Completeness}) If $\verifier_N$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy, then so
  does $\vcompr_n$.
\item (\textbf{Soundness}) If $\valstar(\verifier_N) \leq \tfrac12$ then
  $\valstar(\vcompr_n) \leq \tfrac12$: the class ``$\valstar \leq \tfrac12$'' is
  preserved, in the same value at both ends.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{def:introspection-contract,def:answer-reduction-contract,thm:parallel-repetition,lem:compress-margin,lem:compress-tau,rem:compression-chain}
Let $\ComputeIntroVerifier$ and $\ComputeAnsVerifier$ denote the procedures provided
by $I$ and $A$, respectively. These are assumed ambient procedures in this proof.
$\Compress(\verifier, \lambda)$ is $\ComputeIntroVerifier(\verifier, \lambda, 7)$, then
$\ComputeAnsVerifier(\cdot, \lambda, \mu, \sigma(\lambda))$, then
$\ComputeParrepVerifier(\cdot, 7, \lambda, \tau, \beta)$, with $\mu$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-margin}, $\sigma(\lambda)$ a polynomial in $\lambda$ dominating
$C_{\mathrm{intro}} (\lambda + 1)^{C_{\mathrm{intro}}}$, $\beta$ an exponent with
$\poly\bigl((\lambda n + 1)^\mu + \sigma(\lambda)\bigr) \leq (\lambda n + 1)^\beta$, and
$\tau$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-tau}; $C_0$ dominates $2$, $C_{\mathrm{ar}}$,
the introspection and answer-reduction margin thresholds, the threshold for the lower
bound on $\eps_2$, and $\tau$, as recorded in \texttt{Pipeline/Compress.lean}. The
complexity clauses chain the three complexity clauses through the budgets: every bound is a
monotone polynomial expression in $n$, $\lambda$, $\sigma(\lambda)$ and the sizes of the
programs along the chain, hence at most a polynomial in $n + \lambda$. The value clauses are
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-chain}. In Lean this is
\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression.ofPipeline}: an \texttt{Introspection 7}, an
\texttt{AnswerReduction 5} and a \texttt{Repetition 7} give a \texttt{GapCompression}.
$\sigma(\lambda)$ is computed by iterating the program of $\lambda(n)$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda}, there being no polynomial-time arithmetic in the toolkit for the
paper's $\lceil C_{\mathrm{intro}} \lambda^{C_{\mathrm{intro}}} \rceil$, which only has to be
dominated. Oracularization is not a stage: it is the first step of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:answer-reduction}.
\end{proof}

The assumed ambient introspection contract outputs a $5$-level verifier, and the
answer-reduction contract takes $\ell$ to $\max\{\ell+2,5\}$, here $7$; these match
the exterior levels of the source constructions. Then
direct repetition preserves it (Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition}); so $\Compress$
outputs a $7$-level verifier on every input, and $7$ is consequently the level at which
the recursion closes. A verifier of fewer levels is presented at that level by padding its
decomposition with trivial factors, which an $\ell$-level conditionally linear function
(Definition~\ref{def:cl-function}) admits. In~\cite{JNVWY20} the count is $9$, anchored
repetition adding two levels (Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored}).

\paragraph{The output contract.} The conditional theorem constructs a
\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression}: a
record of the constant $C_0$; the compressed sampler $\lambda \mapsto \scompr_\lambda$, a
valid $7$-level sampler for every $\lambda$, with a polynomial-time program computing its
description from the binary representation of $\lambda$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-sampler-indep});
the polynomial-time map $\Compress$ from pairs of programs and $\lambda$ to the program of
the compressed decider; the packaging of every output, well-formed input or not, as a
$7$-level normal form verifier with that sampler and that decider; the bound $\poly(n,
\lambda)$ on the running times of the output at index $n$, at the degree \texttt{deg} in the
size of the input that Definition~\ref{def:decider} carries, the same bound on the dimension of the compressed
sampler (in the paper a consequence of its running time) and on the length of the answers
the compressed decider can accept (\texttt{output\_rejects\_long}: the paper's repeated
decider accepts only after fully parsing the answers, so an answer longer than its time bound
forces a timeout, the property the amended proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dhalt-values} relies on;
in the ambient model, where running times scale with the input, it is a demand on the
construction, an explicit length check); and the completeness and soundness clauses for
$\lambda$-bounded $7$-level inputs at $n \geq C_0$. The checked pipeline constructs
an instance from the stated transformation hypotheses;
it does not inhabit introspection or answer reduction, nor discharge their source-model
adaptations. The halting reduction consumes an instance
(Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}). Two readings are fixed there.
$\verifier_N$ is taken with answers of length at most $N^\lambda$, the explicitly
chosen input answer cut (also the ambient runtime coefficient), and $\vcompr_n$ with answers
of length at most the stated running-time bound --- beyond which its decider rejects, so
that its value is the same with any larger alphabet
(\texttt{Verifier.valStar\_eq\_of\_rejects}) --- since the exact running times of
Definition~\ref{def:normal-verifier} are not functions of the verifier in the
formalization. And ``a value-$1$ PCC strategy'' is a PCC synchronous strategy of value $1$
for the game on the \emph{doubled} question set (\texttt{Verifier.HasPerfectPCC}, on
\texttt{Verifier.doubledGame}: Alice's questions tagged $0$, Bob's tagged $1$, every other
pair of weight zero and rejected), which is a synchronous game for every verifier, so nothing
is presupposed of the decider. An earlier reading took $\vcompr_n$ itself as the synchronous
game, which asks its decider to reject unequal answers to equal questions --- a property of
the decider that the paper never states, \emph{synchronous} there being a property of
strategies, and one the paper's completeness clause does not supply. The doubled game
is the precise ambient completeness interface. The source's identical operators and
support commutation do not by themselves put its state in the ambient normalized

\begin{lemma}[The compressed sampler does not depend on the verifier]
\label{lem:compress-sampler-indep}
\ledgernode{1.5.1}
\uses{thm:compression,def:normal-verifier,def:cl-function}
\lean{MIPRE.GapCompression, MIPRE.Pipeline.sampler, MIPRE.Pipeline.samplerProg_eq,
  MIPRE.Pipeline.output_sampler}
\leanok
For all inputs $(\verifier, \lambda)$ --- $\lambda$-bounded or not, well-formed or not
--- the sampler $\scompr$ of $\Compress(\verifier, \lambda)$ depends only on $\lambda$,
and there is a Turing machine $\mathtt{ComputeSampler}$ that outputs its description from
$\lambda$ in time $\polylog(\lambda)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Chain the verifier-independence of the sampler through the three stages: introspection's
sampler depends only on $(\lambda, \ell)$, answer reduction's only on its input sampler
and the parameters, and repetition's only on its input sampler and $(\lambda, \ell,
\tau)$. $\mathtt{ComputeSampler}$ is then realized by running $\Compress$ on the trivial
verifier. It is this lemma that makes the recursion of Section~\ref{sec:ps-halting}
possible at all: a fixed point has to quote its own sampler, so the sampler must not
depend on what is being compressed. In Lean the lemma is built into the statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}: the fields \texttt{sampler} and \texttt{samplerProg} of
\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression} are the map $\lambda \mapsto \scompr_\lambda$ and its
polynomial-time description, and \texttt{output\_sampler} says that every output carries it.
The chaining is \texttt{MIPRE.Pipeline.sampler}, the composite of the three sampler maps, with
\texttt{samplerProg\_eq} its polynomial-time description and \texttt{output\_sampler} the
claim for every input.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The margin claim]
\label{lem:compress-margin}
\ledgernode{1.5.3}
\uses{def:introspection-contract,def:answer-reduction-contract}
\lean{MIPRE.Pipeline.eps1, MIPRE.Pipeline.intro_margin, MIPRE.Pipeline.exists_mu,
  MIPRE.Pipeline.eps2, MIPRE.Pipeline.ar_margin}
\leanok
Given the ambient contracts of Definitions~\ref{def:introspection-contract}
and~\ref{def:answer-reduction-contract}, write $(a_1,b_1)$ and $(a_2,b_2)$ for
their respective soundness constants, with $a_1,a_2\geq1$, and
$x = \lambda n$. Put $\eps_1 = \bigl( 8 a_1 x^{a_1} \bigr)^{-1/b_1}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\delta_1(\eps_1, n) < \frac12$ for all $x \geq (4 a_1)^{1/b_1}$.
\item For every $K$ and $C$ there are a universal $\mu \geq C$ and a threshold $N$ such
  that, for all $x \geq N$ and all $1 \leq \sigma \leq x^K$,
  \begin{equation*}
    \sigma^{a_2} x^{-b_2 \mu} \;<\; \frac{\eps_1}{2}\;;
  \end{equation*}
  and then, with $\eps_2 = \bigl( \eps_1 / (3 \sigma^{a_2} x^{\mu a_2}) \bigr)^{1/b_2}$,
  $\delta_2(\eps_2, n) < \eps_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The first term of $\delta_1(\eps_1, n)$ is exactly $\frac18$ and the second is at most
$\frac14$ once $x^{b_1} \geq 4 a_1$. For the second item take $\mu$ with $\mu b_2 \geq K a_2
+ a_1 / b_1 + 1$: then $\sigma^{a_2} x^{-b_2 \mu} \leq x^{-a_1/b_1 - 1}$, which is below
$\eps_1 / 2 = (8 a_1)^{-1/b_1} x^{-a_1/b_1} / 2$ as soon as $x > 2 (8 a_1)^{1/b_1}$; and the
first term of $\delta_2(\eps_2, n)$ is exactly $\eps_1 / 3$. The paper fixes $\sigma =
\lceil C_{\mathrm{intro}} \lambda^{C_{\mathrm{intro}}} \rceil$ and $\mu = \lceil \max\{
C_{\mathrm{intro}}, (9 a_1 + 2 a_2 C_{\mathrm{intro}}) / (b_1 b_2) \} \rceil$ and has the bound
for all $n \geq 2$; the formalization takes $\sigma$ any polynomial in $\lambda$ dominating
$C_{\mathrm{intro}} (\lambda + 1)^{C_{\mathrm{intro}}}$, hence $\sigma \leq x^K$ for a
universal $K$, and a threshold on $n$ that $C_0$ absorbs.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A universal repetition exponent]
\label{lem:compress-tau}
\ledgernode{1.5.5}
\uses{thm:parallel-repetition,def:answer-reduction-contract,lem:compress-margin}
\lean{MIPRE.Pipeline.exists_eps2_lower, MIPRE.Pipeline.exists_tau}
\leanok
With $\eps_2$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-margin}, $1 / \eps_2 \leq x^P$ for a universal
$P$ and all $x$ past a threshold. Consequently, for every $c > 0$ and every $\beta$ there is
a universal integer $\tau$ such that, with $z = \lambda n + 1$, for every $k \geq z^\tau$ and
every $0 \leq B \leq z^\beta$, the repetition bound $\exp\bigl( -c\, \eps^{13} k / (B + 1)
\bigr)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition} is at most $\frac12$ for all $n \geq \tau$,
all integers $\lambda \geq 1$ and all $\eps \geq z^{-P}$; the counts $k(n) \geq z^\tau$ and
$B(n) \leq z^{6\beta}$ of that theorem qualify.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
$1 / \eps_2 = \bigl( 3 \sigma^{a_2} x^{\mu a_2} (8 a_1)^{1/b_1} x^{a_1/b_1} \bigr)^{1/b_2}$,
and each factor is a power of $x$ once $x \geq \max\{3, 8 a_1\}$ and $\sigma \leq x^K$. Then
$c\, \eps^{13} z^\tau / (z^\beta + 1) \geq \frac{c}{2} z^{\tau - 13 P - \beta} \geq
\frac{c}{2} z \geq \log 2$ for $\tau \geq 13 P + \beta + 1 + 2/c$ and $z \geq \tau$. Purely
arithmetic, and computationally testable --- the companion repository tests it.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[How the value chain composes, and what it avoids]
\label{rem:compression-chain}
\ledgernode{1.5.8, 1.5.2, 1.5.4, 1.5.6}
\uses{def:introspection-contract,def:answer-reduction-contract,thm:parallel-repetition,lem:compress-margin,lem:compress-tau}
\lean{MIPRE.Pipeline.output_valStar_le, MIPRE.Pipeline.output_hasPerfectPCC}
The soundness clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} is obtained by applying each
transformation's soundness clause contrapositively, with a margin at every step. From
$\valstar(\verifier_N) \leq \frac12$ and $\delta_1(\eps_1, n) < \frac12$: were
$\valstar(\verifier^{(1)}_n) > 1 - \eps_1$, the assumed ambient introspection contract would give
$\valstar(\verifier_N) \geq 1 - \delta_1 > \frac12$, so
$\valstar(\verifier^{(1)}_n) \leq 1 - \eps_1$. The same step through
the assumed ambient answer-reduction contract, using $\delta_2(\eps_2, n) < \eps_1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-margin}), gives
$\valstar(\verifier^{(2)}_n) \leq 1 - \eps_2$; and
Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel-repetition} with Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-tau} brings that below
$\frac12$. Oracularization is internal to the proposed source proof of answer
reduction; it is not a separate premise of this conditional value chain.

Every step of that chain is an implication between $\valstar$ bounds, which is worth
recording because the route of~\cite{JNVWY20} cannot take it. There the repetition step's
soundness hypothesis is an \emph{entanglement} bound, and $\Ent(\game, 1 - \eps) = \infty$
needs $\valstar(\game) < 1 - \eps$ \emph{strictly} --- a non-strict $\leq$ leaves the value
possibly attained and the entanglement requirement finite. So the paper runs the whole
chain in $\Ent$, starting it at the threshold $1 - \delta_1$ rather than at $\frac12$; the
value-form route would force it to apply repetition at $\eps_2/2$ instead of $\eps_2$, at a
cost of $2^{17}$ in the exponent of its repetition parameter. Direct repetition
(Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-repetition-q}) takes a value hypothesis and so needs no
strictness and pays no such factor: this is the second place, after
Remark~\ref{rem:direct-vs-anchored}, where the value-form pipeline is cheaper than the
entanglement-form one rather than merely equivalent to it. It is also why no entanglement
lower bound has to be tracked anywhere in this chapter.
\end{remark}

Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} instantiates the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}: take $A$ to be the set of (descriptions of) normal
form verifier games with a value-$1$ PCC strategy and $B$ the set of those with $\valstar
\leq \frac12$, with the trivially accepting and the trivially rejecting game as the
distinguished elements; the complement of $B$ is semidecided by enumerating tensor-product
strategies, the $\valstar$ half of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}; and a description
$(\sampler, \decider, n)$ with $n$ in binary is a succinct description of the game
$\verifier_{2^n}$ played by a $\lambda$-bounded verifier. Completeness of compression is
the preservation of $A$; preservation of $B$ is the soundness clause of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} verbatim, hypothesis and conclusion being in the same value.
Taking $B$ in $\valstar$ throughout is what keeps Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync} out of the
pipeline altogether: the source transformations and direct repetition already use
that value (Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}). It is also the form in which the semidecision
procedure is available, the rounding argument for the $\synval$ half not being the same
one. Making this dictionary precise is a
formalization task of this section. The four obligations recorded here from the referee
report (R1) --- the universe of the criterion's strings, compression from a
\emph{succinct} input, preservation on inputs that are not $\lambda$-bounded descriptions,
and the choice of $\lambda$ along the recursion --- are settled as follows, against the
per-level form of the lemma (\texttt{compressibility\_criterion\_levels},
Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}), whose classes $A_n, B_n$ are indexed by the level
of the recursion.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Strings are descriptions, never games.} If they were games, $\compr$ could not
  be polynomial-time, a game of $\verifier_{2^n}$ having $2^{\poly(n, \lambda)}$
  questions. A string is a pair $(\lambda, \decider)$ of a parameter and a decider
  program, read as the verifier $(\scompr_\lambda, \decider)$ with the compressed sampler
  of Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-sampler-indep}, the length check of
  Definition~\ref{def:normal-verifier} being enforced by a wrapper around $\decider$; so
  every string names a normal form verifier, and the interpretation sits outside every
  time bound. In Lean the decider component is the \emph{datum} the string carries, run by
  the universal machine (\texttt{Verifier.ofSamplerDeciderD}): a datum that is not the
  encoding of any program still names a verifier, whatever the interpreter does with it, and
  on the encoding of a program the reading is the program's own run
  (\texttt{Decider.wrap\_accepts\_prog}). Decoding the datum to a program with a fallback,
  the earlier reading, would have obliged the compressor's decider of item 2 to reimplement
  the decoder --- a tree-grammar check --- in the ambient model, to reproduce a fallback
  nobody wanted. $A_n$ is the set of strings whose verifier is $n$-bounded and whose $n$-th
  game has a value-$1$ PCC strategy and whose decider rejects every answer longer than the
  answer bound at index $n$; $B_n$ the set of those that are $n$-bounded, reject long
  answers, and have $\valstar \leq \frac12$ at index $n$ (\texttt{Verifier.InClassA},
  \texttt{InClassB}, the rejection clause being \texttt{Verifier.RejectsLong}; disjoint,
  since a perfect PCC strategy gives $\valstar = 1$). The rejection clause is Step~6 of the
  paper's decider $F$ --- an explicit length check before $\dcompr$ is run, which the amended
  proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dhalt-values} spends to identify the answer alphabets of
  $\vhalt_n$ and $\vcompr_n$ --- made a condition on membership: the soundness direction of
  the compressor's obligation raises the answer bound from the one a class is read with to
  the one Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} takes its hypothesis at, and only a decider that
  rejects beyond the former keeps the value fixed under that
  (\texttt{Verifier.valStar\_eq\_of\_rejects}; enlarging the bound can otherwise only raise
  the value). Only the length check has to go into the wrapper: the answer-length bound is a
  condition on membership in these classes, not on well-formedness, synchronicity is asked
  of no decider at all (the value-$1$ PCC strategy lives on the doubled game), and the
  paper's timeout counter is not needed either --- a decider that fails to halt simply fails
  to be $n$-bounded, and its string then lies in neither class. Tying the bound $\lambda = n$ to the level is what
  replaces the choice of $\lambda$ along the recursion (Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda}): the levels
  $r(e) = 2^{K + 1 + \abs{e}}$ at which the recursion runs are exponential in $\abs{e}$,
  above every polynomial in $\abs{e}$ that the paper's $\lambda$ has to dominate.
\item \emph{Compression from a succinct input.} The compressor cannot read the string it
  is handed: its time is polynomial in the length of $(\hat x, n)$, hence polylogarithmic
  in $n$, and a single bit query to $\hat x$ costs up to $n$. On $(\hat x, n)$ it
  therefore outputs the string $(\lambda(n), \decider_{\hat x})$, where $\lambda(n) =
  \poly(n)$ is a fixed formula and $\decider_{\hat x}$ is the decider that, at its own
  index $N$, reads $x = (\lambda', \decider')$ from $\hat x$ by bit queries (at most $2n +
  O(1)$ of them, rejecting otherwise), forms the verifier $\verifier'$ of $x$ \emph{frozen
  at index $2n + 1$} (\texttt{Verifier.freeze}: a program that replaces the index in its
  input by the frozen one, so that its sampler and decider answer at every index as
  $\verifier'$ does at index $2n + 1$, and its game at index $2^n$ is the game of $x$ at
  level $2n + 1$ --- \texttt{freeze\_valStar}, \texttt{freeze\_hasPerfectPCC},
  with $\lambda$-boundedness transferred by \texttt{freeze\_isBounded}; the answer-length
  bound is immaterial above the length the decider rejects,
  \texttt{Verifier.valStar\_eq\_of\_rejects}, \texttt{hasPerfectPCC\_of\_le}) --- runs
  $\Compress(\verifier', \lambda(n))$, and finally the compressed decider at index $N$. The
  freezing reconciles the recursion's levels $n \to 2n + 1$ with the compression theorem's
  $n \leftarrow 2^n$, at the cost of a logarithmic factor in $\lambda(n)$.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} at index $n \geq C_0$ then transfers the class of $x$ at
  level $2n + 1$ to the class of the output at level $n$, and the output is $n$-bounded
  for $n$ above a universal threshold: its size is $\abs{\hat x} + O(\log n) \leq n$ by the
  slack $2\abs{\hat x} \leq n$ of the lemma, and its time at index $N$ is $\poly(N, n) \leq
  N^n$. In Lean that transfer is done once, against a specification of the output written
  before its construction --- \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.CompressorSpec}: the parameter
  $\lambda(n)$ the output carries, the agreement of its decider at every index with the
  compressed decider of the frozen verifier, its $n$-boundedness, the growth of $\lambda(n)$
  that \texttt{freeze\_isBounded} consumes, and the answer-budget comparison of
  \texttt{exists\_ansBound\_le} --- and \texttt{CompressorSpec.toObligations} is the class
  transfer, in both directions, from those fields alone. What the construction owes is
  exactly the fields of that structure. The program half is done: \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.haltProg}
  is the decider of the output --- it reads $x$ from its succinct description by bit queries
  (\texttt{Cost.Prog.readProg}), rebuilds the description of the wrapped decider of $x$ and
  freezes it at $2n + 1$ in the ambient model, runs $\Compress$, and runs the result on its own
  input through the universal machine (\texttt{prepProg}, \texttt{prepProg\_runs}) ---, the
  output string \texttt{comprStr} is $\lambda(n)$ together with that decider with $(c, n,
  \lambda(n))$ hardcoded, and \texttt{comprStr\_accepts} is the field
  \texttt{accepts\_compr} verbatim, from the forward runs and the inversion
  \texttt{haltProg\_accepts\_iff}. The accounting is done too, with $\lambda(n) = 2^{2 \log n}$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda}: \texttt{isBounded\_comprStr}, \texttt{lamOf\_ge},
  \texttt{ansBound\_le\_lamOf}), the compressor is a polynomial-time function
  (\texttt{comprPoly}), and \texttt{exists\_obligations} inhabits the structure: obligation
  (d) is discharged.
\item \emph{Junk inputs.} Nothing is demanded of the compressor off strings in $A_{2n+1}
  \cup B_{2n+1}$, every string names a verifier, and $\lambda$-boundedness is decided
  nowhere. What the lemma does demand is that the complement of $B_n$ be semidecidable:
  ``not $n$-bounded'' is witnessed by an index and an input on which a time bound fails --- a
  search, so $\Sigma_1$, and this is Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-violation-re} ---, ``accepts some
  answer longer than the bound'' is witnessed by the tuple and a run, a search again
  (\texttt{Verifier.rePred\_not\_rejectsLong}), and
  ``$\valstar > \frac12$ at index $n$'' is the $\valstar$ half of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx} applied to the tabulated game, which is computable from
  an $n$-bounded verifier. No disjunct decides $n$-boundedness, which is $\Pi_1$; the point
  of taking the disjunction is that it does not have to
  (\texttt{Verifier.not\_inClassB\_iff}), and Lemma~\ref{lem:halting-semidecider} is the
  three merged. Two things about the search for a violated bound. The budget it tests against is a
  \emph{cost} and not a number of machine steps, and a step is not a unit of cost, so
  \texttt{Machine.runForD} below does not decide it and Lemma~\ref{lem:cost-budget-decidable}
  is what does. And the index at which a bound fails ranges over all of $\N$, while the bound
  reads its input with the index encoded in binary; carrying the index as a \emph{datum}, read
  back by \texttt{Data.natOf}, is what makes the encoding primitive recursive in the search
  witness and so keeps the enumeration from needing the primitive recursiveness of
  $n \mapsto \mathrm{encode}(n)$, which the development did not have when that enumeration was
  written --- it is now \texttt{MIPRE.Cost.Data.primrec\_encode\_nat}, written for the
  tabulation of item 3 and load-bearing under it. The clause ``computable from an $n$-bounded
  verifier'' is load-bearing and not a convenience: acceptance by a decider is $\Sigma_1$, so a
  tabulation correct at \emph{every} string would decide the halting problem --- take two
  strings whose deciders differ only in how long they run before accepting. Boundedness is what
  makes acceptance decidable, by supplying a budget within which the decider must halt, and one
  budgeted run then settles it (\texttt{Verifier.accepts\_iff\_runForD}, on
  \texttt{Machine.runForD}: iterate the evaluation machine's step function $3k$ times and read
  off the result if the configuration reached is final, which is enough because a derivation of
  cost $t$ is a run of at most $3t$ steps and final configurations are fixed). Every Lean
  statement about the tabulation's \emph{acceptance} carries that hypothesis for this reason ---
  the two sampler runs are budgeted otherwise and carry none, as the paragraph after this
  enumerate records --- and it does one more thing than supply the budget: the time clauses of $\lambda$-boundedness are guarded by
  $2 \leq n$, so at $n = 0, 1$ no budget exists at all, and what excludes those two indices is
  the size clause --- every program encodes to a non-empty datum, so
  $2 \leq \abs{\decider} \leq \abs{\verifier} \leq \lambda$
  (\texttt{Verifier.IsBounded.two\_le}, on \texttt{Cost.Prog.two\_le\_esize}). Each
  statement carried a second hypothesis until the doubling, and why it did is worth naming: a
  game description denotes a \emph{synchronous} game by construction ---
  Definition~\ref{def:game-description} rejects unequal answers to equal questions --- whereas
  $\verifier_n$ does no such thing, so no description can match a verifier that is not
  synchronous at $n$, and $B_n$ does not ask that it be. What is tabulated is therefore not
  the game of $\verifier_n$ but its \emph{doubled} one (\texttt{MIPRE.Game.doubled},
  \texttt{Verifier.doubledGame}): the questions are $\{0,1\} \times \mX$, Alice is asked a
  question tagged $0$ and Bob one tagged $1$, and every other pair --- the diagonal included
  --- carries no weight and is rejected outright. Two things follow, and both are what make
  the repair cheap. The diagonal carries no weight, so the description's veto is
  \emph{implied by the distribution} rather than imposed on the verifier, and the doubled
  game is synchronous whatever $\verifier_n$ is; and since the decision predicate rejects off
  the tag block as well, a description matches the doubled game \emph{totally}, at every
  question pair and not merely on the support, so \texttt{quantumValue\_eq\_of\_equiv} and
  \texttt{SyncStrategy.isPCC\_relabel} apply unchanged and no support-restricted version of
  either is needed. Neither value moves: $\valstar$ is unchanged because a strategy for the
  doubled game restricts to Alice's $0$-tagged and Bob's $1$-tagged measurements while one
  for the original ignores the tag (\texttt{quantumValue\_doubled}), and a value-$1$ PCC
  strategy doubles to one (\texttt{SyncStrategy.double}, \texttt{isPCC\_double}), the doubled
  support mapping \emph{into} the original so that no commutation obligation is added. The
  match (\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.tab\_match}) is therefore asked of every $n$-bounded verifier,
  synchronous or not, which is what Lemma~\ref{lem:halting-semidecider} needs and what $B_n$
  was never going to supply; and the separate soundness clause that used to stand beside it
  --- that the description does not \emph{overshoot}, forcing the diagonal tuples to reject
  being a lowering of the value --- is deleted, the soundness branch of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} taking the equality of values instead. The tabulation itself is
  read with the answer alphabet enumerated by \texttt{Verifier.answerEquiv} and the doubled
  question alphabet by \texttt{Verifier.tagEquiv}, a tag bit prepended to the point's bit
  string, and a description matching the doubled game along those indexings has the value of
  $\verifier_n$ (\texttt{quantumValue\_toGame\_eq\_valStar\_doubled}), which is what carries
  the verdict of a procedure that runs on game descriptions back to the verifier. The same
  transport in $\synval$ is \texttt{Verifier.syncValue\_syncGame\_eq\_one\_doubled}, which is
  what item 1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} needs; see the end of this remark.
\item \emph{The choice of $\lambda$.} Absorbed into item 1.
\end{enumerate}

arithmetic of the two sizes. Both clauses are where the doubling is actually performed, and
each takes one indexing that prepends a tag bit: \texttt{Verifier.accListW} is the acceptance

\texttt{bitsToIdx} twice, with the old \texttt{accList} recovered as the case of two equal

rather than appending it is what keeps the bounds and injectivity of \texttt{bitsToIdx}

instead, with $f = \Ent(\cdot, \frac12)$; it is not used by the main theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[A violated time bound is enumerable]
\label{lem:bounded-violation-re}
\uses{def:lambda-bounded,def:re,lem:cost-budget-decidable}
\lean{MIPRE.Verifier.ComputablyPresented, MIPRE.Verifier.boundBudget,
  MIPRE.Verifier.BoundViolation, MIPRE.Verifier.boundViolationB,
  MIPRE.Verifier.boundViolationB_iff, MIPRE.Verifier.not_isBounded_iff_exists,
  MIPRE.Verifier.rePred_not_isBounded, MIPRE.REPred.of_computable_exists,
  MIPRE.Cost.primrec_natLeB}
\leanok
Call a family $a \mapsto \verifier^a$ of normal form verifiers \emph{computably presented}
when the program of its sampler, the program of its decider, and the dimension $s^a(n)$ of
its sampler's ambient space are computable in $a$ (and, for the dimension, in $n$). For such
a family the set of pairs $(a, \lambda)$ with $\verifier^a$ \emph{not} $\lambda$-bounded
(Definition~\ref{def:lambda-bounded}) is recursively enumerable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:cost-budget-decidable}
Every clause of Definition~\ref{def:lambda-bounded} is universally quantified --- over
indices $m \geq 2$, and, inside the two time bounds, over the inputs of the sampler and of
the decider at $m$ --- and the remaining clause $\abs{\verifier} \leq \lambda$ is a
comparison of two computable numbers. So the negation is an unbounded search for a pair
(index, input), and each test is decidable: the dimension clause because the dimension is
computable by hypothesis, the two time bounds by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cost-budget-decidable}. The index is carried as a datum and read back by
\texttt{Data.natOf}, which is a surjection onto $\N$ inverting the encoding on canonical
binary data, so nothing is lost and the encoding the time bounds prepend to their input is
primitive recursive in the witness.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} The hypothesis is not an artefact of the proof: $\lambda$-boundedness
of a family of verifiers cannot be enumerably refuted unless the family can be run, and the
three data named are exactly what running it takes. They are also what the tabulation of
item 3 needs before anything else --- the question weights come from running the sampler over
$\F_2^{s(n)}$ --- so the hypothesis is discharged once, for the family \texttt{Vof}, and
serves both.

\begin{lemma}[The semidecider of the halting reduction]
\label{lem:halting-semidecider}
\uses{lem:bounded-violation-re,lem:value-lower-approx,lem:semidecide-encoded,def:game-description}
\lean{MIPRE.Halting.exists_sem_of_tab, MIPRE.REPred.or, MIPRE.Verifier.LongAcceptance,
  MIPRE.Verifier.longAcceptanceB, MIPRE.Verifier.longAcceptanceB_iff,
  MIPRE.Verifier.not_rejectsLong_iff_exists, MIPRE.Verifier.rePred_not_rejectsLong}
\leanok
Let the family \texttt{Vof} of verifiers named by strings be computably presented
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-violation-re}) and let $x, n \mapsto \game_{x,n}$ be a computable
family of game descriptions with $\valstar(\game_{x, n}) = \valstar(\verifier^x_n)$
whenever $\verifier^x$ is $n$-bounded. Then there is a closed program of the cost model
halting on $\mathrm{encode}(x, n)$ exactly when $x \notin B_n$: obligation O3 of
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\uses{lem:bounded-violation-re,lem:semidecide-encoded}
$x \notin B_n$ is ``$\verifier^x$ is not $n$-bounded, or its decider accepts some answer
longer than the answer bound at index $n$, or $\valstar(\verifier^x_n) > \frac12$''
(\texttt{Verifier.not\_inClassB\_iff}). Under the hypothesis on the family the last disjunct
may be read on $\game_{x,n}$ instead, in both directions: if $\verifier^x$ is $n$-bounded the
two values are equal, and if it is not then the first disjunct holds anyway. The first
disjunct is recursively enumerable by Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-violation-re}; the second by the
same pattern with a simpler witness --- the tuple and a step budget, tested by one budgeted
run of the decider (\texttt{Verifier.rePred\_not\_rejectsLong}); the third by the $\valstar$
half of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx} along the computable family. A union of recursively
enumerable sets is recursively enumerable, by dovetailing. Lemma~\ref{lem:semidecide-encoded}
turns the result into a program on the encodings of pairs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} The hypothesis is an equality of values under $n$-boundedness
\emph{alone}, and for a while that was more than the tabulation delivered: a game description
denotes a synchronous game by construction, so the match was asked only of a verifier
synchronous at $n$ (item 3 of Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}), while membership in
$B_n$ does not carry synchronicity, and the semidecider was therefore \emph{not} obtainable
from the tabulation together with a disjunction, as the plan had it. Three ways to close the
gap were on the record, none of them this lemma's to choose, and the choice has since been
made: it is the first. The game is tabulated on a doubled question set
$\{0,1\} \times \mX$ --- Alice on the first copy, Bob on the second --- so that the
distribution avoids the diagonal and the forced rejections there are never asked about
(\texttt{MIPRE.Game.doubled} and \texttt{Verifier.doubledGame}, with
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.tab\_match} now matching the doubled game; item 3 there has the
construction). \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.tab\_value} is then this lemma's second hypothesis
exactly, $n$-boundedness being its only condition.

The other two were rejected for what they would have cost elsewhere, which is why the choice
was not this lemma's to make. Making the wrapper of item 1 reject unequal answers to equal
questions would change what a string \emph{denotes}, and the denotation is what
\texttt{accOf\_iff}, the classes $A_n, B_n$ and the compression hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} are all stated about; all three would be reopened, and the
wrapper is deliberately the length check and nothing else (item 1). Adding synchronicity at
$n$ to $B_n$ would leave the tabulation alone but move the cost into the preservation clause
of the compressor, which would then have to produce a verifier synchronous at $n$ as well as
a bounded one --- and that clause is the one obligation of
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract} still open. The doubling costs nothing outside the
tabulation, and it is not the only hypothesis this lemma has lost: the family \texttt{Vof} is
computably presented outright (\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.computablyPresented\_Vof}, off the
re-budgeted sampler runs of Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}), so
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.exists\_sem} takes no hypotheses at all; \texttt{Obligations}
(\texttt{Halting/Reduction.lean}) sits below it in the import order and
\texttt{halting\_reduction} applies it directly, so O3 is a theorem and not a field. The
rejection clause of the classes arrived later than the doubling and cost this lemma one more
disjunct, enumerated by the same search; what it buys is the soundness direction of the
compressor's obligation (Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}, item 1).

\begin{remark}[The entanglement form of~\cite{JNVWY20}]
\label{rem:entanglement-form}
\uses{def:ent,lem:recursive-compression}
The paper states the soundness of each transformation with an additional entanglement
clause, obtained from the same proofs by tracking the Schmidt rank of the extracted
strategies: $\Ent(\vint_n, 1 - \eps) \geq \max\{\Ent(\verifier_{2^n}, 1 -
\delta(\eps, n)),\ (1 - \delta(\eps, n))\, 2^{2^{\lambda n}}\}$ for introspection;
$\Ent(\vora_n, 1 - \eps) \geq \Ent(\verifier_n, 1 - \delta(\eps))$ for
oracularization; $\Ent(\vans_n, 1 - \eps) \geq \frac12 \Ent(\verifier_n, 1 - \delta(\eps, n))$
for answer reduction, the factor $\frac12$ being the Schmidt-rank doubling of the
symmetrization step; the anchored repetition theorem of~\cite{BVY17}; and, composed,
$\Ent(\vcompr_n, \frac12) \geq \frac12 \max\{\Ent(\verifier_N, \frac12),\
2^{N^\lambda - 1}\}$ for compression. Fed into the variant of
Lemma~\ref{lem:recursive-compression} tolerating the factor $\frac12$ (harmless, since
the second term grows much faster than the levels of the recursion), with $f =
\Ent(\cdot, \frac12)$, this yields Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} with the stronger
conclusion $\Ent(\game_\machine, \frac12) = \infty$ for non-halting $\machine$. The
value form adopted here needs none of it; the entanglement clauses remain of interest for
the corollaries of Remark~\ref{rem:further}.

One proviso on the measure: the clauses above are transcribed from~\cite{JNVWY20}, where
the quantity is the least Schmidt rank of a \emph{bipartite} tensor-product strategy,
whereas $\Ent$ of Definition~\ref{def:ent} is the least dimension of a \emph{synchronous}
one. Identifying these measures would require a separate strategy and dimension
comparison; no such comparison follows merely from the synchronization statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:almost-sync}. These clauses are recorded in the paper's measure and
are not consumed by the value-form Lean pipeline.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[A uniform bound on the verifier's parameter]
\label{lem:lambda-bound}
\ledgernode{1.6.4}
\lean{MIPRE.Halting.lambda_bound, MIPRE.Halting.log_lt_div,
  MIPRE.Halting.sq_le_two_pow_of_four_le, MIPRE.Halting.four_mul_succ_lt_two_pow}
\leanok
For all integers $C, C' \geq 1$, all $\lambda \geq 4 \max\{ (4C)^{8C},\ C \log C' \}$
and all $n \geq 2$,
\begin{equation*}
  C\, (C' \lambda n)^{C} \;\leq\; n^{\lambda} .
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
Bound each factor of the left-hand side by a power of $n$ --- $C' \leq 2^{\log C' + 1}$,
$\lambda \leq 2^{\log \lambda + 1}$, $C \leq 2^{C}$, and $2 \leq n$ --- so that the claim
reduces to the \emph{linear} inequality
$4C + C \log C' + C \log \lambda \leq \lambda$. Two of its three terms are a quarter of
$\lambda$ by hypothesis outright. The third, $C \log \lambda \leq \lambda/4$, is the only
one with content, and it is $\lambda < 2^{\lambda / 4C}$: writing $q = \lambda / 4C$, the
hypothesis $\lambda \geq 4(4C)^{8C}$ gives $q \geq 8C + 4$, and there
$4C(q+1) < q^2 \leq 2^q$, the second inequality holding from $q = 4$ on.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} In Lean $\log$ is $\mathtt{Nat.log\ 2}$, which makes the threshold no
larger, and the hypothesis $C' \geq 1$ is dropped: $C' < 2^{\log_2 C' + 1}$ holds at $C' = 0$
as well, so the Lean statement is the stronger one. What consumes this in the halting
reduction is the boundedness of a \emph{compressed} verifier: its running times are
$\poly(n, \lambda)$ (\texttt{GapCompression.decider\_time}, \texttt{sampler\_time}) while
Definition~\ref{def:lambda-bounded} asks for $n^\lambda$, and this is the inequality that
turns the first into the second once $\lambda$ is past a threshold depending on the degree
alone --- which is what keeps $\lambda$ polynomial in the description length rather than
exponential in it.

\begin{lemma}[The parameter of the halting verifier]
\label{lem:lambda}
\ledgernode{1.6.5}
\uses{lem:halt-construction,lem:lambda-bound,def:lambda-bounded}
\lean{MIPRE.Halting.lamOf, MIPRE.Halting.isBounded_comprStr, MIPRE.Halting.lamOf_ge,
  MIPRE.Halting.ansBound_le_lamOf, MIPRE.Halting.exists_compressorSpec,
  MIPRE.Halting.comprPoly, MIPRE.Cost.PolyBounded.absorb, MIPRE.Cost.PolyBounded.absorb_log,
  MIPRE.Cost.Prog.wrapCoreCost, MIPRE.Cost.Prog.wrapCore_cost'}
\leanok
There is a $\lambda$, computable from the description of $\machine$ in polynomial time and
of size $\poly(\abs{\machine})$, such that $\vhalt$ is $\lambda$-bounded and
$\TIME_{\dhalt}(n), \TIME_{\shalt}(n) = \poly(n, \abs{\machine})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.isBounded\_comprStr}: the sampler clauses from the compression
theorem's own bounds at $\lambda(n)$, the size clause from the criterion's slack
$2\abs{c} \leq n$ and \texttt{PolyBounded.absorb\_log}, the decider clause from the explicit
wrapper cost \texttt{Prog.wrapCore\_cost'} around \texttt{innerBound} and
\texttt{PolyBounded.absorb}; the parameter is computed in binary by \texttt{comprPoly}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} In the Lean route of Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract} the parameter
is a function of the \emph{level}, $\lambda(n) = 2^{2 \log n}$
(\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.lamOf}, at least $(n+1)^2$ and written in binary by the compressor in
time $O(\log n)$), and the boundedness claim is the field
\texttt{CompressorSpec.isBounded\_compr}: the verifier the output denotes is $n$-bounded,
uniformly in the description $(c, n)$, for every $n$ past a threshold depending on
$\Compress$ and the universal machine alone (\texttt{isBounded\_comprStr},
\texttt{Halting/CompressorCost.lean}). Its three clauses are the sampler's
(\texttt{GapCompression.sampler\_dim}, \texttt{sampler\_time} at $\lambda(n)$), the size's
(the compressed sampler's program is $\poly(\log n)$ and the wrapper carries $c$, which the
slack $2\abs{c} \leq n$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion} leaves room for), and the
decider's: the wrapper's cost with explicit constants (\texttt{Prog.wrapCoreCost},
\texttt{Halting/WrapperCost.lean}) around the preparation, the universal machine and the
compressed decider's own time (\texttt{GapCompression.decider\_time}). Each is absorbed into
$m^n \cdot (\abs{d}+1)^n$ by \texttt{PolyBounded.absorb} (\texttt{Halting/Absorb.lean}): a
polynomially bounded function of the largest of the index, the level and the input size is
below $m^n \cdot y^n$ once $n$ is past a threshold set by the polynomial, which is
Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda-bound} in a coarser form. The growth conditions that freezing at
$2n + 1$ needs are \texttt{lamOf\_ge} and \texttt{ansBound\_le\_lamOf}, and
\texttt{exists\_compressorSpec} assembles the specification from a polynomial-time program
computing the output string, which \texttt{comprPoly}
(\texttt{Halting/CompressorProgram.lean}) is. There is no timeout counter and no
$\TIME_{\dhalt}$ to compute: totality within a bound is the accounting itself.

\begin{lemma}[Values of the halting verifier]
\label{lem:dhalt-values}
\ledgernode{1.6.2, 1.6.3}
\uses{lem:halt-construction,lem:lambda,lem:compress-sampler-indep,def:pcc,def:q-value}
\lean{MIPRE.Halting.comprStr_accepts, MIPRE.Halting.haltProg_accepts_iff,
  MIPRE.Halting.CompressorSpec.toObligations}
\leanok
For every $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $\machine$ halts within $n$ steps then $\valstar(\vhalt_n) = 1$, witnessed by a
  value-$1$ PCC strategy;
\item if $\machine$ does not halt within $n$ steps then $\vhalt_n$ and $\vcompr_n$ are the
  same game up to an identification of the answer alphabets; in particular
  $\valstar(\vhalt_n) = \valstar(\vcompr_n)$, and $\vhalt_n$ has a value-$1$ PCC strategy
  if and only if $\vcompr_n$ does.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Item 1 is the fixed-answer strategy: when $\machine$ halts within $n$ steps, $\dhalt$
accepts every answer pair on every question pair in the support, so any strategy has value
$1$ and the constant-$0$ strategy is PCC. For item 2, $\dhalt$ at index $n$ performs the
same question-length check as $\dcompr$ and then runs $\dcompr(n, x, y, a, b)$ verbatim,
with $\mathtt{TIMEOUT}$ rejecting in both, so the two deciders accept on exactly the same
inputs; and the two verifiers share the sampler $\shalt = \mathtt{ComputeSampler}(\lambda)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:compress-sampler-indep}), so a strategy for either is a strategy for the
other with the same winning probability. The timeout counter of $\dhalt$ does not
interfere: its bound is chosen in Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda} so that the computations above
finish first.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comments.} Item 2 is, in the Lean route, the acceptance agreement
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.comprStr\_accepts}: the decider of the verifier the compressor's output
denotes accepts, at every index, exactly what the compressed decider of the described
verifier frozen at $2n + 1$ accepts, with the same sampler (\texttt{Vof\_comprStr}); the
transfer of values and of value-$1$ PCC strategies along it, in both directions, is
\texttt{CompressorSpec.toObligations}. Item 1 does not appear at this point of the route: the
halting case is the criterion's, through the distinguished string \texttt{yYes} of
Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}, and the $n$-step simulation of $\machine$ is the
criterion's recursion, not the decider's.

\begin{remark}[Item 2 is a transfer, not a bound]
\label{rem:dhalt-transfer}
\uses{lem:dhalt-values,thm:halting,thm:compression}
Item 2 is deliberately not ``$\valstar(\vhalt_n) \leq \frac12$''. That per-level bound is
false: if $\machine$ halts for the first time at step $T$, then for every $n < T$ it does
not halt within $n$ steps, yet $\valstar(\vhalt_n) = 1$ --- item 1 gives value $1$ at every
level $\geq T$, and item 2 together with the completeness clause of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} carries it \emph{downward} from level $2^n$ to level $n$,
which is exactly how the completeness half of Theorem~\ref{thm:halting} is proved. The
absolute bound needs $\machine$ never to halt, and is then the conclusion of the recursion
in Theorem~\ref{thm:halting}, not an ingredient of it. Recorded here because an earlier
revision of this chapter did state item 2 as the bound, and because the ledger node
`1.6.3' states it that way too; the formalization should follow the paper.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Halting reduction]
\label{thm:halting}
\ledgernode{1.6, 1.6.6}
\uses{thm:compression,rem:compression-abstract,lem:compressible-criterion,lem:value-lower-approx,lem:kleene,def:mipstar,def:game-description,lem:sync-le-valstar,lem:dhalt-values,lem:lambda}
\lean{MIPRE.Halting.halting_reduction, MIPRE.Halting.halting_reduction_of,
  MIPRE.Halting.exists_obligations, MIPRE.Halting.halting_reduces_to_gameValue_of}
\leanok
Assume an ambient \texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression}. There is a computable map
$\machine \mapsto \game_{\machine}$ from Turing machines
to explicit synchronous game descriptions (Definition~\ref{def:game-description}),
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $\machine$ halts on the empty input then $\game_\machine$ has a value-$1$ PCC
  strategy, so $\synval(\game_\machine) = \valstar(\game_\machine) = 1$;
\item if $\machine$ does not halt on the empty input then
  $\valstar(\game_\machine) \leq \tfrac12$, and hence also
  $\synval(\game_\machine) \leq \tfrac12$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\leanok
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.halting\_reduction\_of} in $\valstar$ and
\texttt{halting\_reduces\_to\_gameValue\_of} in $\synval$, from
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} as a \texttt{GapCompression}: Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion}
at the classes of Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}, whose four obligations are the
theorems named below, the last through \texttt{exists\_obligations}. This proves
computability of the explicit tabulation. It does not assert polynomial-time
enumeration of that table.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The efficient source presentation.}
The polynomial-time reduction in the source \texttt{recursive.tex},
\texttt{thm:halting}~\cite{AuditSync26}, returns sampler and decider program
descriptions for a game at a fixed index; those programs run in time polynomial in
$\abs{\machine}$. This is the source efficiency claim. Enumerating their finite
question distribution and accepted answer tuples yields the computable explicit
description used here, but the number of entries can exceed a polynomial in the
program size. The efficient verifier presentation and the explicit table are
different codomains; the latter has no polynomial-time construction claim here.

The proof is Lemma~\ref{lem:compressible-criterion} applied to the instantiation of
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract}; the semidecidability hypothesis of the lemma is the
$\valstar$ half of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-lower-approx}. The obligations of that
instantiation are inherited here, and all four listed in
Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract} are formalized: the tabulation
(\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.tab}, with \texttt{tab\_match}, \texttt{tab\_value} and
\texttt{gameValue\_tab\_eq\_one}), the semidecider on top of it
(\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.exists\_sem}, Lemma~\ref{lem:halting-semidecider}, which takes no
hypotheses), the two distinguished strings \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.yYes\_mem} and
\texttt{yNo\_mem}, and the compressor (Lemmas~\ref{lem:halt-construction},
\ref{lem:lambda} and~\ref{lem:dhalt-values}: \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.comprPoly}, its
specification \texttt{exists\_compressorSpec}, and \texttt{exists\_obligations}). The
synchronization invariant that an earlier revision also required of the games produced
inside $\Compress$ is no longer among them (Remark~\ref{rem:bipartite-route}).

In Lean the assembly is \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.halting\_reduction}
(\texttt{Halting/Reduction.lean}), which takes three hypotheses: a
\texttt{MIPRE.GapCompression} (which is Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}), a universal machine,
and an inhabitant of \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.Obligations}, whose content is exactly the
compressor --- \texttt{compr}, \texttt{compr\_spec}, and the threshold above which its
guarantee holds. The other three obligations of Remark~\ref{rem:compression-abstract} are
theorems that the proof applies directly, and the fourth is now a theorem too:
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.exists\_obligations} (\texttt{Halting/CompressorProgram.lean})
inhabits the structure, so \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.halting\_reduction\_of} takes only a
\texttt{GapCompression} and a universal machine --- of which
\texttt{Cost.exists\_efficient\_universal} provides one --- and is free of \texttt{sorry}.
So the reduction is machine-checked from an inhabitant of the compression contract
alone; supplying that inhabitant is Theorem~\ref{thm:compression-target}. The
\texttt{\#print axioms} check shows no further unproved assumption.
That assembly concludes in $\valstar$ only, and item 1 claims more: the witness is a
\emph{PCC} strategy, so $\synval = \valstar = 1$. The two steps that carry it there are now in
Lean, neither of them part of the instantiation. First, a synchronous strategy transports along
a relabeling of the alphabets (\texttt{MIPRE.syncValue\_eq\_of\_equiv}, from
\texttt{SyncStrategy.relabel}, which preserves the value and the commutation condition), so a
value-$1$ PCC strategy of $\verifier_n$ --- a strategy of its doubled game, which is what
\texttt{HasPerfectPCC} is --- gives one for a description tabulating that doubled game:
\texttt{Verifier.exists\_perfectPCC\_syncGame\_doubled}, hence
\texttt{Verifier.syncValue\_syncGame\_eq\_one\_doubled}. Second, \texttt{MIPRE.syncValue} and
\texttt{HaltingGameValue.gameValue} --- parallel developments with parallel notions of
synchronous strategy --- are the same number on the same description
(\texttt{HaltingGameValue.GameData.gameValue\_eq\_syncValue}), so the conclusion is available
in the vocabulary of \texttt{halting\_reduces\_to\_gameValue}
(Section~\ref{ch:main-theorem}): \texttt{Verifier.gameValue\_toGame\_eq\_one\_doubled}, which
on the tabulation is \texttt{MIPRE.Halting.gameValue\_tab\_eq\_one}. Soundness needs neither
step, only $\synval \leq \valstar$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sync-le-valstar}), which is
\texttt{Verifier.gameValue\_toGame\_le\_of\_valStar\_le\_doubled} in the same vocabulary. The
soundness bridge against $\verifier_n$ itself is still there and still true; the three
un-doubled completeness bridges went with the restatement of \texttt{HasPerfectPCC} on the
doubled game (Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}, ``two readings''), a strategy of the doubled game
having no reason to agree with itself across the two tags on the diagonal, which carries no
weight. Both steps
consume the tabulation's agreement with $\verifier_n$ in the form of the two alphabet
equivalences and the matching of $\mu$ and $D$ along them, not merely the equality of
$\valstar$ they imply --- from that equality alone the synchronous half cannot be recovered,
$\synval \leq \valstar$ pointing the wrong way. That is why
\texttt{MIPRE.Halting.tab\_match} delivers the matching data and not only the equality of
values, with \texttt{tab\_value} reading that equality off it; the tabulation builds the
equivalences anyway, out of \texttt{answerEquiv} and \texttt{questionEquiv}, so the stronger
statement cost nothing.

\begin{remark}[Identical measurement operators]
\label{rem:identical-operators}
\ledgernode{1.6.7}
\uses{thm:halting,def:sync-strategy,def:pcc,thm:separation}
The conditional ambient halting construction yields a witness in
\texttt{SyncStrategy}, represented by a single projective measurement family and
normalized trace probabilities. Within that representation the common family is
part of the definition (Definition~\ref{def:sync-strategy}). The source instead
tracks identical operators on a bipartite state through its transformations. These
are distinct representations: identical operators alone do not supply either
direction of Remark~\ref{rem:pcc-representation-bridge}. The checked downstream
halting and tabulation proofs start with the ambient interface and therefore do
not discharge that source-to-ambient obligation. The entanglement form, with the
stronger conclusion $\Ent(\game_\machine, \frac12) = \infty$ for non-halting
$\machine$, is discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:entanglement-form}.
\end{remark}
